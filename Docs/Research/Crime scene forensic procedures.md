# Every Contact Leaves a Scorable Trace

Crime-scene work at a burgled jewellery store is, at bottom, an ordering problem that a simulation can score: preserve life and fix a generous boundary first, log every person who crosses it, walk the scene along one controlled path, photograph everything before anything is touched, recover the fragile and the biological before the powders and casts that would destroy them, and give every item one unique reference, one package and one unbroken chain of signatures. The authoritative sources (NIJ/OSAC in the US, the Forensic Science Regulator's statutory Code and guidance in the UK, ENFSI, UNODC and ISO 21043) agree on that sequence and differ mainly in paperwork conventions, so a trainer built on the sequence travels well, including to Oman, where the Criminal Procedure Code fixes duties (protect, inspect, seize, report under Public Prosecution supervision) but not methods. The burglary scene is worked along the offender's route (entry point, path, display cases and safe, exit), and across every jurisdiction's solved jewellery raids the decisive exhibit is the same: **blood left on smashed display glass**, which is why DNA swabbing must precede fingerprint powder on that glass. UK volume-crime data put fingermarks at about **45% of non-residential burglary scenes** and DNA at about **10% of scenes visited**, and a UK residential burglary examination has grown from **1.5 to 3 hours**, which gives the designer a realistic yield rate and time budget. The literature supplies enough hard numbers to script mechanics (aluminium powder with a glass-fibre brush on glass, black magnetic powder on textured plastic, 1.5–2 in lifting tape onto 3 × 5 in cards, Kastle-Meyer read within 10 s, BlueStar sprayed from 50 cm in darkness, distilled water not saline on swabs, 2 lb dental stone to roughly 10 oz water with 30 min set and 48 h dry, Mikrosil mixed 20–30 s and set in under 10 min, blue 450 nm light with orange goggles, violet 415 nm to blacken blood) and enough documented trainee failure modes (glove reuse, under-photography, narrow cordons, missing scene-log entries, wet items in plastic) to build rubrics. Published VR trainers show learning equal to physical mock scenes, faster completion, and the weakest scores on the "learning" construct, so the simulator's value lies in repeatable, fully known ground truth and built-in feedback rather than in replacing the crime-scene house.

> **Method caveat (all six researchers reported the same limit).** From the research environment every direct fetch of a primary document (NIJ/NIST/OSAC PDFs, gov.uk FSR guidance, College of Policing APP, ENFSI manuals, state-lab SOPs, vendor instruction sheets, journal articles) was blocked at the DNS/proxy layer; only web search worked. Every finding and quotation in this report therefore comes from search-engine extracts of the named source, not from a reading of the full PDF. Quotations are near-verbatim as returned; section numbers appear only where the extract carried them; figures that differ between sources are flagged. Before any sentence is printed as training text or frozen into a mechanic, the linked document should be opened and the wording re-checked. Where the notes recorded standard practice that could not be sourced, this report says so rather than citing it.

## The first hour: preserve life, set a generous boundary, log everyone, walk one path

Every major standard hands the first officer the same ordered duties. The NIJ guide's opening section covers "receipt of information, safety procedures, emergency care, secure and control persons at the scene, establishment and preservation of scene boundaries, the transfer of control of the scene to the investigators in charge, and documentation of actions and observations at the scene", and its governing principle is to "preserve the scene with minimal contamination and disturbance of physical evidence" ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf); [NIJ 2000](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/178280.pdf)). The OSAC first-responder standard (2021-N-0016 v2.1, Feb 2024) tells the officer, once threats are dealt with, to treat the location as a crime scene until shown otherwise, to record the identities of people at or leaving the scene and of vehicles leaving it, to keep space clear for emergency ingress and egress, to wear PPE at all times, and to move a weapon only when needed to stop a threat ([OSAC 2021-N-0016](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/02/27/OSAC%202021-N-0016%20Standard%20for%20Initial%20Response%20at%20Scenes%20by%20Law%20Enforcement%20Version%202.1_0.pdf)). The UK College of Policing frames the same priorities as preserving life, preserving scenes, securing material and identifying victims, witnesses and suspects, and adds the "golden hour" rule that positive action immediately after the report protects material "that might otherwise have been concealed, lost, damaged, altered or destroyed" ([College of Policing, Investigation process](https://www.college.police.uk/app/investigation/investigation-process)). UNODC puts the stakes plainly: "the reliability and preservation of physical integrity of traces depend to a very large extent on initial actions at the scene" ([UNODC 2009](https://www.unodc.org/documents/scientific/Crime_scene_awareness__Ebook.pdf)).

The reason access control matters is Locard's principle, which the College of Policing states as: anyone who enters a scene "both takes something of the scene away with them and leaves something of themselves behind" ([College of Policing, Forensics](https://www.college.police.uk/app/investigation/forensics)). The UK structure is a **traffic cordon, an outer cordon and an inner cordon**, with placement weighed against wind direction and a single access control point ([College of Policing, Scene management and cordons](https://production.copweb.aws.college.police.uk/app/emergency-procedures-initial-response-and-command/scene-management-and-cordons)); for a shop the three tiers collapse to outer and inner. No source gave a metric distance for cordon placement; boundary size is treated everywhere as judgement, and the FSR Code notes that "the scene boundaries may change during examination", which supports the training rule "generous first, shrink later" ([FSR Code v2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2-accessible)). The **common approach path (CAP)** is a route for the team chosen "away from" the offender's likely entry and exit "to limit disturbance of the scene" ([FutureLearn, Crime scene strategy](https://futurelearn.com/info/courses/science-behind-forensic-science/0/steps/56532)); the FSR scene guidance refers to clearing a route along a CAP with stepping plates as the alternative that protects floor marks for later recovery ([FSR-GUI-0006 Issue 2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a8709243d82f78d5c5145d3/FSR-GUI-0006_Incident_Scene_Examination_Issue_2.pdf)). For a smashed-shopfront burglary this means the CAP is not the broken door: it is a rear or side entrance, or plates laid across the glass field.

The scene log is itself evidence. The FSR's contamination guidance requires that "all individuals entering the scene of crime shall be recorded in the scene log", with a record of PPE worn by everyone with legitimate access including emergency responders, and the first attending CSI must assess the cordon and log, with the appropriateness checked by the Crime Scene Manager ([FSR-G-206](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/393866/206_FSR_SOC_contamination_consultation.pdf)). The PSNI instruction is the most concrete: every person gives the scene log officer "name, rank, grade or number in block letters" plus date and time of entry; "only one log will be maintained for each scene"; handovers between loggers are recorded; and "serious crime scene logs are original notes, must be marked as an exhibit and continuity maintained" ([PSNI SI0616](https://www.psni.police.uk/sites/default/files/2023-11/Serious%20Crime%20Scenes%208%20September%202021.pdf)). A simulation should therefore treat a lost, duplicated or incomplete log as a failure state. Later-arriving staff park at a rendezvous point outside the cordon, and control of entry to specific areas within the scene is itself listed as a cross-contamination measure ([FSR-G-206](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/393866/206_FSR_SOC_contamination_consultation.pdf)). The NIJ guide also requires a "secure area for temporary evidence storage in accordance with rules of evidence and chain of custody", which gives the sim a physical "exhibit table" location ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)).

Oman changes the actors, not the duties. Under the Criminal Procedure Code (Royal Decree 97/99) a judicial police officer who learns of a crime must notify a Public Prosecution member and go to the scene to protect it, carry out the inspection and seize relevant items, recording these steps in a signed report; officers must preserve the instruments of the crime and remain under Public Prosecution supervision, which can seek disciplinary action for negligence ([Royal Decree 97/99, English translation](https://bwcimplementation.org/sites/default/files/resource/OM_Criminal%20Procedures%20Code_EN.pdf)). An Oman-set scenario should therefore include "notify Public Prosecution" and "sign the inspection report" as explicit tasks that UK and US scenarios lack.

## Walk through, write the strategy, then work the offender's route from entry to exit

Nothing is touched until the scene has been assessed and a plan written. The NIJ walk-through "is the first opportunity to identify valuable and/or fragile evidence and determine initial investigative procedures", and where evidence is at risk, documentation and collection happen immediately so that "all evidence that may be compromised is documented, photographed and collected" ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)). The UK Code is more demanding: §90.5.1 requires an initial assessment that identifies and preserves the scene as initially understood, and §90.6.1–90.6.2 require that "an examination strategy shall be documented" (generic for common incident types, bespoke for complex ones) and that it "take a holistic view of the incident scene" and "where necessary, challenge the initial investigation requirements and information received" ([FSR Code v2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2-accessible)). Where two approaches are both acceptable, "the notes must record a sound rationale for the approach taken", and the guidance applies equally to volume and major scenes ([FSR-GUI-0006 Issue 2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a8709243d82f78d5c5145d3/FSR-GUI-0006_Incident_Scene_Examination_Issue_2.pdf)). ENFSI's generalist method runs arrival, initial assessment, examination, search and collection of traces, and completing the scene ([ENFSI AFORE WP2](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/AFORE-WP2-BPM_Accreditation-SoC-1.pdf)), and its scene-of-crime manual covers the whole process "from the first officer's arrival through to the written report" ([ENFSI BPM-SOC-01](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/BPM-SOC-01-v.20220214_final_v2.pdf)).

The processing order is the single most consequential rule set for a simulation. NIJ instructs the team to "identify the most transient evidence and prioritize processing", to "monitor environmental and other factors that may affect evidence" and reprioritise accordingly, and states flatly that "collection of biological or trace evidence should be performed prior to use of powder or chemical enhancement techniques" ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)). The NIJ property-crime course ranks collection priority as biological fluids, trace materials and fragile evidence first, then swabs of handled or moved items, then lower-quality biological evidence ([NIJ, Collection priority](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/collection-priority)). No source used the words "least intrusive first", but the sequence visual, light, photograph, trace/DNA, powder, cast is what the sources add up to. One US agency order instead lists items "marked, photographed, fingerprinted and DNA-swabbed" before collection, so the powder-versus-swab order is agency-specific and the sim should expose it as a parameter ([Paradise Valley PD GO 83.3](https://public.powerdms.com/ParadiseValley/documents/454725/83.3%20Collection%20Of%20Evidence%20At%20The%20Scene)).

Search patterns are a coverage tool. Strip (lane) searches suit large outdoor areas; grid is a strip search repeated at 90 degrees; spiral suits a single searcher in a confined space; zone or quadrant divides the scene into sections, "often rooms or indoor areas", usually combined with another pattern inside each zone; wheel/ray radiates from a centre and is used when time is short ([EBSCO, Crime scene search patterns](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/applied-sciences/crime-scene-search-patterns/)). For a shop interior the zone pattern is the natural default, with the point of entry and the display cases as priority zones; a strip or grid pattern fits the pavement and car park outside.

The burglary-specific logic is to follow the offender. "The best evidence is usually found at the point of greatest activity, which at burglary scenes is often where entry was gained" ([Dutelle, Impression evidence](http://samples.jbpub.com/9781284082852/9781284082852_CH10_Dutelle_SECURE.pdf)); NIJ training notes that at the entry point "fragments of wood, metal, or broken glass may be discovered, along with fingerprints, blood, and fibers from clothing deposited when the perpetrator forced entry" ([NIJ, Location and collection of evidence](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/crime-scene-and-dna-basics-forensic-analysts/evidence-crime-scene/location-collection-evidence)). Investigators then "look along the routes from the point of entry to where items are missing or disturbed", check "the glass on the floor inside the window for impressions", and treat a broken tool tip as a prompt to search for the tool it came from ([Crime and Clues, Burglary investigations](https://crimeandclues.com/2013/01/26/burglary-investigations/)). Petraco's commercial-burglary case is the template: a window broken from outside, a safe "opened with hand tools", and "plaster dust footwear prints led from the back office to the front door, which was the exit point" ([Petraco 2016](https://jjcweb.jjay.cuny.edu/npetraco/pubs/Impression_Evidence_Chapter_2016r.pdf)). The Jewelers' Security Alliance adds the detail that matters most for this scenario: raiders "often cut themselves on small holes in laminated glass" ([JSA via Southern Jewelry News](https://southernjewelrynews.com/press-release/jewelers-security-alliance-offers-smash-and-grab-robbery-recommendations/)). Burglaries with no visible forced entry are a known dead end: an Israeli study of lock-cylinder impressioning observed that "most of these cases are never solved, many times because no visible signs of entry or forensic evidence are found" ([Pertsev et al. 2023](https://ejournals.eu/en/journal_article_files/full_text/018ecee2-5ce5-71a4-b429-c5b14d5816e6/download)).

The yields and timings are documented for the UK. Fingermarks were present at **45% of non-residential burglary scenes** (41% for dwelling burglary and vehicle crime) in an unscreened study, and deploying scene examiners by crime type had a significant effect on detections for commercial burglary ([Lingwood, Smith & Bond 2015](https://hira.hope.ac.uk/3761/1/Lingwood%2C%20Smith%2C%20Bond%202015%20-%20IJPSM.pdf)); the Home Office found "just under one in three attended residential burglary scenes yielding fingerprints", **DNA "at about ten per cent of all scenes visited"**, and that failures were "usually due to scenes being cleaned before examiners arrived, inaccessible scenes, or the offender barely disturbing anything" ([Home Office HOOR 43/05](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7ad567e5274a34770e76f6/hoor4305.pdf)). Surrey reported that the average residential burglary examination rose from **1.5 hours in 2017 to 3 hours**, attributed to stricter regulation and recording ([Surrey PCC](https://www.surrey-pcc.gov.uk/statutory-response/commissioners-response-to-hmicfrs-report-the-police-response-to-burglary-robbery-and-other-acquisitive-crime-finding-time-for-crime/)); a Queensland randomised trial found that removing time pressure and adding training at roughly 978 home-burglary scenes produced "greater evidence collection, identification, and solved crimes" at higher time cost ([Queensland Forensic Unlawful Entry Experiment](https://anzsebp.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/QLD_Forensic_Unlawful_Entry_Experiment-min.pdf)). Attendance is discretionary and varies: Nottinghamshire sent a CSI to **29% of burglary reports** in Q2 2022 ([Nottinghamshire PCC](https://nottinghamshire.pcc.police.uk/Document-Library/Public-Information/Meetings/Accountability-Board/2022/December-2022/Agenda-item-6.2-Burglary-November-2022.pdf)).

The solved cases confirm which exhibit to model as the prize. At Mappin & Webb (Regent Street, 2014) the robber "cut his hand on glass from the cabinet", the scene DNA hit the German database, and a Stansted mouth swab matched ([WatchPro](https://www.watchpro.com/?p=33410)); at Jai Jewellers, Smethwick, "forensic experts from West Midlands Police found blood on a smashed display cabinet" and the match led to a 22-year sentence ([Express & Star](https://www.expressandstar.com/news/crime/armed-shop-robbers-jailed-for-a-total-of-94-years-6171310)); in Fall River, Massachusetts, "blood recovered from broken glass in a smashed display case matched a suspect's DNA profile" ([WWLP](https://digital-release.wwlp.com/?p=192153)). Gulf cases are solved differently: Oman's 1.4 million-rial Seeb and Muttrah heist (blowtorch entry) was cracked "through hours of camera footage and phone records", with suspects "pulled off the plane just before takeoff" ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/oman/oman-police-crack-14m-rial-gold-heist-arrest-three-1.1899832)); a 2026 Muttrah attempt saw the men demolish "part of a wall to get into the shop" ([Oman Observer](https://www.omanobserver.om/ampArticle/1192385)); Dubai Police in 2022 "lifted fingerprints off the crime scene", tracked movements and arrested at the airport within 12 hours ([Gulf Today](https://gulftoday.ae/news/2022/07/27/dubai-police-arrest-jewellery-thieves-minutes-before-their-flight-was-to-take-off)). An Oman-set scenario is therefore well grounded as a night-time wall-breach or cutting entry with CCTV, phone data and airport interception as the investigative frame, and blood on display glass as the forensic win. JSA's 2025 report adds plausible modern artefacts: crews use "cellphone Wi-Fi jammers (which block the cellular backup signal from being sent to the alarm company)", rooftop entries rose from 15 to 25, and burglary remained the largest loss category at about **$80.7 million** ([National Jeweler](https://www.nationaljeweler.com/articles/14966-jsa-s-2025-crime-report-shows-concerning-rise-in-violence)). On the retailer side, the GIA traced stolen diamonds through their laser inscriptions to a wholesale dealer, which is why the hand-over pack should include certificate numbers and stock photographs ([IDEX Online](https://www.idexonline.com/Memo?id=49266)).

## Contamination control is prevention plus detection, and gloves are the weak point

UK regulation is the most explicit framework. The FSR Code's contamination section "requires PPE/barrier clothing when entering scenes and examination, recovery and processing areas, and procedures must minimise DNA transfer by defining when PPE is cleaned and/or changed" ([FSR Code, §103.5](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statutory-code-of-practice-for-forensic-science-activities/forensic-science-regulator-code-of-practice-accessible)). The scene guidance FSR-GUI-0016 (Oct 2023) covers "control avoidance and detection of DNA contamination at incident scenes" and is read with PAS 377:2023 and BS ISO 18385:2016 on DNA-grade consumables ([FSR-GUI-0016](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65362ef6e839fd000d867325/FSR-GUI-0016_DNA_contamination_controls_-_incident_scenes.pdf)); ISO 18385 obliges manufacturers of swabs, containers, packaging, gloves and lab coats to reduce detectable human DNA in their products ([SCC, ISO 18385:2016](https://scc-ccn.ca/standardsdb/standards/8158171)). The three recognised routes are "personnel to exhibit/DNA sample, contaminated consumables to exhibit/DNA sample, and exhibit to exhibit or sample to sample", and controls fall into "prevention and ... detection" ([FSR, DNA contamination detection](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dna-contamination-detection/dna-contamination-detection-accessible)). Detection "primarily involves comparison of DNA profiles against personnel reference profiles" held on elimination databases ([FSR-GUI-0019](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69959ce39f2f510ba1d0b87b/FSR-GUI-0019_Guidance_-_Contamination_Detection-_DNA_Elimination_Samples.pdf)), and the same logic applies to fingerprints: elimination prints are "the prints of all persons whose presence at the place under inspection has been for legitimate purposes" ([FBI, The Science of Fingerprints](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/fbiscienceoffingerprints.html)). In a shop that means two populations, staff and owner on one side and police and CSI on the other.

The scene-specific PPE wording in FSR-GUI-0016 could not be read. The closest written UK rule is the forensic medical examination guidance: a minimum of "two pairs of disposable non-latex powder-free gloves", the outer pair "changed between sampling different areas; before handling equipment; or after touching frequently touched surfaces", and a face mask "preferable" rather than mandatory ([FSR-GUI-0017](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dna-contamination-controls-forensic-medical-examinations/dna-contamination-controls-forensic-medical-examinations-accessible)). US laboratory policy is comparable: FBI staff "will change gloves between each proximal container when directly handling individual items of evidence if transfer of trace, biological, or other materials is a concern" ([FBI EVDC-320-06](https://fbilabqsd.fbi.gov/file-repository/evidence-management/evdc-320-06-minimizing-contamination.pdf)). Other listed scene controls are recording PPE worn by every entrant, controlling entry to specific areas, examining "different rooms on different days or by different personnel", and the rendezvous point outside the cordon ([FSR-G-206](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/393866/206_FSR_SOC_contamination_consultation.pdf)). The empirical case for suits is old but clear: investigator movement "without protective clothing produced enough contamination to yield a full profile", and with protective clothing contamination "still occurred but was greatly reduced" ([NCJRS, Scene of the grime](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/scene-grime)).

Gloves deserve a mechanic of their own because the research shows they are the main transfer vector. A mock-scene thesis found likelihood ratios of 1,221 to 1.5 × 10^8 for DNA transferred by gloves and concluded "nitrile gloves pose a high risk for DNA transfer" ([WVU thesis](https://researchrepository.wvu.edu/etd/12366/)); a Lancashire study found vinyl gloves retained most DNA and that even after bleach cleaning gloves "frequently retained sufficient DNA to yield partial STR profiles" ([Gaskell et al.](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/58699/1/58699%20Gaskell%20et%20al.%20VOR.pdf)); "surgical gloves can transfer DNA of innocent persons to a crime scene" ([AAFS 2010 A62](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2010-A62.pdf)). The RTI/NIST study of 163 practitioners in a VR scene found that "DNA swabbing, glove use, and the number of photos taken varied most across crime scene teams" and that "staff at forensically accredited agencies performed better on glove use" ([RTI/NIST](https://www.rti.org/publication/interagency-study-of-crime-scene-investigation-using-virtual-real)), which marks glove changes as both the most variable behaviour and the most assessable one.

The consequence of failure is a real case. Adam Scott was charged with rape in October 2011 and held about five months after his saliva from an unrelated spitting incident contaminated a rape sample "through the incorrect re-use of disposable plastic trays" at LGC; the batch "showed DNA present in the negative control, but this was not sufficiently dealt with" ([FSR report on the Scott contamination](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7b470f40f0b66eab9a02b7/dna-contam-report.pdf)). A VR debrief can show the trainee a "contamination elimination database hit" to their own profile as the downstream result of a mask or glove failure. Stepping plates complete the control set: UK suppliers sell a "Clear Plastic Stepping Plate 350 x 350mm" for scenes "where officers must be kept from treading on potential evidence" ([Scenesafe](https://scenesafe.co.uk/collections/treadplates/products/clear-plastic-stepping-plate-350-x-350mm)), and a shop with a smashed door and glass on the floor is the textbook case for laying them along the CAP. Victims are told the same thing from the other side: do not touch what the offender touched, and "repairs or re-securing the home may compromise any forensic examination" ([Gloucestershire Constabulary burglary pack](https://www.gloucestershire.police.uk/SysSiteAssets/media/images/gloucestershire/html-email/2020-burglary-pack/2020-10-burglary-advice-pack.pdf)).

## Documentation is layered: three-tier photographs, a rough sketch never altered, notes, logs and seals

No layer substitutes for another: documentation "may include notes, photography, videos, sketches, measurements, or reports, and none of these substitutes for the others" ([UNODC 2009](https://www.unodc.org/documents/scientific/Crime_scene_awareness__Ebook.pdf)), and records "should be produced contemporaneously or for incident scenes, at the first practical opportunity" ([FSR-GUI-0006 Issue 2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a8709243d82f78d5c5145d3/FSR-GUI-0006_Incident_Scene_Examination_Issue_2.pdf)). "The primary means of crime scene documentation is still photography", and nothing moves "until it is photographed" ([NIJ, Documentation](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/crime-scenes/documentation)).

Photography follows the same progression in every guide: the scene "utilizing overall, medium, and close-up coverage" and evidence "with and without measurement scale and/or evidence identifiers" ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)). Overalls come first, then mid-range shots framed around a fixed landmark from "approximately ten to twenty feet", then close-ups "within about five feet", with "two close-up images ... standard: one captured with a labeled scale or ruler adjacent to the evidence, and a subsequent shot without the scale" ([Maine State Police](https://www.maine.gov/dps/msp/sites/maine.gov.dps.msp/files/inline-files/Crime%20Scene%20Photography.pdf)). Robinson's "three cardinal rules" are "fill the frame, maximize depth of field, and keep the film plane parallel", and a mid-range shot is composed as "an isosceles triangle: the camera, the item, and a prominent scene feature" ([Police1 on Robinson](https://www.police1.com/investigations/articles/a-better-understanding-of-midrange-photographs-in-a-crime-scene-sxveGuGs1YbLkC1n/)). Close-ups are shot at **90 degrees** to the surface to prevent parallax, with the scale "as close to the evidence as possible without disturbing it, on the same plane" ([Forensics Digest](https://forensicsdigest.com/crime-scene-photography/)), and a scale is placed beside rather than on an impression because "putting it on an impression can damage it" ([Crime Scene and Evidence Photography ch. 7](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/CSEP/chapters/crime-scene-and-evidence-photography-2nd-edition-chapter7.pdf)). The entry point is photographed "with mid-range and close-up views from both the exterior and interior of the building" ([CSEP ch. 5](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/CSEP/chapters/crime-scene-and-evidence-photography-2nd-edition-chapter5.pdf)). The **ABFO No. 2** L-scale is the reference, but a NIST-affiliated review found commercial copies vary in manufacturing consistency ([Dimensional review of scales](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/dimensional-review-of-scales-for-forensic-photography.html)). The photo log records photographer, date and time, case number, camera, lens, lighting and exposure, "always create a crime scene sketch as an addendum to the photographic log", and "when using a digital camera, never delete a photograph" ([Crime scene documentation outline](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/crime-scene-documentation-outline.pdf)); OSAC's draft photography guide lists the minimum kit and allows "a photo of any document containing that information" to satisfy the identification requirement ([OSAC proposed guide, June 2020](https://nist.gov/system/files/documents/2020/06/26/Crime%20Scene%20Photography%20Doc_OSAC%20Proposed_June2020.pdf)). No source gave a typical photo count for a burglary. Oblique white light is the search mode for footwear in dust, and ALS targets include "fibers, paint, hairs, and glass" ([Phoenix PD crime lab 1700](https://crimelab.phoenix.gov/QTDocuments/1700.PDF)). Laser scanners (FARO Focus, Leica RTC360) appear only in vendor and local-news accounts of homicides, shootings and crashes, at about **$100,000** per unit, and nothing indicates routine use at volume burglaries ([13abc Toledo](https://www.13abc.com/2023/09/12/3d-scanner-gives-tpd-detailed-evidence); [FARO Colorado Springs](https://www.faro.com/en/Resource-Library/Case-Study/colorado-springs-pd-uses-laser-scanner-to-document-mass-shooting)); camera, tape and sketch remain the standard.

The sketch is drawn "before anything is moved, and they should not alter it afterward", measurements "should be accurate to within ¼"", taken "from fixed location reference point, such as walls or curbs" and covering room dimensions, doors, windows and distances between objects ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)). Four methods are standard: rectangular coordinates from two perpendicular walls; triangulation from "two permanent fixed points"; baseline, where a second tape is laid "at a 90-degree angle to the first tape"; and polar coordinates measuring "distance and direction (angle) of an object from a known reference point" ([OSAC 2023-N-0003](https://www.nist.gov/document/osac-2023-n-0003-standard-diagramming-scenes-version-21); [Benha University, Sketching](https://fmed.stafpu.bu.edu.eg/Forensic%20medicine%20&%20clinical%20%20toxicology/303/crs-18978/lecture%20-%20sketching.pdf)). Every sketch needs "case identifiers, a north arrow, a scale or 'not to scale' note, a legend and numbered evidence that matches the evidence log" ([SkyeBrowse summary of NIJ guide](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/crime-scene-sketch.md)). For a rectangular shop, rectangular coordinates from two walls is the natural method; baseline suits the pavement outside.

Notes begin on arrival and record "lighting, odors, and potential evidence locations" and "the condition of the scene as it existed upon arrival" ([NIJ, Documentation](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/crime-scenes/documentation)); agency checklists add "lights on/off; windows/doors open/closed, locked/unlocked", weather and lighting, names of other personnel and "everyone's actions taken at scene, including arrival time" ([Winchester Sheriff policy 111](https://www.winchesterva.gov/files/assets/public/v/2/sheriff/111-crime-scene-processing.pdf); [SAPD procedure](https://public.powerdms.com/SAPDCA/documents/1004910)). For a shop the notes panel should also capture the shutter, alarm panel and CCTV state on arrival. No source stated the "separate observation from inference" rule; treat it as convention.

Exhibit referencing and sealing differ by jurisdiction in form but not in logic. The UK Home Office format is three initials of the seizing officer, an 8-digit date and a sequential number from 1, "ABC25062023-1", with split items as -1-A, -1-B, and the instruction to "record the unique reference numbers of all TEBs to make sure you maintain a chain of evidence" ([Home Office, Tamper evident bags v7.0](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/951472/tamper-evident-bags-v7.0-gov-uk.pdf)); police forces more commonly use initials, slash, number ("ABC/1") ([Forensic Focus](https://www.forensicfocus.com/forums/general/uk-exhibit-reference-convention/)). The UK bag is "a transparent, leak-proof plastic bag with a strong adhesive seal" whose seal shows the word "stop" if tampered ([Home Office TEB, accessible](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tamper-evident-bags/tamper-evident-bags-accessible-version)); US packaging makes "STOP" appear in the seal strip on an opening attempt ([Sirchie, Evidence packaging](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/0/5/05_evidence_packaging_labeling_sealing.pdf)), and UK CJA labels carry "Exhibit Continuity panels" for successive signatures while older card tags on string "can be lost, torn, or replaced" ([SceneSafe](https://scenesafe.co.uk/pages/new-tamper-evident-cja-exhibit-label-from-scenesafe)). US label fields are "the agency case number, item number, date recovered or received, and the investigator's initials", and items are packaged before leaving the scene ([NIJ, Biological evidence packaging](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/evidence-collection/biological-evidence-packaging)). NIST IR 7928 supplies a sample chain-of-custody report and biohazard label and tells handlers to assume all biological material is hazardous ([NIST IR 7928](https://nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/NIST-IR-7928.pdf)). The sim's exhibit record therefore needs: unique reference, description, location (sketch coordinate and photo number), date-time, recovering officer, seal number, and a continuity chain of every handover with signature and time.

Release is a formal gate. The final survey ensures "all evidence is accounted for before departing scene", that "all equipment used in the search is gathered", that hidden areas are rechecked and documentation reviewed for errors; the debrief "lets law enforcement personnel and other responders share information about particular scene findings before the scene is released" ([NIJ NCJ 200160](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/200160.pdf)); "at minimum, documentation should be made of time and date of release, to whom released, and by whom released", with a second formal debrief within 24 hours ([Crime scene response guidelines](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/respon3.html)). What the shop owner inherits is "powder or chemical treatment of surfaces, which leaves residue you will need to clean afterwards" and the advice to "ask the examiner what is safe to clean and when" ([Security Guides](https://securityguides.co.uk/reporting-crime/after-burglary/police-attendance/)); UK victims receive the Victims' Code leaflet and the Met promises updates "every 28 days" ([Met Police victim information](https://www.met.police.uk/SysSiteAssets/media/downloads/met/victim-information.pdf)). No source described handing back a commercial premises (insurer, stock reconciliation, boarding-up).

## Fingermarks: look, light, powder lightly, photograph, then lift once

Almost every evidential surface in a jewellery store is non-porous: showcase glass, chrome and steel frames, polished counters, card readers, door push-plates. That matters because the method follows the surface. Latent residue is eccrine sweat (water above 98% with amino acids, urea, lactate and salts) mixed with sebaceous lipids ([Cyanoacrylate fuming review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5514188/)); on porous paper "the water and water-soluble components are absorbed quickly", so amino-acid reagents are used in the lab, while "nonporous surfaces do not absorb", the residue sits on top, is easily damaged, and is developed with powders, superglue or vacuum metal deposition ([NIJ Fingerprint Sourcebook ch. 7](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/the-fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development.pdf)). Receipts, boxes and pouches go to the lab un-powdered; glass and chrome are powdered in place. A night-time burglary processed the next morning is well inside the usable window: "all sebaceous latent fingerprints on glass are still useful for identification after 6 months" ([De Alcaraz-Fossoul et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233901042_Determination_of_latent_fingerprint_degradation_patterns_-_A_real_fieldwork_study)), contaminated marks have survived "over 2½ years on a non-porous surface" outdoors ([Keith Borer Consultants](https://www.keithborer.co.uk/media/0d3ldq4d/sb-cs-eye-fp-longevity-paper.pdf)), and training text should never claim a mark "looks fresh". A wet surface (rain through the broken shopfront) switches the method to small particle reagent, "the only method that works on wet non-porous surfaces" ([SPR review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2090536X1630065X)).

Non-destructive search comes first. SOPs use a "flashlight at an oblique angle to examine evidence before processing" ([TBI LP-0001](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/latent-print/sop-manual/LP-0001.pdf)), then a forensic light source; most investigators interviewed by NIJ "used single-wavelength blue handhelds around **450 nm**" ([NIJ ALS landscape study](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/nij-landscape-study-of-alternate-light-sources.pdf)) viewed through orange goggles so "the print separates from the background" ([Sirchie ALS handbook](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/0/5/05_ALS_vol_3_.pdf)). The correct hand pose on showcase glass is the torch held almost parallel to the surface, moving the light rather than the head. A patent mark in dust or blood found at this stage is never powdered; powder destroys dust marks and blood marks go to blood-enhancement chemistry (this follows from the method tables, although no single retrieved sentence says it).

Powder and brush pairing follows the UK trial-based guidance, the only published rule set found: "Glass should be powdered with aluminium powder unless contamination prohibits its use"; "Aluminium powder should, where possible, be applied with a glass fibre brush"; Zephyr-style squirrel or tapered polyester brushes are used where glass fibre would clog on contaminated surfaces; and "a black or jet black magnetic powder should be used on textured surfaces, NOT aluminium or black granular powder", with magnetic powder also mandated on u-PVC ([HOSDB Fingerprint Powders Guidelines 09/07](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf); [HOSDB Powders Study 3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5ef5f7aed3bf7f7691f44b7d/Fingerprint_and_Footwear_Forensics_Newsletter_-_Special_Edition_-_Powders_Process_Study_3_67-06.pdf)). Magnetic powders are "usually less effective on ferromagnetic substrates such as steel or nickel" ([Sourcebook ch. 7](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf)), so a steel safe door takes a conventional powder. Contrast decides between aluminium and black on glossy surfaces; fluorescent powders with ALS handle multicoloured or patterned backgrounds. HOSDB also states that technique "is likely to be as important as powder selections for some smooth surfaces" ([HOSDB 09/07](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)), which licenses scoring the motion itself.

The motion is consistent across sources. Loading: "the filament tips are lightly dipped into a sterile, wide-mouth container holding a small amount of powder" ([Sourcebook ch. 7](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf)), or in Sirchie's stricter version "Never dip the brush directly into the powder jar. Measure out a small quantity on paper. Less is best", with a test print first in an area the suspect is unlikely to have touched ([Sirchie Latent Print Development Manual](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/l/a/latent_print_development_manual_4.pdf)); tap off excess and use very little ([Santa Barbara SOP-CSI-007](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf)). Application: "lightly twirling the brush over the object until the print develops, adding more powder only as needed" ([Santa Barbara SOP](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf)); "apply powder with light circular motions, then brush along the direction of ridges as they appear" and "clear excess powder from between ridges" ([DC DFS](https://dfs.qualtraxcloud.com/ShowDocument.aspx?ID=10172)); "too much powder fills in ridge detail" and "limit brush strokes" ([Arrowhead Forensics](https://arrowheadforensics.com/blog/how-to-guide-lift-fingerprints/)). The magnetic wand is different: the magnet picks up "a ball of the iron and particle mixture, essentially forming its own brush", which is "much softer than conventional filament brushes" ([Sourcebook ch. 7](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf)), applied in a circular motion "while making sure only the powder, not the wand, touches the surface" ([FBI Processing Guide](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/jan2001/lpu.pdf)); retracting the rod drops the powder back into the jar ([Sourcebook ch. 11](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225331.pdf)). No source gives a pressure, stroke count or speed; "light", "stop when ridges appear" is as precise as the literature gets, so the sim's thresholds will be design choices. The Sirchie fibreglass brush has a filament cluster of **2¼ in (5.7 cm)** on a ¼ × 2¾ in handle ([Sirchie](https://www.sirchie.com/catalog/product/view/id/2/?___store=international_english)). Two rules the notes could not source anywhere (do not breathe on the surface; one brush per powder colour) should not be printed as cited rules.

Photograph before lifting, because the lift is irreversible. Illinois requires that "all developed impressions should be photographed prior to lifting" ([Illinois State Police](https://isp.illinois.gov/StaticFiles/docs/ForensicServices/ForensicScienceCommandManuals/Latent%20Prints.pdf)); Washington requires it "if the lift process may pose a challenge" ([WSP Latent Print Technical Manual](https://wsp.wa.gov/forensics/docs/crimelab/manuals/technical/latent/Latent%20Print%20Technical%20Manual%20R.%2022.pdf)); the Sourcebook records that "some agencies require that examination-quality photographs be taken of all latent prints developed with powders prior to lifting, whereas others do not" ([Sourcebook ch. 10](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225330.pdf)). The scale goes beside the mark, the camera back parallel to the surface, and SWGFAST notes that "the friction ridge impression alone is not sufficient documentation" without annotation ([SWGFAST ACE-V documentation standard](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2016/10/26/swgfast_standard-documentation-ace-v_2.0_121124.pdf)).

The lift itself: tape comes in **1.5, 2 and 3 in** widths on 360 in rolls, the 1.5 in size "for individual prints or smaller surfaces" and 3 in "for lifting multiple prints at once"; the tape "must be large enough that one piece covers the entire area" ([crimescene.com](https://shop.crimescene.com/product/fingerprint-lifting-tape-roll-p-77/); [Arrowhead](https://arrowheadforensics.com/blog/how-to-guide-lift-fingerprints/)). Anchor the tape about an inch beyond the mark and press it down with the edge of a card "in a smooth constant motion" to avoid trapped air ([Spraggs, Police Magazine](https://www.policemag.com/patrol/article/15349075/how-to-lift-fingerprints)); "rub out bubbles before lifting" ([Santa Barbara SOP](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf)); peel "in one continuous motion" keeping a finger on the free end so it cannot curl back ([crimescene.com](https://shop.crimescene.com/product/fingerprint-lifting-tape-roll-p-77/)). Hinge lifters (**1.5 × 2 in** for single prints, **2 × 4 in** for multiple prints or partial palms, also 4 × 4 in) avoid bubbles on flat surfaces ([Sirchie hinge lifters](https://www.sirchie.com/catalog/product/view/id/1439/?___store=international_english); [Sirchie TB02-102](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/L/P/LP_Dev_mt_Tech_TB02-102ENG-REV6_facing_page_spreads.pdf)); gelatin lifters (**13 × 18 cm**, black, white or transparent, cover film peeled off then replaced) conform to textured surfaces and are applied one edge first, smoothed with a thumb and then pressed firmly ([BVDA Gellifters](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters)), and a Dutch study found marks on vinyl and latex gloves "are best recovered with black BVDA gellifters" ([crime-scene-investigator.net](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/visualization-of-latent-fingerprints-on-used-vinyl-and-latex-gloves-using-gellifters.html)). Backing cards are **3 × 5 in** (7.6 × 12.7 cm) ([Sirchie backing cards](https://www.sirchie.com/products/latent-print-development/latent-print-lifting-backing/backing-cards.html)); black powder goes on white cards and fluorescent powder on black cards, with a small diagram of the print's location and orientation ([Santa Barbara SOP](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf)). The card is marked with lift date, case number, lift location, lifter's name, a unique lift number, a diagram and "an arrow indicating the direction of the lift" ([WSP manual](https://wsp.wa.gov/forensics/docs/crimelab/manuals/technical/latent/Latent%20Print%20Technical%20Manual%20R.%2022.pdf)); "one should be able to pinpoint the area and orientation of a latent print on the object" ([Idaho State Police](https://isp.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/Forensics/Methods/Methods/Archive/Post-2012/Latents/Latent-Prints-Analytical-Methods-Rev-01.pdf)). A failed lift (bubble through the ridges, tape folded on itself) usually consumes the mark, which is exactly why the photograph came first.

Powder and DNA interact, and the UK rule is the useful one. HOSDB states that "photography of the powdered mark on the surface and subsequent swabbing of the mark for DNA may yield better results", and that powders "can be used to locate contact areas on a surface" so the examiner can "swab areas where contact is known to have happened" ([HOSDB 09/07](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)). A Staffordshire trial found gelatin lifts "displayed higher detectable levels of DNA along with recovering more donor alleles" and "the least amount of damage to ridge detail", while nylon swabs removed "all traces of the fingerprints" ([Staffordshire pseudo-operational trial](https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/6026/1/Accepted%20manuscipt%20DNA%20fingermark%20pseudo-operational%20trial.pdf)); conversely, full and partial profiles have been recovered from used brushes and powders, and "powder should be removed before amplification" ([ISFG 2005](https://www.isfg.org/files/61bebd1f8e4d5e47364077a5adee68b15529d225.02005721_131595303851.pdf)). The teaching rule for the sim: on fixed glass, locate with powder then photograph then swab the located contact area; on small items that can be seized, bag un-powdered for the lab.

Lab processes exist so that the trainee knows what seized items go to. Cyanoacrylate fuming at **70–80% RH**, with FBI "microburst" times of 30 s to 4 min and TBI times of 3–5 min, monitored with a test print to avoid over-fuming ([FBI Processing Guide](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/jan2001/lpu.pdf); [TBI LP-0011](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/latent-print/sop-manual/LP-0011.pdf)); DFO at 100 °C for 10–20 min before ninhydrin, because "if ninhydrin is applied after DFO treatment, additional development occurs", and the porous sequence is irreversible ([Sourcebook ch. 7](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/the-fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development.pdf)). Vacuum metal deposition (gold then zinc) is the sensitive follow-up for smooth non-porous items ([Fingerprint Development Techniques review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355065615_Fingerprint_Development_Techniques_A_Review)).

What the trainee produces is a mark "suitable for comparison", not an identification. The UK CAST grading scale runs "0 No evidence of a fingermark; 1 Some evidence of a fingermark; 2 Less than 1/3 clear ridge detail; 3 Between 1/3 and 2/3 clear ridge detail; 4 Over 2/3 clear ridge detail", with grades 3 and 4 "considered of value" ([Dstl Fingermark Visualisation Newsletter, July 2020](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f1ab58ee90e07456caf0c63/2020_Jul_Dstl_Fingermark_Visualisation_Newsletter__5_v1.0_O.pdf)), which maps directly onto a procedural rendering of ridge coverage for scoring. Identification is an examiner judgement under ACE-V with a measured error rate: the FBI black-box study of 169 examiners found **six false positives in 4,083 non-mated comparisons (0.1%)** and about 7.5% false negatives ([Ulery et al., PNAS 2011](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018707108)); PCAST framed the upper bound as "1 error in 306 cases based on the FBI study and 1 error in 18 cases based on a study by another crime laboratory" ([PCAST 2016](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/microsites/ostp/PCAST/pcast_forensic_science_report_final.pdf)); and in the Mayfield case three FBI examiners and a court-appointed expert confirmed a wrong identification while, per the Inspector General, not contravening any SWGFAST procedure ([DOJ OIG](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2018-02/s1105.pdf)). The sim should return "identified / excluded / inconclusive / insufficient" from a bureau rather than an instant match.

## Footwear, tool marks and glass tell the entry story, in that recovery order

Footwear marks in a shop are mostly dry dust marks on polished floor and showcase glass, and the order is locate, photograph, electrostatic lift, gelatin lift, cast only where there is a 3D impression. "A dry impression can lack contrast with the surface, so an oblique light source is preferred", and when origin is uncertain the mark is treated as dry origin "because any enhancement may destroy the impression" ([DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:18227/FULLTEXT01.pdf); [Brainscape footwear cards](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/footwear-and-tiretrack-impressions-18674568/packs/22627506)). Examination-quality photography means "mount camera on a tripod with the focal plane parallel to the impression", manual focus on the bottom of the impression, aperture closed for depth of field, and the scale "on the same plane as the impression, even if the examiner must dig beside it" ([SWGDE 17-P-003](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/17-p-003-swgde-guidelines-for-the-digital-imaging-of-footwear-and-tire-impressions/)); flash at roughly **10°, 30° and 45°** with "the exact flash angle ... determined by depth of impression" ([Virginia DFS Impressions](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf)), from at least three positions about 100° apart ([Crime and Clues, Footwear the missed evidence](https://crimeandclues.com/2013/01/31/footwear-the-missed-evidence/4/)), at 3–5 ft (sources vary) and f/11–f/16 in RAW ([Wisconsin DOJ Footwear brochure](https://www.wisdoj.gov/Forensic%20Science/Footwear%20Evidence%20Brochure.pdf)).

Electrostatic lifting "is normally the first technique used, as unsuccessful attempts will not prevent subsequent lifting and enhancement techniques"; the film's black side goes against the impression, lifts are examined "in a darkened room with a high intensity light source held at an oblique angle, and the film should never be reused" ([SWGTREAD Guide for Lifting](https://www.nist.gov/document/swgtread12guideforliftingfootwearandtireimpressionevidence200703pdf)). The TBI step list is: cut two-ply film with margin on all sides, black side down; connect probe and ground, attach ground to a metal surface or plate; switch on and touch the film with the probe, which pulls it tight; roll out trapped air; switch off, discharge the probe on metal, lift the film and tape it dust-side-up inside a box; a second lift can recover residual dust ([TBI CS010.13](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/crime-scene/CS010.13.pdf)). The devices run at **10–15 kV** with negligible current ([JFSE](https://jfse-ojs-tamu.tdl.org/jfse/article/view/6/pdf)); they pick up "only ... fine dust, and mud marks, for example, are not absorbed" ([BVDA electrostatic lifting](https://www.bvda.com/en/electrostatic_lifting_of_shoeprints)) and work poorly on carpet, while "electrostatic lifting followed by gelatin lifter was the superior order when both methods are used" ([comparative study, HERO record](https://hero.epa.gov/hero/index.cfm/reference/details/reference_id/2580144)). Gelatin lifters for shoeprints come in **13 × 36, 18 × 36, 36 × 45 and 36 × 50 cm**; the lifter is held by its two short ends, one end applied first and the rest rolled down with a rubber roller, and BVDA recommends black gelatin "even for dark-colored prints" ([BVDA Gellifters](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters); [BVDA shoeprints](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters-shoeprints)); one research group found dwell "up to 20 min" improves results ([Gelatin lifters and hydraulic press](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10831242_Lifting_Shoeprints_Using_Gelatin_Lifters_and_a_Hydraulic_Press)). SWGTREAD's adhesive-lifter colour rule conflicts with BVDA's: "white lifters provide greater contrast with impressions enhanced with dark colored powders. Black lifters provide greater contrast with light colored powders and residue impressions" ([SWGTREAD lifting guide, mirror](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-lifting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html)); the sim should accept black gel for dust and match adhesive-lifter colour to powder.

Casting applies to soil or sand outside the shop. Dental stone is "a generic gypsum product generally having a strength rating of 8,000 psi (55 mega pascal) or higher" and "the average footwear impression requires approximately two (2) pounds of dental stone and approximately ten (10) ounces of water" ([SWGTREAD Guide for Casting](https://www.nist.gov/document/swgtread11guideforcastingfootwearandtireimpressionevidence200703pdf)); other agencies give 8–9 oz (Wisconsin), 12 oz (Maine) and 6 oz per pound with about 3 lb per shoe (Virginia), so a mixing mechanic should accept a range and require "the viscosity of heavy cream" ([Wisconsin DOJ Footwear Collection Guide](https://www.wisdoj.gov/Forensic%20Science/Footwear%20Collection%20Guide.pdf); [Maine State Police, Casting](https://www.maine.gov/dps/msp/sites/maine.gov.dps.msp/files/inline-files/Casting%20Impressions_0.pdf); [Virginia DFS](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf)). The mix is poured outside the impression and allowed to flow in, "must set undisturbed for at least 30 minutes before it is carefully lifted", is scratched on the back with initials, date, case number and an exhibit number linking it to photographs and sketch before it hardens, and is air-dried **48 hours** before any cleaning ([crime-scene-investigator.net, Footwear](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/footwear.html); [IAAI, 3-D impressions](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/3-d-impressions); [SWGTREAD casting, mirror](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-casting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html)); "Plaster of Paris is not recommended" ([Virginia DFS](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf)). The comparison logic the trainee should hear in the debrief: class characteristics (size, design, mould) support only a "could have made it" conclusion; identification needs randomly acquired characteristics (cuts, nicks, debris) "in the same location and orientation" on a test impression ([FBI, Forensic analysis of footwear impression evidence](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/july2009/review/2009_07_review02.htm)).

Tool marks at the pry points are either impressed (tool pressed in) or striated, "contour variations, generally microscopic, on the surface of an object caused by a combination of force and motion" ([AFTE glossary](https://afte.org/glossary/toolmark-striated/); [TBI FA_19](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/firearms/FA_19.pdf)). The scene SOP is to "photograph the tool mark with normal and oblique lighting (with and without a scale)", then closer shots from several oblique angles with the photographer perpendicular to the mark, then cast with "Mikrosil or other suitable casting material" ([Santa Barbara SOP-CSI-006](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-006-12-Firearms-and-Toolmarks-Evidence-Collection.pdf)). The photograph "is for identification and orientation only and cannot be used for actual comparisons", which is why the 3D cast is needed, and the tool "should NEVER be placed into the impression to see if it could have made the mark" ([CA DOJ PEB 27](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/peb-27.pdf)). Whole items are preferred: "detached locks, latches, or door mouldings that carry toolmarks are often submitted as found" ([Indiana State Police PEB-13](https://secure.in.gov/isp/labs/files/PEB-13_Toolmark-_7-1-25.pdf)); otherwise "cut any substrate well away from the toolmark" ([IAAI, Toolmark on substrate](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/toolmark-on-substrate)); "do not package tools in the same container as the questioned toolmark", protect marks from rubbing, and preserve trace ([NH State Police lab](https://www.nhsp.dos.nh.gov/our-services/forensic-laboratory/firearms-toolmarks/toolmark-analysis)); "be alert for any other evidence that may have been transferred when the mark was made, such as paint or mortar" ([IAAI, Toolmarks](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/toolmarks)). Mikrosil timings for the mechanic: about **1.5–2 in of base** with a slightly shorter strip of catalyst, mixed "about 20 to 30 seconds until there are no blue streaks" (longer mixing makes it set too fast), "setup time of under 10 minutes at room temperature" ([Sirchie Mikrosil instructions](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item//M/C/MCM100_TI02-59ENG-REV4E.pdf)), or "six to eight minutes" and about fifteen below freezing ([Tri-Tech](https://tritechforensics.com/mikrosil-casting-putty-kit/)); brown is "generally preferred for toolmarks" because the dark colour increases visible detail ([Safariland Mikrosil](https://www.safariland.com/products/forensics/evidence-collection/tool-mark-casting/mikrosil-white-7-oz.-200g-F_155.html)). Safe attacks range from hand tools through power tools and torches to explosives, classified by how much evidence they leave ([Blaze, Safecracking](https://www.mattblaze.org/papers/safelocks.pdf)); no forensic source on drilled or punched safes (swarf, drill-hole striations) was retrieved, so that part of a scenario is inference.

Glass carries three kinds of information: direction and sequence of force, transfer to the breaker, and fingermarks and blood on its faces. Radial cracks form first on the face opposite the blow and concentric cracks afterwards on the struck face; the 3R rule says rib (Wallner) marks on a radial crack near the impact meet the surface at right angles on the reverse side from the force, and a later radial crack terminates at an earlier one, so in a T-junction "the stem is the later crack and the crossbar is the earlier one" ([Wikipedia, Forensic glass analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_glass_analysis); [forensicspot, Glass fractures](https://forensicspot.com/topics/forensic-physics/glass-fragmentation-and-direction-of-force-analysis)). The FBI procedure cautions that "in tempered or laminated glass, Wallner lines cannot be used to determine the side of crack initiation", that tempered glass dices without ridges, and that "small windows held tightly in a frame may not bend enough for the rule to apply" ([FBI GEO-510-06](https://fbilabqsd.fbi.gov/file-repository/trace-evidence/geo-510-06-fractography-of-brittle-materials.pdf)). This constrains scenario design: display cases are typically tempered (dices into cubes) or laminated (cracks but the interlayer holds), and insurers recommend laminated or UL-certified impact-resistant glazing on case fronts, sides and tops ([Jewelers Mutual](https://www.jewelersmutual.com/resources/business/risk-management/jewelry-showcases-2-factors-optimal-physical-security); [VD Showcase](https://vdshowcase.com/blog/glass-display-case-jewelry-security-guide/)), so a "which blow came first" exercise belongs on an annealed shopfront pane, not on a showcase.

Backward fragmentation is what puts the breaker inside the evidence. High-speed photography showed backward fragments every time a pane broke, most landing within about **1–1.5 m** and some reaching about 3 m ([Nelson & Revell 1967](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S001573686770376X)); glass per square metre "decreased rapidly with distance in an approximately exponential manner" ([Pounds et al., NCJRS](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/study-backward-fragmentation-window-glass-and-transfer-glass)), by "a factor of 4–5 for every 45 centimeter increment" ([Luce, Buckle & McInnis 1991](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/037907389190190T)). The suspect side is time-critical: "microscopic glass particles on clothing, shoes, and hair can disappear within 24 hours", and contaminated clothing is "wrapped in paper and submitted to the laboratory" rather than picked over ([Girard, Forensic analysis of glass](https://samples.jbpub.com/9781284142617/9781284142617_CH05_Girard_SECURE.pdf)). At the scene, "a representative glass standard (known sample) from each broken item" is taken directly from the source and labelled by surface, "such as the inside or outside of a window", with questioned and known glass packaged separately ([Evidence collection guidelines](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/collect.html)); hard containers "such as metal cans or plastic boxes" are used, small pieces go in "a pill box or vial, sealed and labelled", with initials across the tape seal ([WSP Crime Lab, Glass](https://wsp.wa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/J.-Glass.pdf)); "pieces of window or door glass lying on the ground can carry footwear impressions and should be preserved carefully" ([Santa Barbara SOP-CSI-004](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-004-12-Trace-Evidence-Collection.pdf)); ENFSI notes fragments "often lodge in cuts, holes, and shoe soles" and requires sealed, uniquely identifiable packages ([ENFSI BPM Glass](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/BPM-Glass_QCC_20221122-003-1.pdf)). For the sim: mark "inside"/"outside" on pane remnants before removal, collect interior and exterior fragments separately, and treat a thrown brick as a tool-mark and trace carrier (glass, paint, hand DNA) boxed rigid.

## Blood, touch DNA, fibres and saliva items: light it, test it, swab it, dry it, paper it

Blood is found with light before chemistry. It "does not show significant fluorescence but does have a strong absorption band around 395–435 nm, with a maximum at **415 nm**", so violet light makes stains look black; it "is the only body fluid that does not fluoresce", while semen, saliva, urine and sweat fluoresce under UV, violet and blue ([Horiba application note](https://www.horiba.com/int/scientific/applications/others/pages/detection-of-body-fluids-with-an-alternate-light-source/); [Foster + Freeman application notes](https://fosterfreeman.com/?p=18173)). Standard pairings are 450 nm blue with orange goggles (Crime-lite ML Blue with an OG550 filter), the Crime-lite 42S bands UV 350–380, violet, blue 420–470, blue/green 445–510 and green 480–560 nm with twin-filter goggles, and the rule that "these high-intensity sources can harm eyes and skin, and that even with goggles, exposure time should be limited" ([San Diego PD Forensic Biology manual](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/fbunittechnicalmanualv08-12-2019.pdf); [Foster + Freeman Crime-lite 42S](https://fosterfreeman.com/?p=4735)). Photographs use "the same color of lens as the goggles you were wearing when the evidence was best viewed" ([Phoenix PD crime lab 1700](https://crimelab.phoenix.gov/QTDocuments/1700.PDF)); detergent brighteners fluoresce near 415 nm and are a false-positive trap ([Horiba](https://www.horiba.com/int/scientific/applications/others/pages/detection-of-body-fluids-with-an-alternate-light-source/)). A VR ALS mechanic therefore darkens the room, forces goggle colour to match the band, makes blood go dark at 415 nm and body fluids, some fibres and fluorescent powders glow under blue, and times exposure.

The presumptive test is Kastle-Meyer or a strip. KM relies on "the peroxidase-like activity of hemoglobin" to turn phenolphthalin pink; the swab is wiped on the stain, a drop of reagent added, then a drop of peroxide, and "a positive result appears as a pink color change within **10 seconds**", while "waiting for a period of time over 30 seconds will result in most swabs turning pink naturally" ([Wikipedia, Kastle-Meyer test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastle%E2%80%93Meyer_test); [Science Notes](https://sciencenotes.org/kastle-meyer-test-for-blood/)). Colour appearing before the peroxide step is read as invalid "due to the presence of chemical oxidants", and reagent order varies between labs ([AAFS 2011 A139](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2011-A139.pdf)). A known-blood positive and a clean negative control run alongside every test ([Science Notes](https://sciencenotes.org/kastle-meyer-test-for-blood/)). Phenolphthalein and leucomalachite green "are the most specific" and tetramethylbenzidine and orthotolidine "the most sensitive" ([Cox 1991](https://www.ncjrs.gov/App/Publications/abstract.aspx?ID=132525)); Hemastix and most others detect blood to 1:100,000 dilution ([Tobe et al. 2007](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/10091/)); Hemastix are 3 in strips moistened with a drop of water, rubbed on the sample and read against the bottle chart ([Ward's Science](https://www.wardsci.com/us/en/product/8876724/hemastix)). All are presumptive and cannot distinguish human from animal blood. The practice of testing the stain edge so the body of the stain survives for collection is standard but was not confirmed in an opened SOP.

Latent blood (an offender who wiped a cut) is the BlueStar/luminol case. The reagent is sprayed "lightly from at least **50 cm**, sweeping side to side" as a fine mist from waist height, in darkness after about five minutes of eye adaptation; "the glow fades after about one minute but can be refreshed by spraying again" ([BLUESTAR Medium Kit user guide](https://www.bluestar-forensic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/USER_GUIDE_medium_kit_EN_20231511.pdf)); a known-blood positive control and a sprayed unstained negative area are required, positives are confirmed with an immunochromatographic test and then the lab, and food residues and some chemicals produce false positives ([BLUESTAR, Use at a suspected crime scene](https://www.bluestar-forensic.com/use-of-bluestar-at-a-suspected-crime-scene/)). Photography is tripod and long exposure, vendor settings ISO 800, f/8, 8 s ([BLUESTAR, Photographing bloodstains](https://www.bluestar-forensic.com/2016/07/)). Luminol "did not adversely effect the PCR testing" ([Gross, Harris & Kaldun 1999](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/effects-luminol-subsequent-analysis-bloodstains)) and 48 h after application neither luminol nor BlueStar had degraded DNA whereas benzidine had ([Almeida et al. 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/20643520)); the mixed solution is "best used within 24 hours" ([BlueStar Magnum manual](https://www.bluestar-forensic.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/MO_A4_gb_magnum.pdf)). Visible staining is photographed and sampled before spraying, because the spray spreads the stain.

Collection of a dried stain follows the NIJ property-crime course: "moisten a sterile cotton swab with distilled water from an ampoule; avoid using saline. Do not touch the ampoule to the swab, or the stain. Instead drop the water onto the swab or stain from a distance of about ½ of an inch"; then collect a control "by slightly moistening the swab with distilled water and rubbing an area of the surface in an unstained region near the stain" ([NIJ, Dry stains on non-absorbent surfaces](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/evidence-collection/dry-stains-non-absorbent-surfaces)). "The stain should be concentrated on the tip", and swabs are "left open to the atmosphere and allowed to air dry before they are packaged in paper envelopes" ([SJSU, DNA sample handling](https://gcp-web.sjsu.edu/people/steven.lee/courses/c2/s1/DNA_Sample_Handling%201.pdf)); some labs follow "with a second dry swabbing", both retained ([FIU/NIJ property crimes module](https://archive.gfjc.fiu.edu/property_crimes/module03/pro_m03_t13.htm)). "Never store blood, whether wet or dry, in a plastic evidence container ... never collect multiple stains on the same swab" ([LibreTexts, Bloodstain collection](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Hartnell_College/The_Introduction_to_the_Forensic_Sciences/14%3A_Evidence_Collection_and_Preservation/14.03%3A_Bloodstain_collection)); "wet and dried items should not be folded over on themselves" ([NIJ, Biological evidence packaging](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/evidence-collection/biological-evidence-packaging)). Scene-level pattern recording is limited to photographing each stain group with scale, classifying it (drip stain, drip pattern, cast-off, transfer per the ASB terminology) and measuring the axes of elliptical drops; the angle-of-impact relation sin θ = width/length (3.0 cm long by 1.5 cm wide gives 30°) is taught content and reconstruction is analyst work ([ASB TR 033](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/033_TR_e1_2017.pdf); [Alabama ACCESS lesson 06-04](https://accessdl.state.al.us/AventaCourses/access_courses/forensic_sci_v11/forensic_ua_v11/6_unit/06-04_lesson.htm)).

Touch DNA targets in this scenario are the forced door or shutter, the showcase sliding-door runners and glass edges, any tool left behind, and dropped gloves. The double swab (wet then dry) dates from a 1997 saliva-on-skin study and "became the standard in DNA sampling used by many police forces and labs worldwide" ([Sweet et al. 1997](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/improved-method-recover-saliva-human-skin-double-swab-technique); [J Forensic Sci 2021](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/10.1111/1556-4029.14733)); a wet-moist double swab for **30 seconds** maximised recovery from cotton, while on tile a single wet or moist-dry swab sufficed ([UTS swabbing study](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/170325/3/Effect%20of%20swabbing%20technique%20and%20duration%20on%20forensic%20DNA%20recovery.pdf)). The interpretation limits are themselves teaching points: on burglary tools, when a second user wore gloves "the first user matched the DNA handle profile in 37% of the cases" ([Pfeifer & Wiegand 2017](https://www.springermedizin.de/persistence-of-touch-dna-on-burglary-related-tools/12075566)); on residential door handles 43 of 70 residents were detectable and about 10% of major components came from unknown people ([Swiss door-handle study](https://boris.unibe.ch/176278/1/1-s2.0-S1875176822000178-main.pdf)); gloves transfer the wearer's and others' DNA, with shedder status affecting results ([Carrara et al. 2023](https://roebuckforensics.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/1-s2.0-S1872497322001648-main.pdf)); and an Indiana appeals court reversed a burglary conviction because "there was no evidence that would support an inference that Meehan's DNA was found on the glove because he handled it during the burglary, as opposed to some other time" ([Meehan v. State](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1665077.html)). A profile from a handle or glove proves presence, not timing or activity. Glove interiors are "sampled by cutting or swabbing the interior surfaces" ([AAFS 2006 B31](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2006-B31.pdf)), and a latex glove on a bedroom floor has produced a database match and arrest ([CrimeWatch PA](https://delaware.crimewatchpa.com/netherprovidencepd/16127/arrests/carrozza-carmen-n-1-count-18-3502-a2-f1-burglary-overnight-accommodation-and-3)).

Hairs and fibres are lifted singly or by tape. "Through use of clean forceps and paper (i.e., trace paper), the sample can be removed from the surface and placed into a clean piece of paper that can be folded and packaged in a paper envelope" ([FIU/NIJ module T08](https://archive.gfjc.fiu.edu/property_crimes/module03/pro_m03_t08.htm)); "do NOT place hairs or fibers directly into an envelope. Always use a druggist fold", and do not tape-seal the fold itself ([Maine State Police Druggist Fold Guide](https://www.maine.gov/dps/msp/sites/maine.gov.dps.msp/files/inline-files/Druggist%20Fold%20Guide.pdf)); tape lifts "use only low tack tapes", vacuuming is least preferred, and reference samples must accompany the lifts ([California DOJ PEB 6](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/peb-06.pdf)). Fibres are found "on woodwork or glass at the point of entry in a burglary" and their strength "depends upon the number and type of fibres recovered" ([Key Forensic Services](https://keyforensic.co.uk/defence/our-services/trace-evidence/fibres)); "some human and animal hair will fluoresce brightly under Violet and/or Blue illumination" ([Foster + Freeman](https://fosterfreeman.com/?p=18173)). Reference fibres from the shop's carpets and staff uniforms are needed to exclude innocent sources. Cigarette ends, drink cans, masks and caps are each a separate exhibit: butts identify people "through DNA profiling of epithelial cells from saliva stains" and are handled with sterile forceps, each "in a separate sealed white envelope" ([Science Diliman](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/sciencediliman/article/download/6743/5842); [Univ. Bologna 2025](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/b58144b5-0107-43bb-a9eb-1c983977a8e4/s12024-025-01151-z.pdf); [NSW exhibit](https://lgbtiq.specialcommission.nsw.gov.au/assets/lgbtiq/hearings/20231010/exhibits/Exhibit-64/Exhibit-64-Tab-039-NPL.0131.0001.1909.pdf)).

Packaging closes every one of these threads. Crime-lab guidance favours "paper bags, envelopes, or boxes" because "sealed plastic lets moisture build up and encourages bacteria and mold"; wet items "can be air-dried by hanging them or laying them flat on a clean surface indoors, away from the scene" or go into paper so drying continues; "sharp items go in boxes or breathable tubes, and the packaging should be marked to say a sharp object is inside"; each item is packaged separately and biohazards flagged ([Iowa DPS crime lab guide](https://dps.iowa.gov/media/111/download); [LAB-BP-01 best practices](https://crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/best-practices-for-collection-packaging-storage-preservation-and-retrieval-of-biological-evidence.pdf)). The UK uses the leak-proof tamper-evident plastic bag for most exhibits and breathable bags for wet biological items; the US uses paper for all biological evidence with a taped and initialled seal. Both: one item per package, label before sealing, record the seal number.

## Training is assessed at mock scenes with known outcomes, and VR so far matches but does not beat them

UK CSI training is short, practical and volume-crime-focused. A College of Policing-approved course at the Wetherby regional centre took about a month and conferred a "CSI Stage 2 qualification" allowing independent deployment to more serious incidents ([Royal Gibraltar Police](https://www.police.gi/news/rgp-officer-passes-csi-course-1417)); the Wetherby centre includes a mock residential house "used to simulate real life scenarios like burglaries, thefts, sexual assaults and murders" ([Police Oracle](https://www.policeoracle.com/news/humberside_police/2020/Oct/15/new-csi-training-centre-for-the-yorkshire-and-humber-region_105943.html)); a private six-week "CSI Level 1" course covers photography on force equipment, scene approach and exhibit handling, fingerprint and DNA recovery, risk assessment and "the CSI's role in court for volume crime scenes" ([Forensic Access](https://forensic-access.co.uk/cpd-training-and-events/law-enforcement/crime-scene-investigation-csi-level-1)). Competence sits on Skills for Justice NOS unit SFJCN403 "Undertake forensic examinations at scenes of incidents" ([UK Standards](https://www.ukstandards.org.uk/en/nos-finder/SFJCN403/undertake-forensic-examinations-at-scenes-of-incidents)) and on the FSR Code, under which scene examination is activity FSA-INC 100; the ISO/IEC 17020 accreditation deadline of 6 April 2027 "has been suspended" in favour of milestones on 6 April and 2 October 2027 ([FSR-NOT-01-2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a3e6bb2d52550a19950f60e/FSR-NOT-01-2026_-_INC_100_Milestones_1_and_2.pdf)). The FSR's guidance is the closest thing to a design brief: competency assessments should "provide an equivalent scope and scale of challenge to the live operating environment", account for weather and lighting, and "a table-top/isolated method of demonstrating competency would not be appropriate for some aspects of initial competence, such as scene approach, which would require examination of a scene with known outcomes" ([FSR-GUI-0006 Issue 2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a8709243d82f78d5c5145d3/FSR-GUI-0006_Incident_Scene_Examination_Issue_2.pdf)). A VR scene has fully known ground truth, which is precisely what that sentence asks for.

The US model is certification plus intensive academies. The IAI Certified Crime Scene Investigator needs "at least one year of experience ... at least 48 hours of board-approved education within the previous five years" and a pass on a three-hour exam, valid five years ([CrimeSceneInvestigatorEDU](https://www.crimesceneinvestigatoredu.org/csi-certification/); [IAI FAQs](https://theiai.org/crime_scene_faqs.php)); the National Forensic Academy is a ten-week programme historically of "over 400 hours of training, 170 hours of in-class work, and over 220 hours of field practicum", its first three weeks on scene management, photography and impression evidence and the rest on mock scenes ([LEIC NFA](https://leic.tennessee.edu/national-forensic-academy); [AAFS 2003 H53](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2003-H53.pdf)). The OSAC training standard requires progress checks "using methods such as oral and written exams, exercises that mirror real casework, and reviews of mock casework" ([OSAC Standard Practice for CSI training, 2018](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2018/11/09/standard_practice_for_crime_scene_investigator_training_continuing_education_professional_development_certification_and_accreditation.pdf)). University rubrics show the weighting instructors use: Staffordshire grades a portfolio at 40%, a mock-scene simulation with statement and reflection at 30%, interview portfolio 20% and a ten-minute mock court 10% ([Staffordshire FORE50366](https://evision.staffs.ac.uk/module_descriptors/FORE50366.html)); Mount Union gives groups of five **25 minutes** to search, mark and collect at a mock burglary, assessed by local police on "fingerprint dusting, evidence collection, photography, and UV residue detection" ([Mount Union](https://www.mountunion.edu/academics/undergraduate-degrees/criminal-justice/mock-burglary)); Western Technical College's outcomes include take control on arrival, mark evidence on a sketch, seal containers at the scene, always wear gloves and release only after a final survey ([Western TC](https://archie.westerntc.edu/webprograms/course_outcomes_summaries/30504508.pdf)). The error literature attributes most scene mistakes to "too much haste at scenes and the lack of experience/knowledge and/or professional training" and warns they "can lead to poor samples and, in some cases, wrongful convictions" ([SJSU Themis](https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1055&context=themis)); "the potential exists for the technician to transfer DNA among the items being collected at the crime scene via their examination gloves if not frequently changed" ([AAFS 2016 B3](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2016-B3.pdf)).

The VR and MR trainers published so far converge on a modest claim. A randomised study of 57 participants bagging and tagging devices found "no significant difference between the VR and physical groups" on pre- and post-tests, and "VR participants finished faster" ([Hassenfeldt, Jacques & Baggili 2020](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10201308)); UWE Bristol's app showed that VR "support[s] learning of practical crime scene processing skills" with minimal motion sickness ([Mayne & Green 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1355030620300927)); the HoloLens 2 trainer built on 3D-scanned scenes for 44 Kuwaiti police officers scored highest on overall impression and lowest on "the learning construct" ([Albeedan, Kolivand & Hammady 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-024-01018-8)); a mixed-reality tagging game found "all participants improved their game scores at the end of the second game session by identifying more traces" ([Applied Sciences 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/app13021177)); Abu Dhabi Police's VR arson trainer saved cost and time but "qualified staff ... were not present when students trained, so feedback relied on built-in mechanisms" ([Halford et al. 2024](https://academic.oup.com/policing/article/doi/10.1093/police/paae122/7921032)); Keele's passive 360° virtual scenes produced "no difference in student performance" and "a strong preference towards solely face-to-face learning" ([Keele repository](https://keele-repository.worktribe.com/output/712827/evaluating-the-use-of-virtual-crime-scenes-to-support-student-understanding-of-crime-scene-investigation)); and the RTI/NIST study found "prior VR experience was not required" but that "motion sickness and headaches can make VR unsuitable for some people" ([RTI/NIST](https://www.rti.org/publication/interagency-study-of-crime-scene-investigation-using-virtual-real)). AR is framed as a way to train "decision-making and psychomotor skills ... simultaneously", as a supplement ([Wilkins et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1355030624000273)), and a College of Policing/Staffordshire PhD comparing VR with the Crime Scene House reports in February 2027 ([College of Policing](https://www.college.police.uk/research/projects/immersive-learning-landscapes-evaluating-role-virtual-reality-crime-scene-investigator-training)). The design lessons: interactivity (powdering, lifting, swabbing, bagging, photographing) separates a trainer from a tour; explicit feedback and debrief must be built in because the learning construct is weakest and instructors are often absent; the behaviours that discriminate between agencies (glove changes, swabbing technique, photo count and sequence) are the ones to track; randomised evidence placement supports mastery through repetition; and a seated comfort mode is needed for the motion-sick minority. No study yet measures skill transfer from VR to real scenes.

The procedures agree; the forms, conventions and a few technical choices do not, and an Oman deployment adds a legal layer with almost no published method behind it. The table collects the variations that would otherwise be hard-coded wrongly.

| Packaging | UK: leak-proof barcoded TEB, breathable bag for wet biological; US: paper for all biological, taped and initialled ([Home Office TEB accessible](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tamper-evident-bags/tamper-evident-bags-accessible-version); [Iowa DPS](https://dps.iowa.gov/media/111/download)) | Rule to enforce everywhere: never sealed plastic for wet or biological items; one item per package |

| Powder vs DNA order | NIJ: biological/trace before powder; HOSDB: powder to locate, photograph, then swab; one US agency powders then swabs ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf); [HOSDB 09/07](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf); [Paradise Valley PD](https://public.powerdms.com/ParadiseValley/documents/454725/83.3%20Collection%20Of%20Evidence%20At%20The%20Scene)) | Visible blood is always swabbed first; for contact areas on fixed glass, powder, photograph, swab is defensible in the UK |
| Glove change frequency | FME: outer pair between areas, before equipment, after common surfaces ([FSR-GUI-0017](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dna-contamination-controls-forensic-medical-examinations/dna-contamination-controls-forensic-medical-examinations-accessible)); FBI: between containers ([FBI EVDC-320-06](https://fbilabqsd.fbi.gov/file-repository/evidence-management/evdc-320-06-minimizing-contamination.pdf)); no scene-level numeric rule found | Score "new gloves per exhibit or area and after touching kit"; mask preferred |
| Dental stone ratio | 2 lb : 10 oz (SWGTREAD), 8–9 oz (Wisconsin), 12 oz (Maine), 6 oz per lb and 3 lb per shoe (Virginia) | Accept a range; test consistency, not a single number |
| Lifter colour for dust | SWGTREAD: black for light residue; BVDA: black gelatin even for dark dust | Accept black gel for dust marks |
| Presumptive blood reagent | KM in the UK commonly; US agencies vary KM/LMG/Hemastix (not sourced) | Model KM with the two-stage read; offer Hemastix as an alternative |
| Attendance | UK discretionary with force variation (Nottinghamshire 29%) ([Nottinghamshire PCC](https://nottinghamshire.pcc.police.uk/Document-Library/Public-Information/Meetings/Accountability-Board/2022/December-2022/Agenda-item-6.2-Burglary-November-2022.pdf)) | A commercial burglary with forced entry and blood is attended everywhere; the scenario needs no attendance decision |
| Standards to cite | NIJ/OSAC (US first response), FSR Code v2 + FSR-GUI-0006/0016/0019 (UK scene management), ENFSI BPM-SOC-01, UNODC 2009, ISO 21043-2:2018 ([NIST ISO 21043 page](https://www.nist.gov/adlp/spo/organization-scientific-area-committees-forensic-science/iso-21043-standards)) | A GCC deployment can describe itself as aligned to ISO 21043-2 and UNODC without conflicting with Omani law, which sets duties not methods |

Oman and the GCC need particular honesty. The only Oman-specific primary source found is the Criminal Procedure Code; no Royal Oman Police scene procedure, first-responder checklist, forensic accreditation statement or CSI syllabus surfaced, although the ROP Forensic Laboratory, the Sultan Qaboos Academy for Police Sciences (founded 5 January 1980) and a Digital Laboratory at Muscat Governorate Police Command are documented ([Times of Oman](https://timesofoman.com/article/166894-royal-oman-police-day-protecting-the-nation-serving-the-community); [Oman Observer 2026](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1197943/oman/omani-women-advance-policing-and-digital-forensics)). The regional academic anchor is the Naif Arab University for Security Sciences under the Council of Arab Ministers of Interior, which offers forensic higher diplomas, MSc and PhD programmes and short courses but published no crime-scene-specific curriculum ([UNODC PNI, NAUSS](https://unodc.org/unodc/en/commissions/CCPCJ/PNI/institutes-NAUSS.html); [NAUSS AJFSFM](https://journals.nauss.edu.sa/index.php/AJFSFM/article/view/338)). The UAE is the visible GCC model: the Federal Crime Scene Village in Ajman runs a three-month "Crime Scene Forensic Investigation – Level 1" course (twelve graduates in its eighth edition), and Abu Dhabi Police's first CSI batch received a diploma "in cooperation with the Scottish Police College" ([Abu Dhabi Police, Ajman graduation](https://www.adpolice.gov.ae/en/Media-Center/News/2021/06/20/Officers-graduate-from-a-specialized-course-at-the-Crime-Scene-Village-in-Ajman); [Abu Dhabi Police, first batch](https://www.adpolice.gov.ae/en/Media-Center/News/2021/06/30/MoI--Graduation-of-the-First-Batch-of-Crime-Scene-Investigators)). GCC CSI training is therefore modelled on UK and Scottish practice, which makes UK-style procedure (CSI Level 1/2 curriculum, exhibit labelling, FSR-type quality framework) the defensible baseline for an Oman-set simulation, and the UAE and Kuwait already use VR and MR for police and CSI training. Gulf case reporting describes CCTV, phone records and airport interception rather than scene forensics, so the Oman scenario should present forensics as one strand in that investigative frame.

The remaining gaps are the ones to budget time for before publication. No source gave a cordon distance, a photo count for a burglary, a brush pressure or stroke count, a peel angle, a glove-change count per scene, a time a developed-but-unlifted powder mark can wait, or a published VR scoring rubric with weights. The texts of FSR-GUI-0016 (scene PPE: suit, mask, double gloving, speaking over exhibits), the Fingermark Visualisation Manual's process-selection charts, the HOSDB powder performance tables, the ENFSI BPM-SOC-01 chapters, the SWGTREAD guides, NIST IR 7928 storage temperatures and the UNODC checklists were all confirmed to exist but not read. No official UK description of how victim and staff elimination prints and DNA are taken and destroyed was found. No forensic literature on drilled or punched safes, on fibre transfer to broken glass edges, on CCTV and alarm-log seizure procedure, or on handing back a commercial premises was retrieved. Any mechanic built on those points is design inference and should be labelled as such in the training content.

## Training checklists by evidence type

The user asked for these as lists so that they can become simulation state machines and rubric rows. Each step is supported by the sources cited in the sections above; steps marked *(unsourced practice)* are standard but could not be confirmed in an opened document and should be presented in training as convention. "Fail" entries are the behaviours the sources identify as errors and are candidates for automatic scoring.

### A. First responder and scene set-up

1. Sweep for dangerous persons and hazards; preserve life first; keep emergency ingress and egress clear ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf); [OSAC 2021-N-0016](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/02/27/OSAC%202021-N-0016%20Standard%20for%20Initial%20Response%20at%20Scenes%20by%20Law%20Enforcement%20Version%202.1_0.pdf)).
2. Treat the location as a crime scene; note people present or leaving and vehicles leaving; obtain an initial account from staff ([OSAC 2021-N-0016](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/02/27/OSAC%202021-N-0016%20Standard%20for%20Initial%20Response%20at%20Scenes%20by%20Law%20Enforcement%20Version%202.1_0.pdf); [College of Policing](https://www.college.police.uk/app/investigation/investigation-process)).
3. Fix an oversized boundary (outer and inner cordon, one access control point); shrink later. **Fail:** boundary "set too narrowly".
4. Start one scene log: name, rank or number in block letters, time in and out, reason, PPE worn; record logger handovers; the log is an exhibit ([PSNI SI0616](https://www.psni.police.uk/sites/default/files/2023-11/Serious%20Crime%20Scenes%208%20September%202021.pdf); [FSR-G-206](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/393866/206_FSR_SOC_contamination_consultation.pdf)). **Fail:** any entry without a log line; a second log.
5. Define a common approach path away from the offender's probable entry and exit; lay stepping plates where floor marks are possible ([FutureLearn](https://futurelearn.com/info/courses/science-behind-forensic-science/0/steps/56532); [FSR-GUI-0006](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a8709243d82f78d5c5145d3/FSR-GUI-0006_Incident_Scene_Examination_Issue_2.pdf)).
6. Record everything touched or moved and why; hand over to the CSI with the log and a written account ([UNODC](https://www.unodc.org/documents/scientific/Crime_scene_awareness__Ebook.pdf)).
7. Oman variant: notify a Public Prosecution member; produce a signed inspection report ([Royal Decree 97/99](https://bwcimplementation.org/sites/default/files/resource/OM_Criminal%20Procedures%20Code_EN.pdf)).
8. Tell the owner not to clean, repair or re-secure before examination ([Gloucestershire pack](https://www.gloucestershire.police.uk/SysSiteAssets/media/images/gloucestershire/html-email/2020-burglary-pack/2020-10-burglary-advice-pack.pdf)).

### B. CSI arrival, PPE, assessment and strategy

1. Park at the rendezvous point outside the cordon; check cordon and log adequacy ([FSR-G-206](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/393866/206_FSR_SOC_contamination_consultation.pdf)).
2. Don barrier clothing before crossing the inner cordon: suit with hood, overshoes, mask, two pairs of nitrile gloves ([FSR Code §103.5](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statutory-code-of-practice-for-forensic-science-activities/forensic-science-regulator-code-of-practice-accessible); [FSR-GUI-0017](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dna-contamination-controls-forensic-medical-examinations/dna-contamination-controls-forensic-medical-examinations-accessible)).
3. Walk through along the CAP without touching; identify fragile and transient evidence and threats to integrity ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)).
4. Open notes: arrival time, weather, temperature, lighting, persons present, state of shutter, door, glass, alarm panel, CCTV, lights and locks as found ([Winchester policy 111](https://www.winchesterva.gov/files/assets/public/v/2/sheriff/111-crime-scene-processing.pdf); [SAPD](https://public.powerdms.com/SAPDCA/documents/1004910)).
5. Write the examination strategy: entry, route, targets, exit; order of recovery; rationale for choices ([FSR Code v2 §90.6](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2-accessible)).
6. Choose a search pattern: zone for the shop interior, strip or grid outside ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/applied-sciences/crime-scene-search-patterns/)).
7. Set up the temporary exhibit storage area ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)).
8. Change outer gloves between exhibits and areas, before handling kit, after touching common surfaces. **Fail:** same gloves across two exhibits; no mask while swabbing; leaning over the entry point unsuited.

### C. Photography, sketch and photo log

1. Exterior establishing shots, then overalls of each room from the corners at normal height with lighting as found ([Maine State Police](https://www.maine.gov/dps/msp/sites/maine.gov.dps.msp/files/inline-files/Crime%20Scene%20Photography.pdf)).
2. Mid-range shots tying each item to a fixed feature (10–20 ft, isosceles triangle) ([Police1](https://www.police1.com/investigations/articles/a-better-understanding-of-midrange-photographs-in-a-crime-scene-sxveGuGs1YbLkC1n/)).
3. Close-ups at 90° to the surface, fill the frame, first without then with scale and numbered marker; scale on the same plane, beside not on an impression ([Forensics Digest](https://forensicsdigest.com/crime-scene-photography/); [CSEP ch. 7](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/CSEP/chapters/crime-scene-and-evidence-photography-2nd-edition-chapter7.pdf)).
4. Entry point: mid-range and close-up from both exterior and interior ([CSEP ch. 5](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/CSEP/chapters/crime-scene-and-evidence-photography-2nd-edition-chapter5.pdf)).
5. Photo log: photographer, date-time, case number, camera, lens, lighting, exposure, description, location ([Documentation outline](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/crime-scene-documentation-outline.pdf)). **Fail:** deleting any frame; photographing after touching.
6. Rough sketch before anything moves, never altered afterwards; measurements within ¼ in from fixed points (walls); north arrow, scale or "not to scale", legend, evidence numbers matching the exhibit log ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf); [SkyeBrowse](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/crime-scene-sketch.md)).
7. Choose the coordinate method: rectangular from two walls indoors, baseline on the pavement, triangulation in irregular layouts ([OSAC 2023-N-0003](https://www.nist.gov/document/osac-2023-n-0003-standard-diagramming-scenes-version-21)).

### D. Latent fingermark: powder, photograph, lift

1. Classify the surface: non-porous (glass, chrome, polished counter) powder in place; porous or small movable items bag for the lab un-powdered; wet surface switch to SPR ([Sourcebook ch. 7](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/the-fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development.pdf); [SPR review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2090536X1630065X)).
2. Torch at a grazing angle, sweep; then 450 nm blue with orange goggles in a darkened room ([TBI LP-0001](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/latent-print/sop-manual/LP-0001.pdf); [NIJ ALS study](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/nij-landscape-study-of-alternate-light-sources.pdf)). **Fail:** powdering a visible dust or blood mark.
3. Photograph any visible mark before touching it.
4. If the area is a likely DNA contact point and blood or biological material is present, swab first ([NIJ 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)).
5. Select powder and brush: aluminium with a glass-fibre brush on glass and smooth surfaces; black granular where contrast requires; black magnetic with a wand on textured plastic and u-PVC (never aluminium or granular black there; never magnetic on steel); fluorescent plus ALS on patterned backgrounds ([HOSDB 09/07](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf); [Sourcebook ch. 7](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf)).
6. Decant a little powder; tip only the bristle ends into it; tap off excess; place a test print in an untouched area ([Sirchie manual](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/l/a/latent_print_development_manual_4.pdf)). **Fail:** dipping the brush in the jar; visible heaps of powder.
7. Light circular or twirling strokes with only the tips touching; on first ridges switch to strokes along ridge flow; stop when crisp; clear excess with a clean brush ([Santa Barbara SOP](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf); [DC DFS](https://dfs.qualtraxcloud.com/ShowDocument.aspx?ID=10172)). **Fail:** pressure or passes beyond threshold; filled-in ridges.
8. Magnetic wand: pick up a powder ball, pass it over the surface, only powder touches; retract rod to drop powder ([FBI Processing Guide](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/jan2001/lpu.pdf)).
9. Photograph the developed mark with scale and mark number, camera parallel, examination quality, before lifting ([Illinois SP](https://isp.illinois.gov/StaticFiles/docs/ForensicServices/ForensicScienceCommandManuals/Latent%20Prints.pdf)).
10. Lift: one piece of tape wide enough to cover the whole mark; anchor about an inch beyond it; press down with a card edge in one smooth motion; rub out bubbles; peel in one continuous motion keeping tension; lay on the backing card (white for black powder, black for aluminium, white or fluorescent) ([Spraggs](https://www.policemag.com/patrol/article/15349075/how-to-lift-fingerprints); [Santa Barbara SOP](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf)). Use a gel lifter on textured surfaces and gloves ([BVDA](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters)). **Fail:** tape folded on itself; bubble through ridges; second lift attempted as if equal.
11. Label the card: case number, unique lift number, date, location text, lifter's name, sketch of the object with an orientation arrow ([WSP manual](https://wsp.wa.gov/forensics/docs/crimelab/manuals/technical/latent/Latent%20Print%20Technical%20Manual%20R.%2022.pdf)).
12. After photographing, swab the powdered contact area for DNA where contact is known ([HOSDB 09/07](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)).
13. Arrange elimination prints from staff and owner ([FBI, Science of Fingerprints](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/fbiscienceoffingerprints.html)).
14. Debrief: grade the mark on CAST 0–4; show the bureau result as identified / excluded / inconclusive / insufficient ([Dstl newsletter](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f1ab58ee90e07456caf0c63/2020_Jul_Dstl_Fingermark_Visualisation_Newsletter__5_v1.0_O.pdf)).

### E. Footwear mark in dust (floor, showcase glass, fallen glass)

1. Darken; sweep with a low-angle torch from the entry point inward; mark each find ([DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:18227/FULLTEXT01.pdf)). Treat uncertain origin as dry origin.
2. Protect the mark from footfall with plates. **Fail:** walking over it.
3. Overall and mid-range photographs, then examination-quality: tripod, sensor parallel, scale on the same plane, manual focus, f/11–16, RAW, oblique flash at ~10°, 30°, 45° from at least three positions ([SWGDE 17-P-003](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/17-p-003-swgde-guidelines-for-the-digital-imaging-of-footwear-and-tire-impressions/); [Virginia DFS](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf)).
4. Electrostatic lift first: film black side down with margin; ground attached; probe on; roll out air; probe off and discharged; film lifted and taped dust-side-up in a box; never reuse film; examine with oblique light in the dark ([TBI CS010.13](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/crime-scene/CS010.13.pdf); [SWGTREAD lifting](https://www.nist.gov/document/swgtread12guideforliftingfootwearandtireimpressionevidence200703pdf)). Not on carpet.
5. Then black gelatin lifter: hold by the short ends, apply one end first, roll down, optional dwell up to 20 min, peel, replace cover film ([BVDA](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters-shoeprints)).
6. A glass shard carrying a mark is packaged whole in a rigid box ([Santa Barbara SOP-CSI-004](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-004-12-Trace-Evidence-Collection.pdf)).
7. Label lift with case, exhibit number, location, date, initials and orientation arrow.

### F. Footwear impression in soil or sand (exterior)

1. Photograph as in E3 before casting ([SWGDE 17-P-003](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/17-p-003-swgde-guidelines-for-the-digital-imaging-of-footwear-and-tire-impressions/)).
2. Optional fixative on fragile impressions; frame around the impression ([SWGTREAD casting](https://www.nist.gov/document/swgtread11guideforcastingfootwearandtireimpressionevidence200703pdf)).
3. Mix about 2 lb dental stone with roughly 8–12 oz water (product dependent) by kneading the bag to heavy-cream consistency ([SWGTREAD](https://www.nist.gov/document/swgtread11guideforcastingfootwearandtireimpressionevidence200703pdf); [Virginia DFS](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf)). **Fail:** plaster of Paris; lumps; pouring directly onto the impression.
4. Pour outside the impression and let it flow in; leave undisturbed at least 30 min ([crime-scene-investigator.net](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/footwear.html)).
5. Scratch initials, date, case and exhibit number and a north arrow into the back before it hardens ([IAAI](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/3-d-impressions)).
6. Lift, bag in paper without cleaning; air-dry 48 h before any cleaning ([SWGTREAD mirror](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-casting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html)).

### G. Tool mark on frame, lock or safe

1. Photograph with normal and oblique light, with and without scale, lens perpendicular, then close oblique angles ([Santa Barbara SOP-CSI-006](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-006-12-Firearms-and-Toolmarks-Evidence-Collection.pdf)).
2. Look for and recover paint, metal or mortar transfer first ([IAAI](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/toolmarks)).
3. Swab the area for touch DNA if a bare hand or glove contact is plausible (*unsourced for tool marks specifically*).
4. Recover the whole lock, latch or moulding if removable; otherwise cut out with a wide margin; otherwise cast ([Indiana SP PEB-13](https://secure.in.gov/isp/labs/files/PEB-13_Toolmark-_7-1-25.pdf); [IAAI substrate](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/toolmark-on-substrate)).
5. Mikrosil: 1.5–2 in base, slightly shorter catalyst strip, mix 20–30 s until no blue streaks, apply within about a minute, wait 6–10 min (longer in cold), peel; brown for tool marks ([Sirchie Mikrosil](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item//M/C/MCM100_TI02-59ENG-REV4E.pdf); [Tri-Tech](https://tritechforensics.com/mikrosil-casting-putty-kit/)).
6. Package the marked item padded against rubbing; never in the same container as a tool; never fit a suspect tool to the mark ([NH State Police](https://www.nhsp.dos.nh.gov/our-services/forensic-laboratory/firearms-toolmarks/toolmark-analysis); [CA DOJ PEB 27](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/peb-27.pdf)). **Fail:** bringing a seized crowbar near the mark.

### H. Glass at the shopfront and display cases

1. Photograph the pane in the frame and the fragment field inside and outside with scales and markers.
2. Check jagged edges and fallen shards for blood, fibres and footwear marks before anything is moved; recover those first (sections I, K).
3. Mark "inside" and "outside" on large pane remnants before removal; take a known control from each broken item directly from the source ([Evidence collection guidelines](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/collect.html)).
4. Collect interior and exterior fragments as separate exhibits; questioned and known separately; rigid containers (can, box, vial), sealed and initialled across the seal ([WSP Glass](https://wsp.wa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/J.-Glass.pdf)).
5. Note the glass type: annealed (radial/concentric readable), tempered (dices, no direction reading), laminated (cracks, holds, cuts hands) ([FBI GEO-510-06](https://fbilabqsd.fbi.gov/file-repository/trace-evidence/geo-510-06-fractography-of-brittle-materials.pdf)).
6. Brick or hammer left behind: photograph in place, note adhering glass or paint, swab for DNA, box rigid.
7. Flag for the investigator that a suspect's clothing, hair and shoes must be seized within hours and wrapped in paper ([Girard](https://samples.jbpub.com/9781284142617/9781284142617_CH05_Girard_SECURE.pdf)).

### I. Visible blood

1. Search with white light, oblique light, then 415 nm violet with yellow or orange goggles ([Horiba](https://www.horiba.com/int/scientific/applications/others/pages/detection-of-body-fluids-with-an-alternate-light-source/)).
2. Photograph each stain group with scale; classify (drip, transfer, cast-off, wipe); measure axes of a few elliptical drops ([ASB TR 033](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/033_TR_e1_2017.pdf)).
3. Presumptive test on a swab from the stain edge (*edge rule unsourced*): reagent, then peroxide; pink within 10 s is positive; colour before peroxide is invalid; run positive and negative controls ([Science Notes](https://sciencenotes.org/kastle-meyer-test-for-blood/); [AAFS 2011](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2011-A139.pdf)). **Fail:** reading after 30 s.
4. New gloves and mask; moisten a sterile swab with distilled water dropped from about ½ in (never saline, never touching ampoule to swab or stain); concentrate the stain on the tip; follow with a dry swab ([NIJ dry stains](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/evidence-collection/dry-stains-non-absorbent-surfaces)).
5. Control swab from an adjacent unstained area.
6. Air-dry swabs; one stain per swab; swab box or paper envelope; never plastic ([SJSU](https://gcp-web.sjsu.edu/people/steven.lee/courses/c2/s1/DNA_Sample_Handling%201.pdf); [LibreTexts](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Hartnell_College/The_Introduction_to_the_Forensic_Sciences/14%3A_Evidence_Collection_and_Preservation/14.03%3A_Bloodstain_collection)).
7. A bloodstained small item (shard, glove) is seized whole, not folded on itself, in paper or a rigid box marked sharp and biohazard ([NIJ packaging](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/evidence-collection/biological-evidence-packaging); [LAB-BP-01](https://crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/best-practices-for-collection-packaging-storage-preservation-and-retrieval-of-biological-evidence.pdf)).
8. Label and seal before moving to the next item.

### J. Latent blood (wiped surface)

1. Photograph and sample any visible staining first.
2. Blackout (lights off, shutters down or night); five minutes of eye adaptation; tripod set ([BLUESTAR guide](https://www.bluestar-forensic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/USER_GUIDE_medium_kit_EN_20231511.pdf)).
3. Positive control on known blood; negative control on a nearby clean area ([BLUESTAR scene page](https://www.bluestar-forensic.com/use-of-bluestar-at-a-suspected-crime-scene/)).
4. Spray a fine mist from at least 50 cm, sweeping; glow lasts about a minute; re-spray to refresh.
5. Long exposure photograph (vendor ISO 800, f/8, 8 s) ([BLUESTAR photography](https://www.bluestar-forensic.com/2016/07/)).
6. Swab positives for DNA (reagent does not degrade STR DNA); confirm with an immunochromatographic test; treat bleach residue and metal fittings as false-positive traps ([Almeida 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/20643520)).

### K. Touch DNA swab (handles, runners, tools, gloves)

1. Photograph the item or area in situ with marker.
2. New gloves, mask on; sterile swab moistened with sterile water; firm rotating pressure about 30 s; dry swab follow-up ([UTS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/170325/3/Effect%20of%20swabbing%20technique%20and%20duration%20on%20forensic%20DNA%20recovery.pdf)). **Fail:** touching the swab tip; talking over the item (*unsourced*).
3. Gloves found at scene: swab or cut interior fingertips and cuff ([AAFS 2006 B31](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2006-B31.pdf)).
4. Air-dry; swab box or paper envelope; label and seal before the next item.
5. Debrief: a profile shows presence, not timing or activity ([Pfeifer & Wiegand](https://www.springermedizin.de/persistence-of-touch-dna-on-burglary-related-tools/12075566); [Meehan v. State](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1665077.html)).

### L. Hairs and fibres

1. Oblique white light, then violet or blue ALS ([Foster + Freeman](https://fosterfreeman.com/?p=18173)).
2. Photograph with scale.
3. Single visible fibres or hairs: clean forceps onto trace paper, druggist fold, then envelope; never loose in an envelope; do not tape-seal the fold ([FIU T08](https://archive.gfjc.fiu.edu/property_crimes/module03/pro_m03_t08.htm); [Maine Druggist Fold Guide](https://www.maine.gov/dps/msp/sites/maine.gov.dps.msp/files/inline-files/Druggist%20Fold%20Guide.pdf)).
4. Invisible fibres on frame or floor: low-tack tape lift onto acetate; keep lifts from sticking to anything ([CA DOJ PEB 6](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/peb-06.pdf)).
5. Collect reference fibres from shop carpet and staff uniforms.

### M. Saliva and abandoned items (cigarette end, can, mask, cap)

1. Photograph in situ with marker.
2. Forceps or fresh gloves touching the minimum area (not filter tip, rim or mouth area; *unsourced*).
3. One item per paper envelope or bag; drain or dry wet containers; biohazard mark; label and seal ([NSW exhibit](https://lgbtiq.specialcommission.nsw.gov.au/assets/lgbtiq/hearings/20231010/exhibits/Exhibit-64/Exhibit-64-Tab-039-NPL.0131.0001.1909.pdf); [LAB-BP-01](https://crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/best-practices-for-collection-packaging-storage-preservation-and-retrieval-of-biological-evidence.pdf)).

### N. Exhibit packaging, labelling and continuity (every item)

1. One item, one package; paper or breathable for wet or biological; tamper-evident bag for dry items; rigid box for glass, sharps and casts ([Iowa DPS](https://dps.iowa.gov/media/111/download); [Home Office TEB](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tamper-evident-bags/tamper-evident-bags-accessible-version)).
2. Label before sealing: unique reference (configured format), description, location, date-time, recovering officer; seal; initials and date across the seal; record the bag's seal number ([Home Office TEB](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/951472/tamper-evident-bags-v7.0-gov-uk.pdf); [NIJ packaging](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/evidence-collection/biological-evidence-packaging)).
3. Enter in the exhibit log with photo number and sketch coordinate; every handover signed with time ([NIST IR 7928](https://nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/NIST-IR-7928.pdf)). **Fail:** wet item in sealed plastic; two items in one bag; unsealed or unlabelled item left at the scene; missing seal number.

### O. Scene release

1. Final survey: all exhibits reconciled against exhibit log and photo log; all kit, plates and markers gathered; hidden areas rechecked; documentation checked ([NIJ NCJ 200160](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/200160.pdf)).
2. On-scene debrief with the team; formal debrief within 24 h ([Response guidelines](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/respon3.html)).
3. Explain residue and damage to the owner; say what can be cleaned and when; victim information and update schedule ([Security Guides](https://securityguides.co.uk/reporting-crime/after-burglary/police-attendance/); [Met Police](https://www.met.police.uk/SysSiteAssets/media/downloads/met/victim-information.pdf)).
4. Collect the retailer pack: stock list with serials and certificate numbers, stock photographs, CCTV export with clock offset, alarm event log (*composition is design inference*).
5. Record release time, date, releasing officer and recipient in the scene log; close the log ([Response guidelines](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/respon3.html)). **Fail:** release without final survey.

## Conclusion

The sources turn out to be more useful as a grammar than as a script. They do not hand the designer a jewellery-store protocol; they hand over a small set of ordering rules (transient before stable, biological and trace before powder, photograph before touch, non-destructive before destructive, one item one package, record now not later) plus a route (entry, path, target, exit) and a handful of material constants. Those rules are what make the scene scorable, because every failure mode in the training literature is a violation of one of them, and a VR scene is the only training venue in which the ground truth needed to detect the violation is fully known, which is also the FSR's own stated requirement for assessing initial competence in scene approach. The practical consequence is that the simulator should be built around a small number of global invariants enforced everywhere, with jurisdiction-specific skins (exhibit reference format, bag type, who gets notified) layered on top, rather than around a long linear procedure.

Two findings should change how the project frames itself. First, the published VR evidence supports parity with physical mock scenes and gains in throughput and repeatability, with the weakest results on learning when feedback is left to the headset; the debrief, with its CAST grade, its bureau verdict, its contamination-database hit and its "presence is not activity" caution, is therefore not an add-on but the part that carries the training value. Second, for an Oman deployment the most defensible procedural baseline is UK practice as already adopted by GCC police academies through the Scottish Police College, with the Omani Criminal Procedure Code supplying the legal actors; the missing piece is a Royal Oman Police procedure document, and obtaining it, together with full reads of FSR-GUI-0016 and the Fingermark Visualisation Manual, is the first research task before any training text is printed.
