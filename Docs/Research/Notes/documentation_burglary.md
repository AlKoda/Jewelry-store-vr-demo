## Photography: progression, scales, axis, lighting, markers, photo log, video, counts

### Takeaway
Every authoritative guide (NIJ/NFSTC 2013 [US], OSAC proposed standard 2020 [US], Robinson's textbook) prescribes the same three-tier progression – overall, midrange, close-up – with evidence shot both with and without scales/markers, close-ups at 90 degrees to the surface, and a photo log that establishes authenticity. No source gives a typical photo count for a burglary scene.

### Cited Findings
- [US] The NIJ/NFSTC guide (2013 edition) specifies photographing the "Scene utilizing overall, medium, and close-up coverage" and "Evidence to be collected with and without measurement scale and/or evidence identifiers." — [Crime Scene Investigation: A Guide for Law Enforcement, 2013 (NIST copy)](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf); also [OJP copy NCJ 243598](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/ncjrs/243598.pdf); earlier 2000 edition [NIJ 178280](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/178280.pdf)
- [US] The same guide: "The scene 'walk-through' provides an overview of the entire scene, identifies any threats to scene integrity, and ensures protection of physical evidence." — [NIJ/NFSTC 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)
- [US] The guide's supporting document is "developed by the Technical Working Group on Crime Scene Investigation", September 2013 update, "supported by the Bureau of Justice Assistance and NIJ"; the opinions "represent the authors' consensus rather than official DOJ positions". — [NIST hosting page](https://www.nist.gov/document/crime-scene-investigationpdf)
- [US] NIJ archived training: "The primary means of crime scene documentation is still photography. Police officers should have an understanding of the importance of keeping the scene preserved, and not moving anything until it is photographed." — [NIJ, Collecting DNA Evidence at Property Crime Scenes: Documentation](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/crime-scenes/documentation)
- Robinson's categories (secondary summaries of *Crime Scene Photography*, 3rd ed., Elsevier): overall photos "show the general conditions of the scene, inside and out, and how it fits its surroundings"; midrange photos exist "to show how a piece of evidence relates to a fixed feature that was already captured in an overall"; Robinson's "three cardinal rules" are "fill the frame, maximize depth of field, and keep the film plane parallel"; a non-parallel film plane "hides the true distance between the evidence and the reference object"; a common midrange method is "an isosceles triangle: the camera, the item, and a prominent scene feature form the triangle". — [Police1, "A better understanding of midrange photographs"](https://www.police1.com/investigations/articles/a-better-understanding-of-midrange-photographs-in-a-crime-scene-sxveGuGs1YbLkC1n/); [Robinson, Crime Scene Photography 3rd ed. (Elsevier)](https://www.educate.elsevier.com/book/details/9780128027646)
- Close-ups "are taken with and without a scale. Photographing at 90 degrees ... helps prevent parallax error. Scales should sit as close to the evidence as possible without disturbing it, on the same plane as the item." — [Forensics Digest, Crime Scene Photography](https://forensicsdigest.com/crime-scene-photography/)
- [US] A police evidence-photography bulletin lists reasons to use scales: "showcasing the relative size of an object" and "to determine the size at the time the incident occurred for something with a non-fixed size (e.g., wounds)". — [VCCCD Basic Evidence Photography bulletin 24-009](https://www.vcccd.edu/sites/default/files/media/pdf_document/2024/24-009%20Basic%20Evidence%20Photography_Redacted.pdf)
- Textbook caution: "not every item of evidence documented photographically at a crime scene requires a scale in the photograph"; where a scale cannot go on the evidence it "may be placed beside the evidence, since putting it on an impression can damage it". — [Crime Scene and Evidence Photography, 2nd ed., ch. 7](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/CSEP/chapters/crime-scene-and-evidence-photography-2nd-edition-chapter7.pdf)
- [US] NIST-affiliated review of scales: "the presence of such scales in an image allows investigators to reconstruct the dimensional context of a scene and provides a means to reproduce one-to-one photographs of physical evidence"; the "ABFO No. 2 reference scale is widely recognised, but commercially available scales vary in manufacturing consistency, so they don't always meet the standard". — [Dimensional Review of Scales for Forensic Photography](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/dimensional-review-of-scales-for-forensic-photography.html)
- [US] OSAC "Standard Guide for Crime Scene Photography" (proposed June 2020) describes itself as "a general guide outlining best practices for all practitioners of crime scene photography"; it was at that date "a draft that has not been published by an SDO". — [NIST / OSAC proposed standard PDF](https://nist.gov/system/files/documents/2020/06/26/Crime%20Scene%20Photography%20Doc_OSAC%20Proposed_June2020.pdf); [NIST listing](https://www.nist.gov/document/standard-guide-crime-scene-photography)
- Impression evidence conventions: "fingerprints, tool marks, and footwear impressions be photographed close-up"; a "scale in the photographs lets prints be reprinted at actual size"; photograph the entry point "with mid-range and close-up views from both the exterior and interior of the building". — [Crime Scene and Evidence Photography ch. 5](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/CSEP/chapters/crime-scene-and-evidence-photography-2nd-edition-chapter5.pdf); [Burglary Investigations, Crime and Clues](https://crimeandclues.com/2013/01/26/burglary-investigations/)
- [US] Phoenix PD crime lab procedure: "using an oblique angle, white light can be a useful tool when searching for footwear impressions"; ALS targets listed include "fibers, paint, hairs, and glass". — [Phoenix PD Crime Lab QT document 1700](https://crimelab.phoenix.gov/QTDocuments/1700.PDF)
- ALS filters (vendor data, not peer-reviewed): yellow barrier filter "commonly used as a barrier filter with a Forensic Light Source emitting UV to violet blue light", rated for "Blue Lasers (315-460nm)"; orange long-pass filter rated for "315-532nm" and "blocks outside laser/ALS light and passes longer wavelengths emitted from trace evidence samples"; "longer wavelength (orange) background fluorescence sometimes overpowers shorter wavelength (yellow) treated and untreated evidence fluorescence". — [Arrowhead Forensics yellow filter 62 mm](https://arrowheadforensics.com/yellow-filter---62-mm); [Arrowhead orange filter](https://arrowheadforensics.com/orange-filter.html); [Arrowhead Filter Formula brochure](https://arrowheadforensics.com/content/a-z/Filter-Formula-Brochure.pdf)
- [US] NIJ landscape study on ALS: fluorescence "is emitted at a longer wavelength than the light that excited it"; visible-range ALS devices emit "400–700 nanometers [nm] within the electromagnetic spectrum". — [NIJ grant report 243213](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/grants/243213.pdf)
- Photo log contents: a sample form's columns are "Date Time Description Location Notes"; a mobile form tracks "the offense, the victim, the scene of the crime, the address, the photographer, the camera used, lens and flash used"; "Exif/Meta Data includes camera make, model, date, time, image number, lens focal length, aperture, shutter speed". For court, "a photo must be authentic — the evidence is what the proponent claims it to be"; a checklist "could have prevented a settings error in which the shutter speed was set above the camera's flash sync speed". — [Elsevier photo log form](https://booksite.elsevier.com/9781455731237/downloads/appendixforms/photo_log.pdf); [GoCanvas Crime Scene Photographic Log](https://www.gocanvas.com/mobile-forms-apps/33408-Crime-Scene-Photographic-Log); [Officer.com, "Checking your list"](https://officer.com/home/article/10249652/checking-your-list-and-checking-it-twice)
- [UK] Glass and marks: "marks suspected to be from tools are examined microscopically"; "footwear marks are captured and enhanced using specialist lighting and photography before comparison with reference items". — [Forensic Science NI, Physical methods](https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/articles/physical-methods); [FSNI Glass evidence](https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/node/12919)

### Inferences
- For the VR sim, a defensible photographic sequence is: exterior establishing shots of the shop front and street, overall interior shots from each corner, midrange shots linking each evidence item (point-of-entry glass, display case, dropped tool/bag, footwear marks in glass dust) to a fixed feature, then close-ups at 90 degrees, first without and then with an L-scale and numbered marker.
- Oblique white light for dust/glass-fragment footwear marks and blue ALS (c. 445–450 nm) with a yellow or orange barrier filter for body fluids/fibres are the two lighting modes worth modelling.

- No source gives a typical number of photographs for a burglary scene; none found despite targeted searching.
- Could not read the OSAC 2020 standard or FSR photography guidance text directly; the UK Forensic Science Regulator's photography-specific guidance did not surface in searches at all.
- No source on video walk-through narration conventions was found.

## 3D scanning, 360-degree cameras, photogrammetry and total stations in modern practice

### Takeaway
Terrestrial laser scanners (FARO Focus, Leica RTC360) are documented in use by US and German police mainly for homicides, shootings and crashes; all case material is vendor-published or local news. Nothing indicates laser scanning is routine at volume-crime burglaries, and no standard exists for 360-camera photogrammetry at crime scenes.

### Cited Findings
- [US] Colorado Springs PD used a FARO Focus on a 2016 mass-shooting scene "and two other locations"; FARO states the scan "captured the scene in under an hour and supported a 360-degree representation for court". — [FARO case study, Colorado Springs](https://www.faro.com/en/Resource-Library/Case-Study/colorado-springs-pd-uses-laser-scanner-to-document-mass-shooting); [Geospatial World](https://resource.geospatialworld.net/casestudy/enhancing-crime-scene-investigations-using-laser-scanning-technology)
- [US] Kearney PD (Nebraska) adopted a FARO Focus "to speed up documentation of crash and crime scenes, and the 3D point cloud was useful in court" (vendor account). — [FARO case study, Kearney](https://www.faro.com/en/Resource-Library/Case-Study/agency-chooses-3d-laser-scanner-to-replace-manual-crime-scene-documentation)
- [US] Clark County Sheriff's Office (WA) detective uses a FARO Focus3D "to capture evidence that might otherwise be missed, such as items under a body" and "rods and spheres to determine bullet trajectory angles". — [Officer.com, Capture the Crime Scene](https://www.officer.com/article/12339566/3d-crime-scene-documentation-for-law-enforcement)
- [Germany] Saxon State Office of Criminal Investigation used a FARO Photon in a double-murder investigation. — [Laserscanning Europe](https://www.laserscanning-europe.com/de/node/1903)
- Leica RTC360 "captures up to 2 million points per second and pre-registers scans in the field"; used for "crime scene mapping and traffic reconstruction" (vendor). Spokane PD adopted the RTC360 "after its ScanStation P30 funding stalled". Toledo PD uses an RTC360 at homicide scenes "at a cost of about $100,000". — [Leica Reporter 85](https://leica-geosystems.com/da/about-us/news-room/customer-magazine/reporter-85/12-changing-the-game-for-public-safety-professionals-with-the-worlds-fastest-laser-scanner); [Leica Spokane PD case study](https://leica-geosystems.com/industries/public-safety-security-and-forensics/learning-centre-for-public-safety-professionals/transitioning-to-3d-workflows-heres-how-spokane-pd-made-it-fast-and-simple); [13abc Toledo](https://www.13abc.com/2023/09/12/3d-scanner-gives-tpd-detailed-evidence)
- 360-camera photogrammetry: a 2022 SAE study compared three 360-degree cameras against RTC360 point clouds outdoors and found distances "suitable for typical accident reconstruction" – a validation study, not a procedural standard. — [SAE 2022-01-0829](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/accuracy-validation-360-degree-camera-use-photogrammetry-2022-01-0829)
- Total station: operator "holds a reflective prism at each evidence point"; "the data collector records the distance, vertical and horizontal angles, and elevation for each 'shot' (evidence point)". Washington and Kentucky studies found officers "record one-third more data points in about half the time it took to measure by hand". Michigan State Police describe "the use of a total station for measuring a scene and CAD software for generating a scale diagram". — [NAFI, Total Station Surveying Technology for Forensic Mapping](https://www.nafi.org/blog/?p=269); [Michigan State Police](https://www.michigan.gov/msp/0,4643,7-123-72297_25680-16187--,00.html)
- Leica Evidence Recorder lets users "review their measurements in 2D or 3D and confirm there are no errors or missing points before leaving the scene". — [Leica public safety solutions](https://leica-geosystems.com/en-SG/industries/public-safety-security-and-forensics/solutions)

### Inferences
- A laser scanner or 360 camera is a plausible optional "major scene" tool in the sim but should not be presented as standard for a volume burglary; the standard remains camera + tape/laser measurer + sketch.

## Sketching and measuring: rough vs final, coordinate methods, what to include

### Takeaway
The rough sketch is drawn at scene before anything is moved and is never altered; the final is drawn later. Four measurement methods are standard (rectangular coordinates, triangulation, baseline, polar coordinates), measured from fixed reference points, with the NIJ guide demanding quarter-inch accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- [US] NIJ/NFSTC 2013: investigators "draw a rough sketch before anything is moved, and they should not alter it afterward"; sketch types: "a sketch of the surrounding area, a measurements-only sketch, or a sketch showing where evidence and victims were located" – "Depending on the crime, draw one or all of these types of sketches"; "Measurements should be accurate to within ¼"."; "Take measurements from fixed location reference point, such as walls or curbs, or from stationary appliances."; measurements "cover room dimensions, doors, windows, and distances between objects and people". — [NIJ/NFSTC 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)
- [US] NIJ archived training: "Investigators usually complete hand-drawn, rough sketches while at the crime scene. These sketches contain all the necessary information for the investigator to subsequently complete a finalized version." Sketches "supplement photos where spatial relationships are hard to show". — [NIJ, Documentation](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/crime-scenes/documentation)
- [US] OSAC "Standard for Diagramming Scenes" (OSAC 2023-N-0003, v2.0/2.1): baseline method – "measurements are then made perpendicular from this tape by laying another tape measure at a 90-degree angle to the first tape and measuring out to the evidence"; polar coordinates – "measures the distance and direction (angle) of an object from a known reference point", angle from "a large protractor or an optical device such as a transit or a compass". — [OSAC 2023-N-0003 v2.1](https://www.nist.gov/document/osac-2023-n-0003-standard-diagramming-scenes-version-21)
- Triangulation: "measurements from two permanent fixed points to each item"; "the most accurate way to measure a scene, but it can require more people measuring the scene and reference points are needed"; "can be used indoors or outdoors; it is a good method to use in areas lacking straight lines". Rectangular coordinates: "the distance to an object from two mutually perpendicular objects, such as walls that meet at a 90-degree angle". — [Benha University forensic lecture, Sketching](https://fmed.stafpu.bu.edu.eg/Forensic%20medicine%20&%20clinical%20%20toxicology/303/crs-18978/lecture%20-%20sketching.pdf); [Forensic Mapping, crime-scene-investigator.net](https://crime-scene-investigator.net/forensicmapping.html)
- Required elements: "every sketch needs case identifiers, a north arrow, a scale or 'not to scale' note, a legend and numbered evidence that matches the evidence log"; "all identification symbols used on the sketch are included and defined on the legend". — [SkyeBrowse, Crime Scene Sketch (secondary summary of NIJ guide)](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/crime-scene-sketch.md); [Crime Scene Documentation outline](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/crime-scene-documentation-outline.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a rectangular retail unit, rectangular coordinates from two walls is the natural indoor method; triangulation from door jambs/pillars suits irregular shop layouts; baseline suits the pavement outside (vehicle position, dropped items).

### Gaps
- No source quantified acceptable measurement tolerance other than the NIJ quarter-inch figure; no UK-specific sketch standard was found.

## Notes and the scene attendance log

### Takeaway
Notes begin on arrival and record conditions as found (lighting, weather, temperature, state of doors/windows/locks/switches/alarm), who was present and when, and all actions taken. The UK FSR requires the rationale for the chosen approach to be recorded; no source was found that explicitly mandates separating observations from inferences.

### Cited Findings
- [US] NIJ archived training: notes "start at arrival and are updated throughout the investigation, covering items like lighting, odors, and potential evidence locations"; record "the condition of the scene as it existed upon arrival". — [NIJ, Documentation](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/collecting-dna-evidence-at-property-crime-scenes/crime-scenes/documentation)
- [US] Winchester (VA) Sheriff's Office crime scene processing policy checklist: "Lights on/off; Windows/doors open/closed, locked/unlocked"; narrative must include "weather and lighting conditions" and "the name(s) of additional scene personnel". — [Winchester Sheriff policy 111](https://www.winchesterva.gov/files/assets/public/v/2/sheriff/111-crime-scene-processing.pdf)
- [US] San Antonio PD procedure: observations should include "lighting, weather, temperature, and the positions of switches, doors, and windows"; record "everyone's actions taken at scene, including arrival time". — [SAPD procedure, PowerDMS](https://public.powerdms.com/SAPDCA/documents/1004910)
- Report-writing text: "The weather could account for a lack of evidence" (e.g. missing blood or trace). — [LibreTexts 15.03 Crime Scene Reports](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Hartnell_College/The_Introduction_to_the_Forensic_Sciences/15%3A_Forensic_Report_Writing/15.03%3A_Crime_Scene_Reports)
- [UK] FSR-GUI-0006 Issue 2 (Incident Scene Examination): where two approaches are both acceptable, "the notes must record a sound rationale for the approach taken"; its requirements "do not distinguish between activities performed at volume and major incident scenes"; scope is "activities at incident scenes where the primary purpose is managing, searching, recording, or obtaining forensic science evidence". Issue 1 contents include "developing an examination strategy (Code 90.6)" and "technical records (Code 90.8)". — [FSR-GUI-0006 Issue 2 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a8709243d82f78d5c5145d3/FSR-GUI-0006_Incident_Scene_Examination_Issue_2.pdf); [GOV.UK publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/incident-scene-examination-fsr-gui-0006); [Issue 1 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/683d93c5d23a62e5d32680ac/FSR-GUI-0006+Incident+Scene+Examination+Issue+1+.pdf)

### Inferences
- A sim notes panel should capture: arrival time, weather/temperature, lighting, who was present (owner, first officer, CSI), condition of shutter/door/glass/alarm panel/lights on arrival, each action taken with time, and a free-text rationale line (UK FSR style).

### Gaps
- No source explicitly frames the "observation vs inference" rule for notes; treat as training convention rather than a cited requirement.
- Could not read FSR-GUI-0006 Code 90.8 (technical records) text; the search excerpt only confirmed it exists.

## Burglary routine: evidence sources, processing order, DNA-vs-powder sequencing, time budget, attendance criteria, forensic strategy

### Takeaway
Commercial burglary scenes are worked along the offender's path – point of entry, route, target (cases/safe), exit – with glass, toolmarks, footwear marks in dust, and blood on broken glass the dominant yields. UK data put fingermarks at about 45% of non-residential burglary scenes and DNA at about 10% of scenes; UK guidance (HOSDB 2007) says photograph the powdered mark then swab it for DNA, and research shows gel lifts recover both. No published average scene time or national commercial-burglary attendance criterion was found.

### Cited Findings
- [US] Petraco & Petraco's impression-evidence chapter describes "a multimillion-dollar commercial burglary with broken glass, footwear marks, and tool marks"; the entry point "was a window broken from outside", the safe "was opened with hand tools", and "plaster dust footwear prints led from the back office to the front door, which was the exit point". — [Petraco, Impression Evidence chapter 2016](https://jjcweb.jjay.cuny.edu/npetraco/pubs/Impression_Evidence_Chapter_2016r.pdf)
- Burglary investigation guide: "investigators should look along the routes from the point of entry to where items are missing or disturbed"; for broken glass, "checking the glass on the floor inside the window for impressions"; "Impressions on glass or doors can be recovered using fingerprint powders and lifting tape"; "pry and slip-lock tools leave scratches or other impressions, and the marks can be compared to any tools which are later recovered"; a broken tool tip "should prompt a search for the tool it came from". — [Crime and Clues, Burglary Investigations](https://crimeandclues.com/2013/01/26/burglary-investigations/)
- [UK] Home Office report *The use of forensic science in volume crime investigations* (hoor4305): "policies about the deployment of CSEs range from blanket attendance for certain crime types, to discretionary attendance on the basis of information provided to a call taker, or by first attending officers (FAOs)"; for discretionary cases "decisions to send a CSE will be influenced primarily by the potential to recover forensic material and the perceived seriousness of the offence"; "there are police force variations in crime scene attendance rates (even allowing for similar crime types)"; dwelling-burglary attendance estimated "about 85 per cent for 2004/05"; "just under one in three attended residential burglary scenes yielding fingerprints"; "DNA was retrieved at about ten per cent of all scenes visited"; failures to recover were "usually due to scenes being cleaned before examiners arrived, inaccessible scenes, or the offender barely disturbing anything". — [Home Office hoor4305](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7ad567e5274a34770e76f6/hoor4305.pdf)
- [UK] Fingermarks were present at "41%" of burglary-dwelling and vehicle crimes and "45 per cent of non-residential burglaries" (unscreened study); deploying a SOCO by crime type "was shown to have a significant impact on detection rates for dwelling burglaries, commercial burglaries and theft of motor vehicles". — [Lingwood, Smith & Bond 2015, IJPSM](https://hira.hope.ac.uk/3761/1/Lingwood%2C%20Smith%2C%20Bond%202015%20-%20IJPSM.pdf)
- [UK] Home Office PRG 1996 (Bridgeman & Taylor-Browne): routine SOCO attendance at every burglary "was a poor use of resources"; one Kent unit found "fingerprints found at 42% of referred scenes, with 30% of those giving a positive identification". — [Bridgeman & Taylor-Browne 1996](https://popcenter.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz3631/files/problems/burglary_home/PDFs/Bridgeman_TaylorBrowne_1996.pdf)
- [UK] NPCC: "all police forces in England and Wales are now attending every home burglary"; Home Office Counting Rules "retain a separate sub-category to cover burglary in business and commercial properties". Nottinghamshire: officers "'tag' the report for CSI attendance where appropriate"; "in the second quarter of 2022, 29% of burglary reports in Nottinghamshire were attended by a Crime Scene Investigator". — [Police Professional](https://policeprofessional.com/?p=138447); [Nottinghamshire PCC Accountability Board, Burglary Nov 2022](https://nottinghamshire.pcc.police.uk/Document-Library/Public-Information/Meetings/Accountability-Board/2022/December-2022/Agenda-item-6.2-Burglary-November-2022.pdf)
- [UK] HOSDB Fingerprint Powders Guidelines (March 2007): "Photography of the powdered mark on the surface and subsequent swabbing of the mark for DNA may yield better results. All fingerprint development processes, including powders, can be used to locate contact areas on a surface. This enables the scene examiner to swab areas where contact is known to have happened." — [HOSDB Fingerprint Powders Guidelines 2007](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)
- [UK] Staffordshire pseudo-operational trial: "the gelatine lifts displayed higher detectable levels of DNA along with recovering more donor alleles. The lifts also resulted in the least amount of damage to ridge detail"; "the nylons swabs displayed a low affinity for collecting DNA while removing all traces of the fingerprints"; "in some circumstances it may be possible to locate the fingermark prior to any contact of DNA recovery or visualisation process such as powder". — [Staffordshire eprints 6026](https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/6026/1/Accepted%20manuscipt%20DNA%20fingermark%20pseudo-operational%20trial.pdf); [Staffordshire eprints 2764](https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/2764/3/eprints2764.pdf)
- 2005 study: "both full and partial DNA profiles were recovered from used brushes and used powders, and transfer was occasionally seen between brushed surfaces"; "powder presence seems to inhibit the PCR process, so powder should be removed before amplification". — [ISFG proceedings paper](https://www.isfg.org/files/61bebd1f8e4d5e47364077a5adee68b15529d225.02005721_131595303851.pdf)
- 2017 study: infrared fluorescent powder fpNatural 1 showed "no significant effect ... on either the quantity or quality of recovered DNA". 2026 study: "fingerprint powders significantly reduce the quantity and quality of recovered DNA"; "non-ionic surfactants like Triton X-100 in swabbing improved DNA recovery and stability". — [Wolverhampton repository](https://wlv.openrepository.com/items/e5589f18-e26b-4131-9b1d-2498ab95cf31); [Hayati Journal 2026](https://journal.ipb.ac.id/hayati/article/view/64370)
- [AUS] Queensland RCT (c. 978 home-burglary scenes): the treatment group "had time pressure removed and more training" and was "encouraged to be more thorough and spend more time at these high-volume crime scenes"; the enhanced approach gave "greater evidence collection, identification, and solved crimes" but "was more costly in relation to time". — [Queensland Forensic Unlawful Entry Experiment](https://anzsebp.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/QLD_Forensic_Unlawful_Entry_Experiment-min.pdf); [Springer, J Exp Criminol 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11292-016-9273-z)
- [UK] Adderley & Bond 2008 (Northamptonshire): "the deprivation index of the crime-scene location had no bearing on the time spent investigating the scene" (minute totals not in abstract). — [NCJRS abstract](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/effects-deprivation-time-spent-examining-crime-scenes-and-recovery)
- [DK] Residential-burglary fingerprint submissions "produced a positive identification in 11% of cases in 2019", versus an all-crime mean hit rate rising "from 37% in 2017 to 48% in 2021". — [Nordic Journal of Criminology](https://www.scup.com/doi/10.18261/njc.27.1.4)
- [UK] Glass on clothing: Home Office labs measured "over 2000 glass fragments recovered from items of clothing"; a 1995 follow-up (500+ individuals, 4,000+ fragments) found "large amounts of non-matching glass on clothing were common, usually from several different sources". — [NCJRS, survey of glass fragments](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/survey-glass-fragments-recovered-clothing-persons-suspected); [NCJRS 159234](https://www.ncjrs.gov/App/Publications/abstract.aspx?ID=159234)
- Alarm/CCTV as evidence: "System logs help determine the exact timing of a criminal incident"; recorded data "can later be presented as admissible evidence in court" (academic overview, Iraq). In a Bermuda case "the CCTV cameras continued to operate independently of the disabled alarm systems". In a Victoria (Canada) case "the alarm had been tampered with from outside, and there was no video recovered ... as the store's surveillance system had gone down earlier in the day". — [Al-Mustaqbal University, Alarm and Protection Systems](https://uomus.edu.iq/DepartmentEn/Details/92276); [Bernews](https://bernews.com/2011/09/cctv-catches-paraquet-bac-burglar/); [Oak Bay News](https://www.oakbaynews.com/news/thieves-tamper-with-security-systems-at-two-computer-stores/)

### Inferences
- Processing order for the sim: (1) exterior and point of entry (glass, frame toolmarks, footwear outside); (2) route (footwear in glass/plaster dust, dropped items); (3) target (display-case glass for blood/prints/gloves, safe for toolmarks); (4) exit and vehicle position. This matches the Petraco scene narrative and the "routes from point of entry" guidance.
- Sequencing rule to teach: photograph the mark, then DNA-swab or gel-lift before (or instead of) heavy powdering on small items that can be seized; on fixed glass, powder to locate contact then swab the located area (HOSDB).
- Blood on display-case glass is the single highest-value burglary exhibit (see case studies) and should be recovered before any powder is applied.

### Gaps
- No published average time-on-scene for a volume burglary; Adderley & Bond's minute figures are in the full paper only.
- College of Policing APP text on volume-crime attendance criteria did not surface; only Home Office research and one force (Nottinghamshire) figure.
- No published force-level attendance criteria specific to non-dwelling/commercial burglary.
- The forensic strategy document content (what a CSI writes) could only be confirmed to exist as FSR Code 90.6; its required headings were not retrievable.
- No source on formal seizure/chain-of-custody procedure for retailer CCTV or alarm-panel logs, or on reconciling alarm vs CCTV clocks.

## Case studies: forensic evidence that identified jewellery raiders (UK, US, Canada, Gulf/Oman)

### Takeaway
Across jurisdictions the recurring solving evidence is blood left on smashed display glass (DNA), fingerprints on glass, DNA on dropped bags/handles, and CCTV plus vehicle/phone tracking; Gulf cases are solved mainly by CCTV, phone records and airport interception rather than reported forensics.

Rimantas Borisovas "cut his hand on glass from the cabinet, allowing police to recover a DNA sample at the scene. ### Inferences
- For a realistic Oman-set scenario, a wall-breach or blowtorch entry at night with the offenders identified by CCTV, phone data and airport interception is well grounded; blood on display glass is the forensic "win" that appears in every jurisdiction's solved cases.

## Retailer contribution: loss list, stock photos, hallmarks and laser inscriptions

### Takeaway
Recovery depends on item-level identifiers: GIA/IGI girdle inscriptions have directly traced stolen diamonds; serial numbers and stock photos are what pawn databases and police appeals use; UK and US registers (SaferGems/Watch Register, LeadsOnline) let dealers check provenance. Speed matters because gold is melted quickly.

### Cited Findings
- [US] Colorado case: detectives "worked with the insurance company and with the GIA, which supplied full descriptions of the stolen items and their value"; "the GIA told police it had recently graded diamonds matching those descriptions, with the same laser inscriptions"; police "traced the stones to a wholesale dealer and searched the business". — [IDEX Online memo 49266](https://www.idexonline.com/Memo?id=49266)
- IGI Laserscribe "places a laser inscription of a diamond identification number, personal message, trademark or logo on the girdle of a diamond"; "invisible to the naked eye, does not harm the stone and is permanent unless the diamond is repolished"; "comparable to a vehicle identification number". Graff London raid: "each Graff diamond was inscribed with an identification number not visible to the naked eye ... 18 of the recovered diamonds, some of which had been re-polished, turned up in New York four months after the hold-up". — [IDEX Online article 22759](https://idexonline.com/FullArticle?Id=22759)
- [UK] Merseyside Police appeal: owners asked to prove ownership with "receipts, serial numbers, pictures of you wearing the item or the crime reference number from when you reported the burglary". — [Merseyside Police via Mynewsdesk](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/merseyside-police/news/officers-hope-to-reunite-stolen-jewellery-with-rightful-owers-373280)
- [UK] "SaferGems Watch Register lets jewellers and pawnbrokers check the provenance of second-hand items with a single phone call or email." — [Jewellery Focus](https://www.jewelleryfocus.co.uk/?p=13212)
- [US] LeadsOnline "a national database that prevents people from pawning stolen items"; a Texas detective found a watch "worth nearly $30,000 through a pawn-shop database" but "the serial numbers were not recorded by the pawn shops" so he "searched on name and series of the watches". El Dorado County: property returned "thanks to their diligence of taking down serial number". — [LeadsOnline news](https://news.leadsonline.com/50000-of-stolen-jewelry-recovered); [Fox5 Vegas](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2024/09/27/las-vegas-police-pawn-shops-work-together-recover-stolen-items); [CBS Sacramento](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/el-dorado-county-stolen-property-recovered)
- [Cayman] RCIPS: "photographs of the items were taken and are now being issued to the public for viewing in an effort to link the items to their rightful owners". — [RCIPS](https://www.rcips.ky/recovered-property-for-public-viewing-24-june)
- Trade forum: "time is of the essence. Things get melted pretty fast"; visit pawn shops and gold buyers in person with photos, "phone calls don't work". — [Ganoksin Orchid forum](https://orchid.ganoksin.com/t/where-to-search-for-stolen-jewelry/38949)

### Inferences
- In the sim, the retailer's hand-over pack should contain: stock list with SKU/serials and certificate numbers, stock photographs, CCTV export with clock offset noted, alarm-panel event log, and a signed loss schedule for the insurer; the trainee should be prompted to circulate inscription/serial data to registers immediately.

### Gaps
- No source found on hallmarks (assay marks) aiding recovery – hallmarks identify maker/fineness/year, not the individual piece; only engraved initials (Bermuda appeal) and laser inscriptions were documented as identifying.
- No source on insurance-claim loss-list format requirements for jewellers.
