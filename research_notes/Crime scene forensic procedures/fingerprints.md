## 1. Types of fingermarks, what a latent mark consists of, and surface classes (porous / non-porous / semi-porous)

### Takeaway
Three categories are universal in the literature: latent (invisible, needs development), patent (visible, transferred contaminant such as blood, ink, dust, grease), plastic (3-D impression in a soft material). Latent residue is a mixture of eccrine sweat (>98% water with amino acids, urea, lactate, salts) and sebaceous lipids (squalene, wax esters, fatty acids); the surface class decides the method because porous surfaces absorb the water-soluble part (so amino-acid reagents are used) while on non-porous surfaces the residue sits on top (so powders, superglue, VMD are used) and is easily smeared.

### Cited Findings
- Patent prints are visible without enhancement because a visible substance (blood, ink, dirt, grease, paint) transferred from the finger; plastic prints are three-dimensional impressions in soft material such as putty, wax or soap; latent prints are not readily visible and need development — [NIJ Fingerprint Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf); [Speckin Forensics blog (SECONDARY)](https://4n6.com/blog/latent-patent-plastic-fingerprints/); [UCSB ScienceLine (SECONDARY)](http://scienceline.ucsb.edu/getkey.php?key=3401) (which notes a finger pressed into dried blood counts as plastic, while blood transferred to a surface is patent).
- "latent fingerprint residues consists of secretions of the eccrine (sweat), sebaceous and apocrine glands"; "Sweat contains water (>98%), minerals (0.5%) and organic compounds (0.5%)"; "Eccrine sweat consists of proteins, urea, amino acids, uric acid, lactic acid, sugars, creatinine, choline while sebaceous sweat consists of glycerides, fatty acids, wax esters, squalene and sterol esters" — [Cyanoacrylate fuming review, Egyptian J. Forensic Sci. (PMC) (PRIMARY, peer-reviewed)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5514188/); composition review also at [Springer, Egyptian J. Forensic Sci. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41935-021-00262-2). A study-aid site gives "roughly 99% water" instead — [Forensicspot (SECONDARY)](https://forensicspot.com/topics/ugc-net-forensic-science/fingerprints-development-lifting-and-afis-comparison).
- Porous surfaces: "The water and water-soluble components are absorbed quickly by porous surfaces such as paper", which is why amino-acid reagents (ninhydrin etc.) are used on them — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/the-fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development.pdf).
- Non-porous surfaces: "Nonporous surfaces do not absorb. These surfaces repel moisture and often appear polished. They include glass, metal, plastics, lacquered or painted wood, and rubber." The residue sits on the outer surface and is more vulnerable to damage; cyanoacrylate fuming, dye stains, powders and vacuum metal deposition are the typical methods — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/the-fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development.pdf).
- Semi-porous surfaces "both resist and absorb" residue; examples are glossy cardboard, glossy magazine covers, some finished wood and some cellophane; practitioners apply methods meant for both porous and non-porous surfaces — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/the-fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development.pdf).

### Inferences
- For a jewellery-store scene, nearly every evidential surface (glass showcase tops and doors, chrome/steel frames, polished stone or laminate counters, polymer card-readers, door push-plates) is non-porous; paper receipts, cardboard boxes, jewellery pouches and cash are porous/semi-porous and go to the lab rather than being powdered at scene.
- Because non-porous residue is "on top" and fragile, the simulation should penalise any contact with the mark before development (gloved finger, brush pressure, tape dragged across it).

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve a primary-source percentage split of eccrine vs sebaceous contribution in a typical "natural" mark; the literature usually says marks are a variable mix, which is why no single method is universal.

## 2. Visual search first: oblique lighting, torches, forensic light sources (wavelengths/filters), and why you examine before powdering

### Takeaway
Every SOP found puts a non-destructive visual/optical examination first: white-light torch held at a low (oblique/grazing) angle to the surface, then a forensic/alternate light source, mostly a blue band around 450 nm viewed through orange goggles (plus a cyan 505 nm band for some dye-treated marks), because this can reveal marks and contaminants without altering them, tells you what is already there (patent marks in dust/blood) and guides where and with what to powder.

### Cited Findings
- "flashlight at an oblique angle to examine evidence before processing"; oblique white light "can reveal dust prints on floors and indented writing on paper" — [Tennessee TBI SOP LP-0001 Alternate Light Source (PRIMARY)](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/latent-print/sop-manual/LP-0001.pdf) and [CTS Forensic Testing Program report](https://cts-forensics.com/reports/38190_Web.pdf) (as relayed by search extract).
- NIJ/FTCoE landscape study: "most investigators it interviewed used single-wavelength blue handhelds around 450 nm" — [NIJ Landscape Study of Alternate Light Sources, Jan 2018 (PRIMARY)](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/nij-landscape-study-of-alternate-light-sources.pdf).
- Blue light near 450 nm viewed through orange goggles is described as the standard approach; Sirchie's handbook describes a 455 nm setting with an orange filter so "the print separates from the background" — [Sirchie ALS handbook (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/0/5/05_ALS_vol_3_.pdf); [HORIBA FLS applications chart (VENDOR)](https://www.horiba.com/fileadmin/uploads/Scientific/Documents/Forensics/fls.pdf).
- 505 nm (cyan) is used for indanedione-zinc and DFO treated prints and rhodamine dye stains, paired with an orange filter near 550 nm; Sirchie's MMX100 chart lists 505 nm for cyanoacrylate stained with Rhodamine 6G and 455 or 505 nm for DFO-treated prints — [tech-led.com (VENDOR blog)](https://tech-led.com/505-nm-cyan-leds-for-forensic-examination-and-fluorescence-excitation/); [Sirchie MMX100 technical information (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/m/m/mmx100_ti09_05-496eng-rev5e.pdf).
- Orange barrier filters pass only the orange emission to the eye or camera, improving visualisation of fluorescing stains — [HORIBA FLS chart (VENDOR)](https://www.horiba.com/fileadmin/uploads/Scientific/Documents/Forensics/fls.pdf).
- Fluorescent powders "are used in conjunction with an alternative light source (i.e. lasers and low powered ultraviolet lamps)" — [Forensic Tools SA powders page (VENDOR)](https://www.forensictools.co.za/fingerprint-powders).
- Sequencing guide: examine under alternate light first, then chemical methods; "the chemical methods are irreversible and must follow a fixed order" — [Forensicspot (SECONDARY)](https://forensicspot.com/topics/fingerprint-sciences/chemical-methods-ninhydrin-dfo-indanedione-physical-developer-and-vmd); [CBDIAI Selection & Sequencing Guide (SECONDARY, practitioner association)](https://www.cbdiai.org/uploads/1/2/4/0/124025868/latent_fingerprint_chemical_reagent_techniques_guide.pdf).

### Inferences
- VR step order for every surface: (1) torch at grazing angle, sweep, look for sheen/dust/blood marks; (2) blue ALS ~450 nm + orange goggles, dark room, sweep; (3) photograph anything found; (4) only then choose a powder. A "patent" mark in dust or blood must never be powdered (powder destroys dust marks; blood marks go to blood-enhancement chemistry) — this follows from the method tables but no single sentence saying "never powder a dust mark" was retrieved.
- Searching the showcase glass with the torch held almost parallel to the glass surface is the correct hand pose; the examiner moves the light, not the head.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative sentence of the form "always examine before powdering" was retrieved; the practice is implied by the SOPs and sequencing guides above. The UK Fingermark Visualisation Manual's "visual examination" chapter would state it but could not be opened.
- Exact torch lux, distance or angle figures: none found.

## 3. Powder development on non-porous surfaces (powders, brushes, loading, motion, clearing, what destroys a mark, magnetic powder, contrast rules)

### Takeaway
UK HOSDB guidance (the only published trial-based rule set found): aluminium powder with a glass-fibre (Zephyr-style) brush on glass and most smooth surfaces; black granular powder where contrast demands; black/jet-black magnetic powder with a magnetic wand on textured surfaces and u-PVC, never aluminium or granular black there; and application technique matters as much as powder choice. Universal technique rules across SOPs/vendors: load very little powder (tip the brush in powder, tap/flick off excess; Sirchie says never dip into the jar), apply with light circular/twirling strokes with only the bristle tips touching, switch to strokes along the ridge flow as ridges appear, stop as soon as the mark is clear, and clear excess with a clean brush; too much powder or pressure fills/smears the ridges.

### Cited Findings
Powder and brush selection (UK trial-based guidance):
- "Aluminium powder should, where possible, be applied with a glass fibre brush." — [HOSDB Fingerprint Powders Guidelines 09/07 (PRIMARY)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)
- "Zephyr style squirrel brushes or tapered polyester brushes should be used on surfaces where glass fibre brushes may tangle and clog up eg, contaminated surfaces" — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)
- "Glass should be powdered with aluminium powder unless contamination prohibits its use." — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)
- "Where appropriate, a black or jet black magnetic powder should be used on textured surfaces, NOT aluminium or black granular powder." and "Black or jet black magnetic powders should be used on u-PVC." — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf); repeated in [HOSDB Powders Process Study 3 (PRIMARY)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5ef5f7aed3bf7f7691f44b7d/Fingerprint_and_Footwear_Forensics_Newsletter_-_Special_Edition_-_Powders_Process_Study_3_67-06.pdf), which "gives advice for the powdering of surfaces which generally yield fewer marks, such as textured surfaces and u-PVC."
- On technique vs powder: "scene examiners must receive appropriate training and maintain good application technique when applying powders" and "This is likely to be as important as powder selections for some smooth surfaces." — [HOSDB Guidelines 09/07 (PRIMARY)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb736273806a000cec7774/Fingerprint_Powders_Guidelines__March_2007_09-07.pdf)
- HOSDB survey (Study 2): glass fibre brushes were the most common choice for applying aluminium powder; squirrel Zephyr brushes were used about 15% of the time; the study's objective "was to ascertain if there is a significant difference in the performance of fingerprint powders on smooth surfaces encountered at scenes of crime." — [HOSDB Powders Process Study 2 (PRIMARY)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5ef5f7af86650c129fbc1daa/Fingerprint_Update__Special_Edition_-_The_Powders_Process_Study_2_-_Evaluation_of_Fingerprint_Powders_on_Smooth_Surfaces_08-06.pdf)
- Brush materials: applicators "can be made from camel hair, squirrel hair, goat hair, horse hair, feathers, synthetic or natural fibers, carbon filaments, or fiberglass"; soft brushes "reduce the risk of damaging the fragile print" — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 11 (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225331.pdf)
- Vendor/training consensus: camel- or squirrel-hair brushes suit regular powders on flat surfaces, fibreglass brushes suit fluorescent and non-magnetic powders, magnetic wands are needed for magnetic powders — [forensicreader.com brush guide (SECONDARY)](https://forensicreader.com/fingerprint-brushes/); [fingerprintexpert.in (SECONDARY)](https://www.fingerprintexpert.in/blog-details/types-of-fingerprint-brushes)
- Magnetic powders "should be used on surfaces that are not perfectly smooth" — [forensicreader.com powders (SECONDARY)](https://forensicreader.com/types-of-fingerprint-powders/); magnetic powders "are usually less effective on ferromagnetic substrates such as steel or nickel and are therefore not recommended on those substrates" — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf)
- Fluorescent powders suit "rough or contaminated surfaces or multi coloured surfaces where it may not be possible to choose a conventional powder which separates the latent print from its background" — [Forensic Tools SA (VENDOR)](https://www.forensictools.co.za/fingerprint-powders); "used to recover fingerprints on multi-colored surfaces" — [forensicfield.blog (SECONDARY)](https://forensicfield.blog/fingerprint-dusting/)
- Contrast: "Choose a powder that contrasts with the background."; Sirchie's silver/black dual powder "develops black on light-colored surfaces and silver on dark-colored surfaces"; "Silk Black" is "the standard oxide powder" for light surfaces — [Sirchie (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/forensics/latent-print-development/regular-powders-brushes/dual-purpose-fingerprint-powder/silver-black-fingerprint-powder.html); [Sirchie Latent Print Development Manual (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/l/a/latent_print_development_manual_4.pdf)

Loading the brush:
- "the filament tips are lightly dipped into a sterile, wide-mouth container holding a small amount of powder" — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf)
- "Never dip the brush directly into the powder jar. Measure out a small quantity on paper. Less is best." — [Sirchie Latent Print Development Manual (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/l/a/latent_print_development_manual_4.pdf) (conflicts in detail, not in spirit, with the Sourcebook's "dip the tips" wording)
- "dip the brush in powder and tap off the excess before application" — [forensiccoe.org training guide (SECONDARY)](https://forensiccoe.org/private/6509e31cb7951); SOP: remove excess powder before applying it to the object and use very little on the brush — [Santa Barbara Sheriff SOP-CSI-007 (PRIMARY, agency SOP)](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf)
- Sirchie: place a test print on the surface in an area not likely to have been touched by the suspect (to check powder/surface behaviour before working the real area) — [Sirchie manual (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/l/a/latent_print_development_manual_4.pdf)

The motion:
- "lightly twirling the brush over the object until the print develops, adding more powder only as needed" — [Santa Barbara Sheriff SOP (PRIMARY)](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf)
- "apply powder with light circular motions, then brush along the direction of ridges as they appear"; "clear excess powder from between ridges" — [DC Dept of Forensic Sciences procedure (PRIMARY)](https://dfs.qualtraxcloud.com/ShowDocument.aspx?ID=10172)
- "A swinging motion or side-to-side motion will both work." — [Sirchie manual (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item/l/a/latent_print_development_manual_4.pdf)
- "brush gently and follow the ridge direction to avoid smearing" — [forensicfield.blog (SECONDARY)](https://forensicfield.blog/fingerprint-dusting/)
- Over-powdering "is a common error because excess powder obscures ridge detail"; use "a soft brush in gentle, circular motions" — [Cerafor police guide (SECONDARY)](https://cerafor.com/blog/what-police-officers-need-to-know-about-latent-prints/); "too much powder fills in ridge detail" and "limit brush strokes" — [Arrowhead Forensics How-To (VENDOR)](https://arrowheadforensics.com/blog/how-to-guide-lift-fingerprints/); Spraggs: "use less powder than seems necessary" — [Police Magazine (SECONDARY, practitioner)](https://www.policemag.com/patrol/article/15349075/how-to-lift-fingerprints)
- Magnetic wand: "The magnetized applicator (magna brush) is dipped into the powder, picking up a ball of the iron and particle mixture, essentially forming its own brush. The ball is then passed back and forth over the substrate"; the powder ball "is much softer than conventional filament brushes and typically causes less damage to fragile latent prints" — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf); FBI: place the wand with its magnet engaged into the magnetic powder, apply in a circular motion "while making sure only the powder, not the wand, touches the surface" — [FBI Processing Guide (PRIMARY)](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/jan2001/lpu.pdf); "The magnetic rod is moveable and can be retracted within the case. When the rod is not retracted, the head of the applicator is magnetized." (retracting the rod drops the powder back into the jar) — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 11 (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225331.pdf)

Equipment numbers:
- Sirchie kit-size fibreglass brush: filament cluster "2 1/4"L (5.7cm)", handle "1/4" dia. x 2 3/4"L (0.635 cm x 7 cm)" — [Sirchie (VENDOR)](https://www.sirchie.com/catalog/product/view/id/2/?___store=international_english)
- Wet surfaces: small particle reagent, not powder, "is used to develop prints on wet, non-porous surfaces including glass, plastic, metals and adhesive sides of tape" — [forensicreader.com (SECONDARY)](https://forensicreader.com/types-of-fingerprint-powders/); SPR "is the only method that works on wet non-porous surfaces" — [Fingerprint Development Techniques: A Review (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355065615_Fingerprint_Development_Techniques_A_Review)

### Inferences
- A concrete VR state machine consistent with the sources: select powder by surface (glass/chrome/polished counter: aluminium + glass-fibre brush; dark glossy surface where aluminium contrast is poor: black granular; textured plastic, u-PVC, leather-grain: black magnetic + wand; multicoloured/patterned: fluorescent + ALS) -> tip bristles into a small heap of powder decanted onto paper -> tap handle to shed excess -> hold brush vertically so only tips touch -> light twirl/circular strokes over the suspect area -> on first sign of ridges switch to short strokes following ridge flow -> stop when ridges are crisp -> clear loose powder with a clean, empty brush or gentle air (not breath) -> photograph. Penalties: pressure above a threshold, more than N passes over a developed mark, powder quantity above a threshold, powder on a visibly wet surface, aluminium/granular on textured plastic.
- The HOSDB "technique matters as much as powder" statement supports scoring the motion itself, not just the powder choice, in training.

### Gaps
- No source gave a numeric pressure, stroke count or rotation speed; "light", "gentle", "stop when ridges appear" is as precise as the literature gets.
- The detailed performance tables from the HOSDB powders studies (percent marks found per powder/brush) were not retrievable.
- The "don't blow on the surface / breath moisture" rule and "one brush per powder colour to avoid contamination" rule were not found in any retrieved source (the HOSDB leaflet does not say it); the search explicitly noted this absence.
- No published figure comparing success of aluminium vs black on glass was retrieved beyond the HOSDB recommendation.

## 4. Photograph before lifting (scale, label, lighting, 1:1/macro, why first)

### Takeaway
Lifting is irreversible and can fail, so the photograph of the developed mark in situ is the primary record; several agency manuals require photographing all developed marks before lifting (Illinois SP) or at least when the lift may be difficult (Washington SP, DC DFS); the NIJ Sourcebook notes agencies differ. A scale and identifier go in frame; training sites specify examination-quality 1:1 capture.

### Cited Findings
- "all developed impressions should be photographed prior to lifting" — [Illinois State Police Latent Prints manual, rev. 2 Jun 2025 (PRIMARY)](https://isp.illinois.gov/StaticFiles/docs/ForensicServices/ForensicScienceCommandManuals/Latent%20Prints.pdf)
- "If the lift process may pose a challenge, the latent prints should be photographed prior to lifting." — [Washington State Patrol Latent Print Technical Manual (PRIMARY)](https://wsp.wa.gov/forensics/docs/crimelab/manuals/technical/latent/Latent%20Print%20Technical%20Manual%20R.%2022.pdf)
- Photograph prints before lifting "when surface texture may prevent a proper lift" — [DC DFS (PRIMARY)](https://dfs.qualtraxcloud.com/ShowDocument.aspx?ID=10172)
- "Some agencies require that examination-quality photographs be taken of all latent prints developed with powders prior to lifting, whereas others do not." — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 10 Documentation (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225330.pdf)
- Photograph the print with a scale before lifting — [Santa Barbara Sheriff SOP (PRIMARY)](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-007-12-Detecting-Latent-Prints.pdf); [forensicfield.blog (SECONDARY)](https://forensicfield.blog/fingerprint-dusting/)
- Photograph at 1:1 scale; the lift is irreversible and the photo is the primary record — [Forensicspot (SECONDARY)](https://forensicspot.com/topics/fingerprint-sciences/powder-methods-and-the-latent-print-lifting-workflow)
- "a visible print should be photographed before any lift is attempted" — [TacticalGear Officer's Guide (SECONDARY)](https://tacticalgear.com/experts/officers-guide-to-fingerprints); prints "may be photographed before collection, depending on agency policy" — [Spraggs/Police Mag via archive (SECONDARY)](https://sites.google.com/site/alejandrapizarrolegal/20-levantamiento-de-huellas-dactilares/to-lift-fingerprints)
- SWGFAST: "the friction ridge impression alone is not sufficient documentation"; the impression or a legible copy needs annotations or accompanying notes — [SWGFAST Standard for Documentation of ACE-V, v2.0 (PRIMARY)](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2016/10/26/swgfast_standard-documentation-ace-v_2.0_121124.pdf)

### Inferences
- VR step: after development, place a scale (e.g. ABFO-type L-scale or a ruler on the lift label) beside, not over, the mark; write the mark number on the scale/label; camera back parallel to the surface (film plane parallel), fill frame with mark + scale, use oblique or diffuse light so powder contrasts; for aluminium on glass photograph against a dark backing or with the glass dark behind. (The scale type and parallel-camera rule are standard forensic photography practice but were not directly retrieved here; see Gaps.)

### Gaps
- No retrieved source specified the scale type (ABFO No. 2), resolution (e.g. 1000 ppi) or lighting geometry for fingermark photography; the NIJ ch. 10 PDF would contain this but could not be opened.

## 5. Lifting: tapes, hinge/gel/rubber lifters, exact technique, backing cards, labelling, multiple marks, failed lifts

### Takeaway
Clear lifting tape (commonly 1.5", 2" and 3" wide; hinge lifters 1.5x2" and 2x4"; gel lifters 13x18 cm) is anchored about an inch beyond the mark, pressed down in one smooth motion (a card edge is a common tool) to avoid bubbles, peeled in one continuous motion and laid on a contrasting backing card (black powder on white card, aluminium/silver/white or fluorescent powder on black card; transparent for photography); the card back carries case/lift number, date, location, examiner and a sketch with an orientation arrow. Gel lifters are for textured surfaces; hinge lifters avoid bubbles on flat ones.

### Inferences
- A faithful VR lift: cut/peel ~10 cm of 2" tape; anchor the leading edge ~2.5 cm beyond one side of the mark; lay the tape down progressively while pressing with a card edge or thumb in one direction; rub out any bubble over the ridges; lift the trailing edge and peel back in one continuous motion while keeping tension; lay sticky-side-down onto the backing card starting at one edge and smoothing across; trim; turn over and complete the label fields (case no., exhibit/lift no., location text, sketch with arrow, date/time, name) before moving on. Place a white card for black powder and a black card for aluminium/white/fluorescent.
- Failed lift (bubble through ridges, tape folded on itself, powder lifted off smeared): the mark on the surface is usually consumed, which is exactly why the photograph came first; a second lift of the same mark, if attempted, will be fainter. (Consequence inferred from the "irreversible" statements.)
- Aluminium powder lifted onto transparent tape and a black card produces a white-on-black image; UK practice commonly lifts aluminium on clear tape onto clear acetate/COC sheets for photography, but no retrieved source states this; see Gaps.

## 6. Chemical and other methods for porous and difficult surfaces, and sequence of techniques

### Takeaway
Non-porous items that cannot be powdered well at scene go to a lab for cyanoacrylate (superglue) fuming at ~70–80% RH (minutes, with a test print to avoid over-fuming), optionally dye-stained and viewed under ALS, with vacuum metal deposition (gold then zinc) as a very sensitive follow-up; wet non-porous items get small particle reagent; porous paper gets amino-acid reagents in the fixed, irreversible order DFO or 1,2-indanedione(-zinc) (fluorescent, viewed at ~505 nm) then ninhydrin (purple), then physical developer; iodine fuming is an old, fading, non-destructive first step. Non-destructive optical examination always precedes any of these.

### Cited Findings
- Cyanoacrylate "is the most suitable chemical method to develop latent fingermarks on dry and wet nonporous surfaces"; "reacts with traces of amino acids, fatty acids and proteins in the latent fingerprint and moisture in the air" — [CA fuming review, PMC (PRIMARY, peer-reviewed)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5514188/)
- FBI lab recommends a humidified chamber at 70–80% RH; FBI "microburst" method: "fuming times ranging from 30 seconds to 4 minutes are sufficient in most instances" — [FBI Processing Guide (PRIMARY)](https://www.fbi.gov/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/jan2001/lpu.pdf); "Studies have also indicated the importance of an optimum relative humidity of 80% during the fuming" — [Reading Univ. paper on CA on fabric (PRIMARY, peer-reviewed)](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/66613/1/Emilypaperpdf.pdf); TBI SOP: "fuming times from 3-5 minutes are typically sufficient" — [TBI LP-0011 (PRIMARY)](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/latent-print/sop-manual/LP-0011.pdf); one vacuum-chamber study found 40–50% RH optimal (outlier, different apparatus) — [ResearchGate vacuum CA study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264108116_A_study_on_the_optimal_conditions_for_latent_fingerprint_development_using_cyanoacrylate_fuming_method_in_vacuum_chamber)
- Over-fuming: "fuming times of two minutes or less produce optimal print clarity, whereas longer fuming times produce excessive deposition of ester on the background" — [Processes in CA development (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224945777_Processes_Involved_in_the_Development_of_Latent_Fingerprints_Using_the_Cyanoacrylate_Fuming_Method); lab SOP uses a control: "the test card must be visible so that latent print development may be monitored" — [Ada County Sheriff Latent Analytical Method (PRIMARY)](https://adacounty.id.gov/sheriff/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2018/10/Latent-Analytical-Method-V4.pdf)
- Portable scene fuming tent (LumiFume) develops marks in minutes; measured humidity "varied between 80–97% depending on where the humidity sensor was situated within the tent" — [Forensic Science International 2019 (PRIMARY, peer-reviewed)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0379073819303780)
- Ninhydrin (Sourcebook): room temperature, dark, ~50–80% humidity over 1–2 days — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf); UK CAST/ANZPAA accelerated protocol 65% RH at 80 °C for 3–5 minutes — [Forensicspot (SECONDARY)](https://forensicspot.com/topics/fingerprint-sciences/chemical-methods-ninhydrin-dfo-indanedione-physical-developer-and-vmd); ninhydrin sublimation (fuming) alternative — [manupatra ninhydrin paper](http://docs.manupatra.in/newsline/articles/Upload/7D9C2CCE-05B9-4DE9-86B9-E8685F66870D.pdf)
- DFO: 100 °C oven for 10–20 minutes; needs a dry, low-humidity environment; "DFO is reported to be a more sensitive fingerprint development reagent than ninhydrin"; "if ninhydrin is applied after DFO treatment, additional development occurs, producing Ruhemann's purple" — [NIJ Sourcebook ch. 7 (PRIMARY)](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/the-fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development.pdf); "DFO must be used before ninhydrin or silver nitrate" — [Marshall Univ. validation paper (Wilberg 2016)](https://www.marshall.edu/forensics/files/Wilberg_Amanda_Research-Paper8_4_16.pdf)
- 1,2-Indanedione "has become the state-of-the-art method for detecting fingermarks on porous substrates"; "could be used in sequence with DFO but not with ninhydrin"; "no new prints could be observed when ninhydrin was applied after indanedione" — [Griffith Univ. review of solvent-free paper methods (PRIMARY)](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/6b15bd6d-c5da-48b9-90db-3a54a87c190c/content); [Optimisation of 1,2-indanedione, FSI 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0379073806003720); humidity below 60% RH gives poorer results, above 70% better; brief 160 °C heat-press treatment; zinc chloride stabilises the fluorescent product — [BVDA IND page (VENDOR)](https://www.bvda.com/en/ind); [Forensicspot (SECONDARY)](https://forensicspot.com/topics/fingerprint-sciences/chemical-methods-ninhydrin-dfo-indanedione-physical-developer-and-vmd)
- Fixed porous sequence: fluorescent amino-acid reagents first, then ninhydrin, then physical developer last — [Forensicspot (SECONDARY)](https://forensicspot.com/topics/fingerprint-sciences/chemical-methods-ninhydrin-dfo-indanedione-physical-developer-and-vmd); CA before iodine; some metals that corrode should not be iodine-fumed — [CBDIAI Sequencing Guide (SECONDARY)](https://www.cbdiai.org/uploads/1/2/4/0/124025868/latent_fingerprint_chemical_reagent_techniques_guide.pdf)
- Small particle reagent: "a suspension of molybdenum disulphide in detergent-water"; "the only method that works on wet non-porous surfaces"; works on items "immersed in water for an extended period"; "zinc carbonate or titanium dioxide particles are used for developing latent fingermarks on dark surfaces" — [SPR review, Egyptian J. Forensic Sci. 2016 (PRIMARY)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2090536X1630065X); [Fingerprint Development Techniques: A Review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355065615_Fingerprint_Development_Techniques_A_Review)
- UK CAST on polymer banknotes: iron-oxide and carbon-based powder suspensions "are two of the most effective processes if only visual examination is used"; TiO2 suspension effective "but fingermarks are very difficult to see" — [Home Office CAST polymer currency note, Sept 2017 (PRIMARY)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6895eba0ad0cbc0e27643197/fingermark-visualisation-polymer-currency-september-2017.pdf)
- Vacuum metal deposition: "a thin film of gold followed by zinc is evaporated onto the substrate in vacuum"; "extremely sensitive for old or weak prints on smooth non-porous surfaces"; normal development (zinc between ridges) and reverse development (zinc on ridges) both possible — [Fingerprint Development Techniques: A Review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355065615_Fingerprint_Development_Techniques_A_Review); [VMD on polyethylene, excess gold](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11625260_Vacuum_metal_deposition_Developing_latent_fingerprints_on_polyethylene_substrates_after_the_deposition_of_excess_gold)
- Iodine fuming "has been used for more than a century"; mechanism now thought to be physical adsorption; "the color tends to disappear" — [Fingerprint Development Techniques: A Review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355065615_Fingerprint_Development_Techniques_A_Review)
- Portable items: scene vs lab is a practical split; the UK manual is "written for UK police operational reference" with research in a companion Source Book — [gov.uk FVM 2nd ed. (PRIMARY)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fingermark-visualisation-manual-second-edition)

### Inferences
- For the VR scene: powder the fixed glass/metal/counter surfaces in place; bag small movable non-porous items (dropped tools, bottles, torch, broken-glass fragments with smooth faces) un-powdered for lab superglue/VMD, since lab processes are more sensitive than scene powdering; bag paper (receipts, notes, gift boxes) in paper/plastic without powdering for DFO/indanedione/ninhydrin; a wet item (rain-soaked glass shard) is a candidate for SPR, not powder.
- "Non-destructive first" ordering rule to encode: visual -> ALS -> (scene) powder or (lab) CA -> dye stain -> VMD; porous: visual -> ALS -> IND-Zn or DFO -> ninhydrin -> PD; never reverse.

- Exact VMD chamber parameters, iodine "starch fixing" step, and powder-suspension recipe figures were not retrieved.
- The Fingermark Visualisation Manual's own process-selection charts could not be opened; the above sequence is reconstructed from US sourcebook, peer-reviewed reviews and training guides.

## 7. Elimination prints, mark quality grading, what "identification" requires (ACE-V, minutiae), limits and controversies

### Takeaway
Elimination prints are inked/scanned prints from people with legitimate access (victim, staff, householders, first responders) so their marks can be excluded. The UK CAST grading scale (0–4) treats grades 3–4 (over one-third clear ridge detail) as potentially identifiable. Identification follows ACE-V with no fixed minimum point count in the US and many jurisdictions (some historically used 12 or 16), concluding identification/exclusion/inconclusive; a VR trainer should present scene recovery as producing a mark "suitable for comparison", not an instant ID. The best black-box study found 0.1% false positives and ~7.5% false negatives, PCAST (2016) framed upper bounds of 1 in 306 and 1 in 18, the 2009 NRC report found no scientific validation of ACE-V as practised, and the 2004 Mayfield case shows that a wrong ID can be "verified" by several experts.

### Cited Findings
Elimination prints:
- "the prints of all persons whose presence at the place under inspection has been for legitimate purposes" are set aside: "Inked prints taken for this purpose are referred to as elimination prints." — [FBI, The Science of Fingerprints, via crime-scene-investigator.net (PRIMARY text)](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/fbiscienceoffingerprints.html)
- At a residence, prints must be taken from all household members as a point of comparison; usual sources are victims, witnesses, other residents, employees — [Chevy Chase Village PD policy 5-26.3 (PRIMARY, agency policy)](https://www.chevychasevillagemd.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1216/5-263-Latent-Fingerprint--Evidence-and-Elimination-Prints-PDF); [crime-scene-investigator.net Collection & Preservation part III](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/evidenc3.html)

Grading:
- CAST scale: "0 No evidence of a fingermark; 1 Some evidence of a fingermark; 2 Less than 1/3 clear ridge detail; 3 Between 1/3 and 2/3 clear ridge detail; 4 Over 2/3 clear ridge detail"; grades 3 and 4 "are considered of value, i.e. the fingerprint may contain sufficient detail for identification"; the scheme "is intended to be adaptable depending on the focus of the associated study" — [Dstl Fingermark Visualisation Newsletter, July 2020 (PRIMARY)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f1ab58ee90e07456caf0c63/2020_Jul_Dstl_Fingermark_Visualisation_Newsletter__5_v1.0_O.pdf); "only fingermarks with a grade of 3 or 4 were considered to be suitable for comparison purposes" — [ChemRxiv 2022 indanedione-zinc comparison](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/pdf/10.26434/chemrxiv-2022-g6x80); IFRG assessment guidelines — [JFI 2014 (PRIMARY)](https://www.theiai.org/docs/JFI-2014-02-174.pdf); examiner/researcher survey on quality — [FSI 361 (2024) 112100](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/PDF/fingermark-quality-a-survey-of-examiners-and-researchers.pdf)

ACE-V and identification:
- ACE-V = Analysis, Comparison, Evaluation, Verification; Analysis examines the latent in isolation using Level 1, 2 and 3 detail before seeing the reference print; Evaluation ends in "identification, exclusion, and inconclusive"; Verification is an independent repeat by a second examiner — [Cal State ACE-V training manual (SECONDARY, academic)](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/d791sg30j); [Nikoui & Associates on Analysis phase (SECONDARY)](https://www.nikouiandassociates.com/the-analysis-phase-of-the-ace-v-method-determining-the-quality-and-suitability-of-fingerprint-impressions/); SWGFAST ACE-V documentation standard — [NIST-hosted SWGFAST Doc #8 (PRIMARY)](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2016/10/26/swgfast_standard-documentation-ace-v_2.0_121124.pdf)
- "some countries historically required 12 or 16 corresponding points, but the U.S. and many other jurisdictions have moved away from fixed numerical thresholds"; examiners "rely on a holistic assessment considering the quantity, quality, and rarity of the features" — [LegalClarity (SECONDARY)](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-fingerprint-analysis-and-how-does-it-work/); a less authoritative source says some agencies may still require 8–16 minutiae — [studyguides.com (SECONDARY)](https://studyguides.com/study-methods/overview/cmotwkhczjhsn01nee1mudipa)

Error rates and controversy:
- Ulery, Hicklin, Buscaglia & Roberts, PNAS 108(19):7733–7738 (2011), doi 10.1073/pnas.1018707108: 169 examiners, ~100 latent–exemplar pairs each from a pool of 744; six false positives in 4,083 non-mated comparisons = 0.1% false-positive rate; ~7.5% false-negative rate; independent examination by different participants detected all the false positives — [PNAS (PRIMARY)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018707108); [NIJ "History and Legacy of the Latent Fingerprint Black Box Study"](https://nij.ojp.gov/topics/articles/history-and-legacy-latent-fingerprint-black-box-study); [phys.org summary](https://phys.org/news/2011-04-false-positives-rare-fingerprint.html)
- Miami-Dade 2014 study: false-positive rate ~3.0% (4.2% excluding inconclusives) — [Ausdemore, Hendricks & Neumann review, arXiv 1809.03910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.03910); [NIJ abstract](https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/review-several-false-positive-error-rate-estimates-latent-fingerprint)
- PCAST 2016: latent print analysis "foundationally valid" but "The false-positive rate could be as high as 1 error in 306 cases based on the FBI study and 1 error in 18 cases based on a study by another crime laboratory." — [PCAST report (PRIMARY)](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/microsites/ostp/PCAST/pcast_forensic_science_report_final.pdf); OSAC FRS response: PCAST "based its quoted estimates on only a subset of the examination methodology" and the rates "did not account for any verification step"; false positives "were rarely reproduced by a second examiner" — [NIST-hosted OSAC document](https://www.nist.gov/document/113uleryfullibpcpdf); critique calling the 1-in-18 framing misleading — [Forensic Science, Statistics & the Law blog](http://for-sci-law.blogspot.com/2016/12/on-ridiculous-estimate-of-error-rate.html); legal context — [UCLA Law Review](https://www.uclalawreview.org/reliable-application-of-fingerprint-evidence/)
- NRC 2009: "We have reviewed available scientific evidence of the validity of the ACE-V method and found none." (pp. 142–143, as quoted) — [Forensic Resources (SECONDARY quoting NRC)](https://forensicresources.org/forensic-disciplines/fingerprints/)
- Mayfield (Madrid bombing, 11 March 2004): FBI misidentified Brandon Mayfield; three FBI experts confirmed, a court-appointed fourth expert also confirmed; Spanish authorities attributed the print to Ouhnane Daoud; the DOJ OIG concluded the examiners "did not contravene any procedures or requirements set forth in the FBI Laboratory or SWGFAST standards" — [DOJ OIG report (PRIMARY)](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2018-02/s1105.pdf); [Open University (SECONDARY)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/health-sports-psychology/health/forensic-science-and-fingerprints/content-section-2.3); [FJC Reference Guide on Forensic Identification Expertise (PRIMARY)](https://www.fjc.gov/sites/default/files/2012/SciMan3D05.pdf). The case "drove adoption of blind verification and sequential unmasking protocols" — [Forensicspot (SECONDARY)](https://forensicspot.com/topics/fingerprint-sciences/ace-v-methodology-and-the-brandon-mayfield-madrid-bombing-error)

### Inferences
- The VR trainee's job ends at a well-documented, well-lifted mark plus elimination prints; the sim should show the lift going to a fingerprint bureau/AFIS and return "identified / excluded / inconclusive / insufficient", with explicit text that a scene mark is graded for suitability (CAST 3–4 ~ usable) and that identification is an examiner judgement with a measured, non-zero error rate.
- For scoring, grade the simulated developed mark on the CAST 0–4 scale (fraction of ridge area clearly rendered) — this maps directly to a procedural rendering of ridge coverage.

### Gaps
- No single current UK Forensic Science Regulator or IAI standard text on verification/blind verification was retrieved.
- Minimum point standards still used by specific countries today were not confirmed from primary sources.

## 8. Numbers: bristle length, tape widths, time windows, survival of latent marks on glass, success rates

### Takeaway
Concrete figures found: fibreglass brush filament cluster 2.25" (5.7 cm); tape 1.5/2/3" wide; hinge lifters 1.5x2" and 2x4"; gel lifters 13x18 cm; backing cards 3x5"; CA fuming 30 s–5 min at 70–80% RH; DFO 10–20 min at 100 °C; ninhydrin 3–5 min at 80 °C/65% RH (accelerated) or 1–2 days at room temperature; sebaceous marks on glass still identifiable at 6 months, and contaminated marks surviving >2.5 years outdoors; glass preserves marks better than plastic. No published scene-level "percent of surfaces yielding marks" by surface type was retrieved.

### Inferences
- A night-time jewellery-store burglary processed the next morning is well within the window where marks on glass and chrome remain developable; the sim can legitimately treat age (hours) as not limiting, while weather exposure (rain on the broken shop-front glass) switches that surface to SPR/lab.
- Training text should avoid claiming a mark "looks fresh" or "looks old".

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or agency figure for "percentage of burglary scenes / glass surfaces yielding identifiable marks" was retrieved; the HOSDB powders studies hold comparative percentages but the PDFs could not be opened.
- No figure for how long a developed-but-unlifted powder mark can be left before lifting.
