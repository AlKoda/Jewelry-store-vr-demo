## 1. Footwear marks: finding, documenting, lifting, casting, comparing

### Takeaway
Standard order is: locate (oblique light), photograph (tripod, sensor parallel to the mark, scale in the same plane, oblique flash from several angles), then recover (electrostatic lift first for dry dust, then gelatin lift; dental stone cast for 3D impressions), then compare against test impressions in the lab. Dental stone is mixed roughly 2 lb powder : 8-12 oz water (sources disagree), poured outside the impression and allowed to flow in, left at least 25-30 min before lifting, marked on the back, and air-dried about 48 h before cleaning. Class characteristics (size, design, mould) can only say a shoe "could have" made the mark; randomly acquired characteristics (cuts, nicks, debris) in the same location and orientation are needed for identification.

### Cited Findings

**Locating dry (dust) and wet-origin marks**
- "A dry impression can lack contrast with the surface, so an oblique light source is preferred. Impressions made by a dry sole with dust or residues may be detected on almost all surfaces except for dirty floors. Dust and powder prints can sometimes be detected with the naked eye, but they can be detected much more easily with oblique light." — [Footwear Impression as Forensic Evidence (thesis, DiVA)](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:18227/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- Oblique lighting "works by having light directed at a low angle across the surface of interest reflect off the residues and make the prints easier to see." — [EBSCO Research Starters: Oblique lighting analysis](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/science/oblique-lighting-analysis/)
- Wet-origin prints "can be hard to detect because they are often dry when found. Still, a disturbance in the surface can be detected by applying oblique light or fingerprint powder." — [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:18227/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- Training flashcards define wet-origin prints as "wet matrix on a wet or dry substrate" and advise that when origin is uncertain "the impression should be treated as dry origin because any enhancement may destroy the impression." (secondary study aid) — [Brainscape footwear flashcards](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/footwear-and-tiretrack-impressions-18674568/packs/22627506)
- BVDA (gelatin lifter manufacturer): "use a torch or floodlight at a low angle to find faint prints, or cover the suspected area with lifters if prints aren't visible under that light." — [BVDA Gellifters for shoeprints](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters-shoeprints)
- Electrostatic lifting "is ideally suited for quickly examining a large area for shoe marks" — [BVDA electrostatic lifting page](https://www.bvda.com/en/electrostatic_lifting_of_shoeprints)

**Photography (examination-quality)**
- SWGDE digital imaging guideline: "Mount camera on a tripod with the focal plane parallel to the impression. Manually focus on the bottom of the impression and close aperture to optimize depth of field." Scale "should sit on the same plane as the impression, even if the examiner must dig beside it." — [SWGDE 17-P-003 Guidelines for the Digital Imaging of Footwear and Tire Impressions](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/17-p-003-swgde-guidelines-for-the-digital-imaging-of-footwear-and-tire-impressions/)
- Hartnell College text: matching the scale plane "keeps the scale and impression both in focus and corrects parallax distortion." — [LibreTexts 12.04 Documenting and preserving footwear impressions](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Hartnell_College/The_Introduction_to_the_Forensic_Sciences/12%3A_Impression_Evidence/12.04%3A_Documenting_and_preserving_footwear_impressions)
- Aperture: Wisconsin DOJ brochure points to roughly f/11-f/16 for maximum depth of field; current SWGDE and Wisconsin guidance call for the highest-quality digital setting such as RAW. — [Wisconsin DOJ Footwear Evidence Brochure](https://www.wisdoj.gov/Forensic%20Science/Footwear%20Evidence%20Brochure.pdf); [SWGDE](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/17-p-003-swgde-guidelines-for-the-digital-imaging-of-footwear-and-tire-impressions/)
- Flash angles: Virginia DFS protocol suggests flash at roughly 10°, 30° and 45°, "the exact flash angle is determined by depth of impression." — [Virginia DFS Evidence Handling: Impressions (2021)](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf)
- At least three flash positions with about 100° of separation around the impression. — [Crime and Clues, "Footwear, the Missed Evidence"](https://crimeandclues.com/2013/01/31/footwear-the-missed-evidence/4/)
- Flash distance from the impression: 3-4 ft (Wisconsin DOJ), 3-5 ft (Hartnell/LibreTexts), 4-5 ft (NIST/SWGTREAD guidance). — [Wisconsin DOJ](https://www.wisdoj.gov/Forensic%20Science/Footwear%20Evidence%20Brochure.pdf); [LibreTexts](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Hartnell_College/The_Introduction_to_the_Forensic_Sciences/12%3A_Impression_Evidence/12.04%3A_Documenting_and_preserving_footwear_impressions); [SWGTREAD photography guide (crime-scene-investigator.net mirror)](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-the-forensic-documentation-and-photography-of-footwear-and-tire-impressions-at-the-crime-scene.html)
- SWGDE general lighting guidance: light sources at about 45° to the surface to limit glare; "adjusting the angle changes contrast in impression evidence." — [SWGDE 21-P-002 Lighting Techniques in Forensic Photography](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/21-p-002-swgde-lighting-techniques-in-forensic-photography/)
- "oblique light provides better details for comparison so some photographs should be taken in oblique light"; "an external flash placed at an oblique angle is used to light the print properly." — [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:18227/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- Older Law Enforcement Training Network material says digital cameras should not be used and 35 mm film is the minimum; this is superseded by SWGDE/Wisconsin guidance (noted as a conflict). — [Scribd LETN document](https://www.scribd.com/document/70577967/Untitled)

**Electrostatic dust lifting (dry-origin dust marks on floors, counters, glass)**
- SWGTREAD lifting guide: "electrostatic lifting is normally the first technique used, as unsuccessful attempts will not prevent subsequent lifting and enhancement techniques." The film has a black side and an aluminium-coated side; black side goes against the impression; a high-voltage charge transfers the dust pattern to the film. Lifts "should be examined in a darkened room with a high intensity light source held at an oblique angle, and the film should never be reused." — [SWGTREAD Guide for Lifting Footwear and Tire Impression Evidence (NIST copy)](https://www.nist.gov/document/swgtread12guideforliftingfootwearandtireimpressionevidence200703pdf); [mirror](https://crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-lifting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html)
- Tennessee Bureau of Investigation procedure (steps): cut two-ply film large enough to cover the print with margin on all sides; place black side toward the impression; connect probe and ground wire to the base unit, attach ground to a metal surface (or ground plate); switch on and touch the film with the probe, which pulls the film tightly against the surface; roll out trapped air with a roller; switch off, touch probe to metal to discharge, lift the film and tape it dust-side-up inside a box; a second lift may yield more detail if loose dust remains. — [TBI CS010.13 Electrostatic Dust Print Lifter](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/crime-scene/CS010.13.pdf)
- Physics: lifting mat takes a negative charge while the ground plate becomes positive, so dust under the mat is charged and drawn to the film; "the high voltage is not strong enough to be dangerous for the user"; "only picks up fine dust, and mud marks, for example, are not absorbed." — [BVDA electrostatic lifting](https://www.bvda.com/en/electrostatic_lifting_of_shoeprints)
- Commercial devices "produce a charge in the range of 10 to 15 kilovolts, though the current is negligible." — [JFSE, Construction and Characterization of an Inexpensive Electrostatic Lifter](https://jfse-ojs-tamu.tdl.org/jfse/article/view/6/pdf)
- Comparison study: "electrostatic lifting followed by gelatin lifter was the superior order when both methods are used. Gelatin lifters performed better on most substrates and for wet prints." Electrostatic "worked poorly on fabric carpet" (uneven, highly porous). The electrostatic method "requires an electric charge and carries a risk of shock to the user." — [EPA HERO record of comparative study](https://hero.epa.gov/hero/index.cfm/reference/details/reference_id/2580144); [ResearchGate: A Comparative Research of Two Lifting Methods](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47793234_A_Comparative_Research_of_Two_Lifting_Methods_Electrostatic_Lifter_and_Gelatin_Lifter); [Thai forensic journal article](https://so02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/forensic/article/view/269667)

**Gelatin lifters**
- BVDA black gelatin lifter sizes for shoeprints: B-12000 13x36 cm (5.2x14.4"), B-12500 18x36 cm (7.2x14.4"), two per pack; B-12900 36x45 cm; extra-large 36x50 cm (14.4x20") for covering a whole suspected area; 8x8 cm is usually enough for micro-trace sampling. — [BVDA Gellifters](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters); [BVDA Gellifter manual (PDF)](https://www.bvda.com/images/content/downloads/Gellifter_manual_EN.pdf)
- Application: "Hold the lifter by its two short ends ... Apply one end first, then work toward the other, using a rubber roller to avoid air bubbles." BVDA advises "lifting shoeprints in dust with the black Gellifters, even for dark-colored prints." — [BVDA Gellifters for shoeprints](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters-shoeprints)
- Dwell time: "leaving the lifter on the print for a longer period (up to 20 min) yields better results than immediate lifting"; pressure can distort because the gelatin is elastic; authors recommend a hydraulic press at about 2 bar for 5-10 s (single research group). — [Lifting Shoeprints Using Gelatin Lifters and a Hydraulic Press (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10831242_Lifting_Shoeprints_Using_Gelatin_Lifters_and_a_Hydraulic_Press)
- "Gelatin lifters collect less dust than adhesive lifters and are therefore more suitable for lifting shoeprints from loosely fibrous materials" (paper, flannel, cardboard). — [BVDA Gellifter manual](https://www.bvda.com/images/content/downloads/Gellifter_manual_EN.pdf)
- Used gelatin lifters "cannot be stored once they have been used on dye-enhanced prints" because the dye keeps diffusing. — [BVDA Gellifter manual](https://www.bvda.com/images/content/downloads/Gellifter_manual_EN.pdf)
- Glossary: a gelatin lift "captures the impression in a gelatin layer on a rubber backing." — [eCampusOntario Footwear Impression Terms](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/loffootwear/?p=30)

**Adhesive lifters**
- SWGTREAD lifting guide (as mirrored): "adhesive lifts are normally used for impressions developed with dark colored powders and are not recommended for lifting dust or residue impressions." "White lifters provide greater contrast with impressions enhanced with dark colored powders. Black lifters provide greater contrast with light colored powders and residue impressions." — [Guide for Lifting Footwear and Tire Impression Evidence](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-lifting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html)
- Note the conflict on lifter colour: BVDA recommends black gelatin even for dark-coloured dust prints. — [BVDA](https://www.bvda.com/en/gellifters-shoeprints)
- White adhesive lifters can be sprayed with Bromophenol Blue, which turns blue where it reacts with dust, to visualise the lift. — [ResearchGate figure, adhesive lifter on gelatin](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Applying-an-adhesive-lifter-directly-to-the-surface-of-the-gelatin-lifter-covered-with_fig1_7388214)

**Casting 3D impressions with dental stone**
- SWGTREAD casting guide (03/2007) scope: "procedures that should be used for the casting of footwear and tire impression evidence" and "is not intended as a substitute for training." Dental stone defined as "a generic gypsum product generally having a strength rating of 8,000 psi (55 mega pascal) or higher." "The average footwear impression requires approximately two (2) pounds of dental stone and approximately ten (10) ounces of water. The amount of water required may vary depending on the casting product." "When casting a fragile impression, it may be necessary to apply a fixative. Care should be exercised when applying fixatives"; dense soil may need a release agent. — [SWGTREAD Guide for Casting (NIST copy)](https://www.nist.gov/document/swgtread11guideforcastingfootwearandtireimpressionevidence200703pdf); [mirror](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-casting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html); AAFS standard for dental stone definition: [AAFS 126 BPR e1](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/126_BPR_e1.pdf)
- Water-to-powder figures differ by source: Wisconsin DOJ roughly 8-9 oz water per 2 lb, added gradually, "if lumps appear, add more water, and re-mix", pour "starting at the highest point outside the impression and working the material in"; Maine State Police about 12 oz per 2 lb; Virginia DFS 6 oz per pound and "generally, about three (3) pounds of dental stone is sufficient to cover a standard shoe impression"; Cast-PRO product: "plastic zipper bag, adding 6 oz. of water to every pound of casting material"; Hard-Core Blue 3 lb kit: "Add 24 oz. (709.77 ml) of water to the bag, seal, and mix" (= 8 oz/lb). — [Wisconsin DOJ Footwear Collection Guide](https://www.wisdoj.gov/Forensic%20Science/Footwear%20Collection%20Guide.pdf); [Maine State Police Casting Impressions](https://www.maine.gov/dps/msp/sites/maine.gov.dps.msp/files/inline-files/Casting%20Impressions_0.pdf); [Virginia DFS Impressions 2021](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf); [Evident Cast-PRO](https://www.shopevident.com/category/casting-footwear/cast-pro-bulk-dental-stone); [Sirchie Hard-Core Blue 3 lb](https://www.sirchie.com/products/tire-tool-footwear-casting-impressions/dental-stone-casting/hard-core-casting-blue-3.html)
- Mixing: "add six (6) ounces of water per pound of dental stone, then mix by kneading the bag until a uniform consistency is reached"; target consistency "the viscosity of heavy cream" or "similar to pancake batter." — [Virginia DFS](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf); [SIFS blog, Footwear Impression Casting](https://www.sifs.in/blog-details/footwear-impression-casting)
- Setting before lift: "the cast must set undisturbed for at least 30 minutes before it is carefully lifted from the surface"; product instructions give 25-30 minutes to set. — [Footwear, The Missed Evidence](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/footwear.html); [Evident Cast-PRO Shaker](https://www.shopevident.com/category/casting-footwear/cast-pro-shaker-dental-stone)
- Marking: identifying marks are scratched into the back before the cast fully hardens; list "1. name or initials 2. date 3. case number 4. other important information" and "This information may differ from one police department to another" **[JURIS]**. IAAI guide: write on the back "initials, date, exhibit number, and a directional arrow pointing north." Another guide: "identifier numbers which link the casts to diagrams and/or photographs; date and initials; ... case number." — [Footwear, The Missed Evidence](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/footwear.html); [IAAI Evidence Guide, 3-D Impressions](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/3-d-impressions); [SWGTREAD casting guide mirror](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-casting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html)
- Drying: "allow dental stone casts to thoroughly dry for 48 hours prior to any attempts to clean the cast"; "remove and air dry 48 hours." No source gave a 24 h figure. — [SWGTREAD casting guide mirror](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/guide-for-casting-footwear-and-tire-impression-evidence.html); [IAAI 3-D Impressions](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/3-d-impressions)
- "Plaster of Paris is not recommended for creating casts of shoe or tire impressions." — [Virginia DFS](https://dfs.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/Impressions-2021-12-1.pdf)
- NIJ-funded evaluation compared dental stone, Traxtone and Crime-Cast products (abstract only). — [OJP NCJRS abstract](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/evaluation-dental-stone-traxtone-and-crime-cast)

**Class vs individual characteristics; test impressions; conclusions**
- FBI review: class characteristics come from manufacturing (physical size, design, mould characteristics); a class match alone supports only a "could have made it" conclusion. Randomly acquired characteristics (RACs, "accidentals") are wear marks such as cuts, nicks, scratches and attached debris. Identification requires confirmable RACs in the same location and orientation; unexplained differences lead to exclusion. — [FBI Forensic Science Communications, July 2009, The Forensic Analysis of Footwear Impression Evidence](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/july2009/review/2009_07_review02.htm); [ASTEE examination page](https://www.asteetrace.org/examination)
- Test/known impressions are high-quality prints made with the suspect shoe under controlled lab conditions and compared with the scene print and the sole to find RACs. SWGTREAD terminology standard cross-references "known impression" to "test impression." — [arXiv 1906.05244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.05244); [SWGTREAD Standard for Terminology (NIST)](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2016/10/26/swgtread_15_standard_for_terminology_used_for_forensic_footwear_and_tire_impression_evidence_201303.pdf)
- Range of conclusions standard for footwear/tire examinations exists (SWGTREAD). — [Range of Conclusions Standard](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/range-of-conclusions-standard-for-footwear-and-tire-impression-examinations.html)

### Inferences
- For the simulation, a defensible generic workflow: (1) darken room/low-angle torch sweep from the point of entry inward; (2) mark each find; (3) overall, mid-range and examination-quality photos with tripod, scale on the same plane, flash at ~10/30/45° from at least three directions; (4) dry dust mark on hard floor or glass counter → electrostatic lift first, then black gelatin lifter; (5) soil/sand outside → dental stone cast, mark back, bag after ≥30 min, dry 48 h; (6) lab compares against test impressions of seized shoes.
- The ~2 lb : 10 oz SWGTREAD figure is the most-cited; agency kits range 6-12 oz per 2 lb depending on product, so a VR mixing step should accept a range and require a "heavy cream/pancake batter" consistency rather than one exact number.
- In a jewellery store, glass display-case tops and polished floors are the surfaces most likely to carry dust marks; electrostatic lifting suits these but fails on carpet.

### Gaps
- Could not read the SWGTREAD casting, lifting and photography guides directly (fetch blocked); lifting-cast time, cleaning and packaging steps come second-hand.
- No figure found for test-impression methods (e.g., inked/powdered walking impressions) or for lifter storage temperatures.
- Exact wording of the SWGTREAD "wet origin / dry origin" definitions not retrieved.

## 2. Tool marks: pry marks, striations vs impressions, documentation, casting, recovery

### Takeaway
Tool marks are classed as impressed (tool pressed in) or striated (tool slid across; "friction/abrasion/scratch marks" per AFTE), and a pry bar or screwdriver can leave either or a combination. Scene practice: photograph with and without a scale under normal and oblique light, perpendicular to the mark; recover the whole item or cut out the section with a wide margin; otherwise cast with a silicone such as Mikrosil (1.5-2 in base plus a slightly shorter strip of catalyst, mix 20-30 s, sets in under 10 min at room temperature); never fit a suspect tool into the mark; preserve paint/metal transfers and package tools separately from marked items.

### Cited Findings
- AFTE glossary, striated toolmark: "contour variations, generally microscopic, on the surface of an object caused by a combination of force and motion" where the tool moves roughly parallel to the marked surface; synonyms friction marks, abrasion marks, scratch marks. — [AFTE Glossary: Toolmark, Striated](https://afte.org/glossary/toolmark-striated/)
- Tennessee Bureau of Investigation procedure: impressed marks are made "when a tool is place[d] against an object and enough pressure [i]s applied to the tool that it leaves an impression in the object"; striated marks form when the tool is moved across the object, producing a scrape. — [TBI FA_19 Physical Examination and Classification of Tools and Toolmark Methods](https://downloads.tbi.tn.gov/forensic-services/firearms/FA_19.pdf)
- "the tip of a pry bar scratches or indents a door jamb" (striation example); "a crowbar being used to pry open a door may leave an impression"; an AFTE article notes "an impressed mark can sometimes be confused with a series of striated markings." — [IntechOpen, Forensic Analysis and Interpretation of Tool Marks](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/77222); [Missouri State Highway Patrol Toolmarks](https://www.mshp.dps.missouri.gov/MSHPWeb/PatrolDivisions/CLD/Firearms/toolmarks.html); [AFTE, Tool Marks Striated vs Impressed](https://afte.org/shop/articles/tool-marks-striated-vs-impressed/)
- Three mark types used in some training: "moulded, where the object is inserted into the surface; slipped, where the object is dragged across the surface; and combo, where it is both inserted and dragged." — [Science World, Tool Mark Analysis](https://www.scienceworld.ca/resource/tool-mark-analysis-crime-fighters-station-1/)
- California DOJ Physical Evidence Bulletin 27: the tool "should NEVER be placed into the impression to see if it could have made the mark." — [CA DOJ PEB 27](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/peb-27.pdf)
- Indiana State Police guidance warns against testing a suspect tool by fitting it into an impression since this can alter evidentiary value; "Objects such as detached locks, latches, or door mouldings that carry toolmarks are often submitted as found." Investigators "usually compare detailed photos or silicone casts and collect residue left by the tool." — [Indiana State Police PEB-13 Toolmark (2025)](https://secure.in.gov/isp/labs/files/PEB-13_Toolmark-_7-1-25.pdf); [2024 version](https://secure.in.gov/isp/labs/files/PEB-13_Toolmark_7-18-24.pdf)
- Cutting out: toolmarks "may be cut out of that object provided a large margin can be left around the toolmark"; IAAI: after photographing, "cut any substrate well away from the toolmark so the mark is not obliterated"; New Hampshire State Police lab: "when this is not possible due to the size or location of the object bearing the mark, a section of the object bearing the toolmark may be collected"; "For large items, a covering over the area of interest and secured with evidence tape is sufficient for submission to the lab." — [Encyclopedia.com Toolmarks](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/toolmarks); [IAAI Toolmark on Substrate](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/toolmark-on-substrate); [NH State Police Forensic Lab, Toolmark](https://www.nhsp.dos.nh.gov/our-services/forensic-laboratory/firearms-toolmarks/toolmark-analysis)
- Packaging: "do not package tools in the same container as the questioned toolmark"; "toolmark evidence should be secured in packaging containers that will prevent alterations of the marks due to rubbing"; "care should be taken so that any trace evidence is not destroyed or lost." — [NH State Police Forensic Lab](https://www.nhsp.dos.nh.gov/our-services/forensic-laboratory/firearms-toolmarks/toolmark-analysis)
- Transfers: "be alert for any other evidence that may have been transferred when the mark was made, such as paint or mortar." — [IAAI Toolmarks](https://www.iaaievidenceguide.com/toolmarks)
- Photography: Santa Barbara County Sheriff SOP CSI-006-12 (2012): "Photograph the tool mark with normal and oblique lighting (with and without a scale)", then closer shots from several oblique angles, photographer perpendicular to the mark; casting with "Mikrosil™ or other suitable casting material (e.g. Accutrans, Forensil)" following the material's directions. — [Santa Barbara SO SOP CSI-006-12](https://www.sbsheriff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SOP-CSI-006-12-Firearms-and-Toolmarks-Evidence-Collection.pdf)
- CA DOJ: the close-up photograph "is for identification and orientation only and cannot be used for actual comparisons"; casting is needed because "a tool mark is three-dimensional and casting preserves three-dimensional evidence." — [CA DOJ PEB 27](https://oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/cci/reference/peb_27.pdf); [Crime Scene and Evidence Photography, ch. 9](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/CSEP/chapters/crime-scene-and-evidence-photography-2nd-edition-chapter9.pdf)
- "the scale allows for a 1:1 photo for comparison to be made"; 45° lighting "uses one or more lights positioned at 45-degree angles", while oblique (low-angle) lighting "is usually used to show detail by creating shadows on the surface of the evidence." — [Lighting Methods for Copy and Evidence Close-up Photography](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/closeup.html)
- Mikrosil instructions (Sirchie sheet): about 1.5-2 inches of base and a slightly shorter strip of catalyst, base wider than catalyst, measure lengths before mixing; mix "about 20 to 30 seconds until there are no blue streaks"; longer mixing makes it set too quickly; "setup time of under 10 minutes at room temperature." — [Sirchie Mikrosil instruction PDF](https://www.sirchie.com/media/resourcecenter/item//M/C/MCM100_TI02-59ENG-REV4E.pdf)
- Tri-Tech Forensics: cast "sets within six to eight minutes" and about fifteen minutes below freezing; mix putty and catalyst on a mixing card with the supplied spatula, apply to the surface. — [Tri-Tech Mikrosil Casting Putty Kit](https://tritechforensics.com/mikrosil-casting-putty-kit/)
- Colour: brown "generally preferred for toolmarks" because the dark colour "increases the visibility of detail in the casts made from tool marks, firing pin marks, breech face marks"; grey preferred by some for side-lit photography. — [Safariland Mikrosil](https://www.safariland.com/products/forensics/evidence-collection/tool-mark-casting/mikrosil-white-7-oz.-200g-F_155.html); [Evident Mikrosil](https://www.shopevident.com/category/casting-footwear/mikrosil-forensic-silicone-casting-material)
- Mikrosil, Coe-Flex and Dip-Pak are recognised toolmark casting materials; a 2009 JFI study found Mikrosil, AccuTrans, ReproRubber Thin Pour and Medium Body "all outperformed dental stone for detailed 3D impressions." — [FIU firearms module 11](https://archive.gfjc.fiu.edu/firearms/module11/fir_m11_t09_01.htm); [NCJRS abstract 251096](https://www.ncjrs.gov/App/Publications/abstract.aspx?ID=251096)
- Czech thesis on mechanoscopic identification of tools in burglaries exists (not read). — [Charles University repository](https://dspace.cuni.cz/handle/20.500.11956/186384)

### Inferences
- VR step order for a pry mark on a display-case frame or back-door jamb: locate → photograph (overall, mid, close-up with/without scale, oblique and normal light, lens perpendicular) → look for and collect paint/metal flakes first → if the frame section or lock can be removed, bag it whole with padding; else cast with Mikrosil → never bring a seized crowbar near the mark.
- Mikrosil timings for a simulation: mix 20-30 s, apply within ~1 min, wait 6-10 min (longer in cold), peel.

## 3. Glass: fracture mechanics, glass types, collection and the thrown object

### Takeaway
Radial cracks form first on the face opposite the blow and concentric cracks later on the struck face. The 3R (4R) rule: Wallner/rib marks on a radial crack near the impact meet the surface at Right angles on the Reverse side from the force. Sequence of impacts is read from crack terminations: a later radial crack stops at a pre-existing one (T-junction: stem is later, crossbar earlier). These rules fail for tempered glass (dices) and are unreliable for laminated glass and tightly framed small panes. Backward fragmentation deposits fragments on the breaker, most within about 1-1.5 m and up to ~3 m, falling off exponentially with distance; suspect clothing, hair and shoes must be seized quickly (microscopic particles can be lost within 24 h) and packaged in paper, with scene control glass from each broken item in rigid, labelled containers, inside/outside surfaces identified.

### Cited Findings

**Fracture mechanics and direction of force**
- 3R rule: radial cracks show a right-angle relationship (of the rib/Wallner marks) to the side opposite the impact; "the 3R and 4R names describe the same rule, with the 4R version referring to ridges on radial cracks." "Wallner lines on the radial cracks nearest the impact point are oriented perpendicular to the side opposite the impact." — [Forensic glass analysis (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_glass_analysis); [forensicfield.blog, Glass Fractures & Their Types](https://forensicfield.blog/glass-fractures-their-types/)
- FBI Laboratory fractography procedure: Wallner lines "are almost always concave toward the direction from which the crack was propagating"; examiners "should look at the Wallner lines on the radial cracks closest to the impact, and if the impact site is lost, the glass may need reconstruction"; "in tempered or laminated glass, Wallner lines cannot be used to determine the side of crack initiation." Tempered glass "dices" without forming ridges; "small windows held tightly in a frame may not bend enough for the rule to apply." — [FBI Lab GEO-510-06 Fractography of Brittle Materials](https://fbilabqsd.fbi.gov/file-repository/trace-evidence/geo-510-06-fractography-of-brittle-materials.pdf); [NIST glass fractures PDF](https://www.nist.gov/document/glassfracturespdf)
- Concentric cracks "form on the same side as the applied force." — [forensicfield.blog Q&A on Glass Fracture](https://forensicfield.blog/glass-fracture-patterns/)
- Sequence: "The radial fractures from the right impact were stopped by the pre-existing fractures caused by the left impact, so the left impact occurred first." T-junction rule: "The stem is the later crack and the crossbar is the earlier one." Radial cracks form first on the face opposite the strike, concentric afterwards on the struck face. Exit holes larger than entry holes. "Tempered glass usually lacks clear radial and concentric lines, so this method does not work on it." — [CourseHero Glass Fractures worksheet](https://www.coursehero.com/file/127854146/Glass-Fracturespdf/); [forensicspot.com Glass Fractures and Direction of Force](https://forensicspot.com/topics/forensic-physics/glass-fragmentation-and-direction-of-force-analysis); [EBSCO Glass as evidence](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/law/glass-evidence/)

**Glass types in a shopfront and display cases**
- "laminated glass resists breakage better than tempered or annealed glass"; Jewelers Mutual recommends "U.L. certified impact resistant glazing materials or laminated glass on the sides, fronts and tops of cases" and notes "framing may reinforce a case's ability to withstand blunt force." — [Jewelers Mutual, Jewelry Showcases: 2 Factors](https://www.jewelersmutual.com/resources/business/risk-management/jewelry-showcases-2-factors-optimal-physical-security); [Jewelers Mutual, 10 Tips to Prevent Smash-and-Grab](https://www.jewelersmutual.com/resources/business/risk-management/ten-tips-prevent-enclosed-mall-smash-grabs)
- Tempered glass "is a safety feature, not a security one, since it breaks into small pieces and offers little defense against a determined thief." Benchmarks cited: UL 972 (burglary-resistant glazing) and EN 356 (vendor claim, unverified). — [VD Showcase, Glass Display Case Security Guide](https://vdshowcase.com/blog/glass-display-case-jewelry-security-guide/); [Solar Screen, Jewelry store glass protection](https://solarscreen.eu/en/blog/jewelry-store-glass-protection/); [Solink, high security display cases](https://solink.com/resources/industry-insights/high-security-display-cases/)

**Backward fragmentation (glass onto the breaker)**
- Nelson & Revell (1967), high-speed photography: backward fragments appeared every time a pane broke; most landed within about 1-1.5 m of the window, some reached roughly 3 m. — [Backward Fragmentation from Breaking Glass (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S001573686770376X)
- Pounds et al. (1978): fragments collected "out to a distance of 3 metres from the window"; "The amount of glass per square metre decreased rapidly with distance in an approximately exponential manner"; dummies in clothing at varying distances carried up to 15 fragments on their jumpers. — [NCJRS abstract, Study on the Backward Fragmentation of Window Glass](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/study-backward-fragmentation-window-glass-and-transfer-glass); [ScienceDirect, Distribution of Glass Fragments in Front of a Broken Window](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S001573687871203X)
- Luce, Buckle & McInnis (1991): "as the distance increased, the total quantity of fragments decreased by a factor of 4-5 for every 45 centimeter increment." — [ScienceDirect, Breaking of flat glass Part 1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/037907389190190T)
- Transfer of glass part 4: "in 7 out of 12 of the experiments more than 200 glass fragments were recovered from the surface of a box, after it had been carried a distance of 10 metres"; forward fragmentation reached up to 4.0 m. — [ScienceDirect, The transfer of glass part 4](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0379073898000449); [Part 2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0379073898000425)

**Collection and packaging**
- From a suspect: comb hair and shake clothing over clean paper; "microscopic glass particles on clothing, shoes, and hair can disappear within 24 hours"; "glass should not be removed from clothing unless absolutely necessary for its preservation"; clothing and shoes contaminated with glass "wrapped in paper and submitted to the laboratory." — [Girard, Forensic Analysis of Glass (Jones & Bartlett sample ch. 5)](https://samples.jbpub.com/9781284142617/9781284142617_CH05_Girard_SECURE.pdf); [Evidence Collection Guidelines](https://www.crime-scene-investigator.net/collect.html)

- Washington State Patrol: hard containers "such as metal cans or plastic boxes" for glass; small pieces in a rigid container "such as a pill box or vial, sealed and labelled"; initials across the tape seal onto the packaging. — [WSP Crime Lab, J. Glass](https://wsp.wa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/J.-Glass.pdf)
- "If two or more glass shards from a suspect's hair or clothes are found to be indistinguishable from a control sample of glass from the scene, they can be considered significant associative evidence." — [Forensic Science Simplified, Trace Evidence](https://www.forensicsciencesimplified.org/trace/how.html)
- ENFSI Best Practice Manual for the Forensic Identification and Comparison of Glass (ENFSI-PGT-BPM-02, Dec 2022) has a "Handling Items" section with scene-recovery recommendations (8.1); ENFSI guideline PGT-GDL-006 stresses preventing cross-contamination and notes glass fragments "often lodge in cuts, holes, and shoe soles"; ENFSI Scene of Crime BPM requires sealed containers and uniquely identifiable packages linked to contents on the label. — [ENFSI BPM Glass](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/BPM-Glass_QCC_20221122-003-1.pdf); [ENFSI GDL PGT-GDL-006](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/06.0-Guideline-for-the-Recovery-1.pdf); [ENFSI BPM-SOC-01](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/BPM-SOC-01-v.2021115_final.pdf)
- Cellmark (UK provider) offers glass casework including fracture/transfer interpretation. — [Cellmark Forensics, Glass](https://www.cellmarkforensics.co.uk/services/forensic-casework/glass/)

### Inferences
- For a jewellery-store sim: shopfront window is likely annealed or laminated; display cases often tempered (dices into small cubes, no usable radial pattern) or laminated (cracks but holds). Direction-of-force and sequence puzzles only work on an annealed pane, so the "which blow came first" exercise should be set on the shopfront or an annealed internal pane.
- A thrown brick/stone should be treated as a tool-mark and trace carrier: photograph in place with scale, note glass fragments/paint adhering to it, swab for DNA (hands), package in a rigid box; the thrower will have backward-fragmentation glass on sleeves/front (most within 1.5 m).
- Collect large pane remnants from the frame (control + fracture analysis), marking "inside"/"outside" surface with a marker before removal, and collecting fragments inside and outside the shop separately, since distribution tells which side was struck.

## 4. Point of entry/exit examination: what to look for, order of processing, documenting the route

### Takeaway
At a burglary, attention centres on the point of entry, where tool marks, broken glass/wood/metal fragments, fingerprints, blood and clothing fibres are concentrated. Guidance is: survey first, identify the central point of attack and entry/exit points, photograph and document, then collect trace material from probable entry points, swab likely-contact areas for DNA, locate and lift latent prints, and trace the intruder's path through the scene from footwear marks and touched items. Exact sequencing is agency-specific **[JURIS]**.

### Cited Findings
- NIJ training: "at the scene of a burglary, attention may focus on the point of entry. Fragments of wood, metal, or broken glass may be discovered, along with fingerprints, blood, and fibers from clothing deposited when the perpetrator forced entry." — [NIJ Crime Scene and DNA Basics, Location/Collection of Evidence](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/crime-scene-and-dna-basics-forensic-analysts/evidence-crime-scene/location-collection-evidence)
- Northwestern J. Crim. Law & Criminology burglary guide: "Ascertain the central point of attack and points of entrance and exit. General Survey. Make a general survey of the crime scene for the purpose of selecting a starting point ... The search should not be hurried, but should be thorough, searching for any trace of footprints, fingerprints, articles left at the scene, and any other evidence. Minute evidence is easily overlooked, and easily destroyed." Work outward from the central point of attack and trace the intruder's path from entry onward. — [Northwestern Scholarly Commons JCLC article](https://scholarlycommons.law.northwestern.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2648&context=jclc)
- NIST/NIJ "Crime Scene Investigation: A Guide for Law Enforcement" (2013): note "Windows: Are they open or closed? Is there broken glass?"; protect latent prints on glass. — [NIST Crime Scene Investigation 2013](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf)
- Standard collection sequence: "Photograph and document the scene. Collect trace materials (especially from probable points of entry). Collect low-level DNA evidence by swabbing areas of likely contact. Locate and collect latent fingerprints." — [Forensic Science Simplified, CSI How It's Done](https://www.forensicsciencesimplified.org/csi/how.html); [PDF guide](https://www.forensicsciencesimplified.org/csi/CrimeSceneInvestigation.pdf)
- State police guidance: handle doors and windows so latent prints aren't damaged; "These usually occur near the points of entry or exit"; take special note of "any broken, forced, or cut locks, latches, or bolts in the immediate area." — [Indiana State Police PEB-13 Toolmark](https://secure.in.gov/isp/labs/files/PEB-13_Toolmark_7-18-24.pdf); [CA DOJ PEB 27](https://oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/cci/reference/peb_27.pdf)
- Route: "Shoeprints may reveal a perpetrator's every step. Fingerprints may indicate the things the perpetrator touched." — [Dummies, Forensics: Assessing the Scene of the Crime](https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/science/forensics/forensics-assessing-the-scene-of-the-crime-200080/)
- Burglary investigation guide: most evidence is concentrated near where the suspect entered; investigators look for tool marks at entry points, fingerprints, DNA and shoe prints. — [Crime and Clues, Burglary Investigations](https://crimeandclues.com/2013/01/26/burglary-investigations/)
- Israeli case study: burglaries with no visible forced entry were explained by inspecting lock cylinders for foreign objects (foil impressioning); "Most of these cases are never solved, many times because no visible signs of entry or forensic evidence are found at the scene." — [Pertsev et al. 2023, ejournals.eu](https://ejournals.eu/en/journal_article_files/full_text/018ecee2-5ce5-71a4-b429-c5b14d5816e6/download)
- ENFSI Scene of Crime BPM: sealed containers, protection in transport, each package uniquely identifiable and linked to contents on the label. — [ENFSI BPM-SOC-01](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/BPM-SOC-01-v.2021115_final.pdf)
- ENFSI fibre BPM exists (not read) for snagged fibres at entry points. — [ENFSI BPM Fibres THG-04](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/BPM-for-the-Forensic-Examination-of-Fibres.-ENFSI-BPM-THG-04.pdf)
- Local policy example (VCU Police 7-3) and municipal procedure (Hattiesburg ch. 83) show evidence-collection rules are set per agency **[JURIS]**. — [VCU Police 7-3](https://police.vcu.edu/facts/policies/7-3-crime-scene-evidence-collection-and-preservation/); [Hattiesburg Chapter 83](https://www.hattiesburgms.com/wp-content/uploads/Chapter-83-Evidence-Collection.pdf)

### Inferences
- A VR "route" mechanic consistent with the sources: entry point (glass/tool marks/fibres/blood on jagged glass edges) → floor path (dust marks; electrostatic/gel lifts) → targets (display cases: tool marks, glove/finger marks on glass, dropped items) → exit (second set of marks, often the same door). Each find gets a numbered marker tied to the photo log and sketch, satisfying the ENFSI label-to-contents rule.
- Processing order inferred from the sources: non-destructive first (visual/oblique light, photography), then trace/DNA (fibres on glass edges, blood swabs), then lifts/powder, then casts and cutting-out, so that powdering or casting never destroys fibres or DNA.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative, current protocol for "order of processing a burglary point of entry" was retrieved; the sequence above is pieced from NIJ/NIST training and a dated law-journal guide.
- No source found specifically on recovering fibres snagged on broken glass edges or on bloodstain handling at a broken window beyond general statements.
- Fingerprinting of glass edges/fragments (e.g., superglue fuming of shards) was not covered by retrieved results.

## 5. Safes and display cases in a jewellery theft: attack methods and the evidence each leaves

### Takeaway
Sources classify safe attacks by tool class (hand tools → power tools → torches → explosives) and by how much evidence they leave; examiners aim to determine whether marks came from heat, explosives, manual impact or drilling. Display-case attacks in a jewellery store are typically smash (blunt force on tempered/annealed glass) or pry (tool marks on frames/locks); laminated glass delays entry. Detailed forensic literature on safe-attack evidence was thin in the results.

### Cited Findings
- Blaze, "Safecracking for the computer scientist": attacks ranked by tools from hand tools to power tools, torches and explosives, and classified by how much evidence they leave, "surreptitious" attacks leaving none. — [mattblaze.org safelocks.pdf](https://www.mattblaze.org/papers/safelocks.pdf)
- Forensic examiners on safes "try to determine whether marks came from heat, explosives, manual impact, or drilling." — [Crime and Clues, Burglary Investigations](https://crimeandclues.com/2013/01/26/burglary-investigations/); [CliffsNotes burglary investigation notes](https://www.cliffsnotes.com/study-notes/33779180)
- California PC 464 (safe burglary with explosives/torch) does not apply when "using manual tools, such as a crowbar, a drill, or a sledgehammer"; defence teams often challenge tool-mark evidence **[JURIS, California only]**. — [Singh Law, Burglary of a Safe or Vault](https://www.singh-law.com/burglary-of-a-safe-or-vault/)
- Display cases: tempered glass "breaks into small pieces and offers little defense"; laminated glass cracks but the interlayer holds; insurer recommends UL-certified impact-resistant glazing or laminated glass on sides, fronts and tops; frames matter. — [VD Showcase](https://vdshowcase.com/blog/glass-display-case-jewelry-security-guide/); [Jewelers Mutual](https://www.jewelersmutual.com/resources/business/risk-management/jewelry-showcases-2-factors-optimal-physical-security)
- ABUS guide lists the most common burglary methods (prising of doors/windows dominant in European statistics; page content not retrieved). — [ABUS Burglary methods](https://www.abus.com/int/Guide/Burglary-protection/Burglary-methods)
- Lockwiki safecracking overview (tertiary). — [Lockwiki Safecracking](https://www.lockwiki.com/index.php/Safecracking)

### Inferences
- Evidence mapping for the sim (inferred from sections 2-3): prying a case → impressed/striated marks on aluminium frame and lock, paint/anodising transfer onto the tool, frame section recoverable; smashing → tempered dice or annealed radial/concentric pattern, backward fragmentation onto attacker, blood if cut, the striking object (hammer/brick) carries glass; drilling a safe → swarf (metal shavings) on the floor, drill-bit striations in the hole wall, possible abandoned bits; torch/explosive → heat discolouration/soot, residue swabs.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or agency source retrieved describing forensic examination of drilled or punched safes (swarf, striations in drill holes, punch impressions). This section is the weakest and should be treated as inference pending a direct read of safe-crime literature.
